# Evidence: solar-falls

Fetched 2026-10-07 from osha.gov: 1926.501, 1926.502, 1926.250; guidance /green-jobs/solar/falls.

Paragraph corrections and scope notes for reviewer:
- 1926.501(b)(10) (low-slope roofs) covers employees "engaged in roofing activities". Installing solar panels may not be roofing activity, so the warning-line / safety-monitor options from (b)(10) are deliberately NOT offered in the talk. The talk uses the general 6-foot rule, 1926.501(b)(1) (also restated by OSHA's solar falls page), and (b)(11) for steep roofs, which applies to "each employee on a steep roof" with no roofing-activity limit. Note that OSHA's solar falls page cites 1926.501(b)(2) for the 6-foot rule; (b)(2) is actually leading edges. The talk cites (b)(1).
- 1926.501(b)(4) skylight/hole protection applies to falls "more than 6 feet"; the talk doesn't restate the threshold for holes, it states the solar page's "employers must make sure that skylights are guarded or that workers near skylights use personal fall protection".
- Wind and panel handling: no fetched OSHA standard or guidance addresses wind loads on panels being carried or set. The only sourced wind line is 1926.502(i)(3) (covers secured against wind). No separate wind line was written.

## Hook
- "Solar crews work on roofs, climb ladders, and carry big panels."
  - guidance: solar falls: "Workers who install and/or maintain solar panels often work on roofs, use ladders and scaffolding," (exact excerpt). "Carry big panels" reflects the page's ladder/lifting guidance.
- "Every row you set takes away walking room you had this morning."
  - guidance: solar falls: "As more solar panels are installed on the surface of a roof, the walking area which may once have been available, may no longer be available to workers." (paraphrase)

## The 6-foot line
- "Installing panels with a fall of 6 feet or more? You need a guardrail, a safety net, or a personal fall arrest system."
  - guidance: solar falls: "Construction workers involved in the installation of solar panels exposed to fall distances of 6 feet or more must be protected from falls by using one of the following methods:" "Guardrail Systems," "Safety Net Systems," "Personal fall arrest systems" (exact)
  - 1926.501(b)(1): "...unprotected side or edge which is 6 feet (1.8 m) or more above a lower level shall be protected from falling by the use of guardrail systems, safety net systems, or personal fall arrest systems." (paraphrase)
- "On a steep roof, guardrails need toeboards. Nets or a fall arrest system also work."
  - 1926.501(b)(11): "Each employee on a steep roof with unprotected sides and edges 6 feet (1.8 m) or more above lower levels shall be protected from falling by guardrail systems with toeboards, safety net systems, or personal fall arrest systems." (paraphrase)
- "Your company has to make sure the roof is strong enough to hold you before you work on it."
  - 1926.501(a)(2): "The employer shall determine if the walking/working surfaces on which its employees are to work have the strength and structural integrity to support employees safely. Employees shall be allowed to work on those surfaces only when the surfaces have the requisite strength and structural integrity." (paraphrase)
- "Maintenance on finished systems usually falls under general industry rules, where protection starts at 4 feet."
  - guidance: solar falls: "Maintenance work on solar panels is generally considered to fall under OSHA's general industry standards." "Such workers when exposed to fall hazards of 4 feet or more must be protected by a standard railing." "If such a railing is not possible then the workers must be protected by a fall protection device such as a personal fall arrest system or a safety net." (paraphrase). "usually" = "generally".

## Skylights and holes
- "As panels fill a roof, walkways shrink. You end up squeezing past skylights and roof hatches."
  - guidance: solar falls: "This may force workers to squeeze by or walk very close to skylights and/or roof hatches." (paraphrase)
- "Your company has to make sure skylights are guarded, or that you're tied off when you work near them."
  - guidance: solar falls: "employers must make sure that skylights are guarded or that workers near skylights use personal fall protection." (paraphrase; page cites 1910.23(a)(4)). Construction counterpart: 1926.501(b)(4)(i), "protected from falling through holes (including skylights) more than 6 feet (1.8 m) above lower levels, by personal fall arrest systems, covers, or guardrail systems erected around such holes."
  - Reviewer note: "tied off" = "use personal fall protection".
- "A cover over a hole has to hold twice the weight that could be on it."
  - 1926.502(i)(2): "All other covers shall be capable of supporting, without failure, at least twice the weight of employees, equipment, and materials that may be imposed on the cover at any one time." (paraphrase; (i)(1) roadway covers not relevant)
- "Secure it so wind, equipment, or people can't knock it loose, and mark it HOLE or COVER or color-code it."
  - 1926.502(i)(3): "All covers shall be secured when installed so as to prevent accidental displacement by the wind, equipment, or employees." (paraphrase)
  - 1926.502(i)(4): "All covers shall be color coded or they shall be marked with the word "HOLE" or "COVER" to provide warning of the hazard." (paraphrase)

## Tying off
- "Your anchor has to hold 5,000 pounds for each person tied to it, or be part of a system a qualified person designed and oversees."
  - 1926.502(d)(15): "capable of supporting at least 5,000 pounds (22.2 kN) per employee attached, or shall be designed, installed, and used as follows: (i) as part of a complete personal fall arrest system which maintains a safety factor of at least two; and (ii) under the supervision of a qualified person." (paraphrase)
- "Rig it so you can't free fall more than 6 feet or hit anything below."
  - 1926.502(d)(16)(iii): "be rigged such that an employee can neither free fall more than 6 feet (1.8 m), nor contact any lower level" (paraphrase)
- "Inspect your harness and lanyard before each use for wear and damage."
  - 1926.502(d)(21): "Personal fall arrest systems shall be inspected prior to each use for wear, damage and other deterioration" (paraphrase)

## Getting panels up there
- "Never climb a ladder carrying a panel."
  - guidance: solar falls: "Workers should never be allowed to climb ladders while carrying solar panels." (paraphrase)
- "Use a ladder hoist, swing hoist, or truck-mounted crane or conveyor wherever you can."
  - guidance: solar falls: "Lifting equipment, such as ladder hoists, swing hoists, or truck-mounted cranes/conveyors, should be used wherever possible." (paraphrase)
- "Stack panels so they can't slide, fall, or collapse."
  - 1926.250(a)(1): "All materials stored in tiers shall be stacked, racked, blocked, interlocked, or otherwise secured to prevent sliding, falling or collapse." (paraphrase; applied to stacked panels)
