# hearing-con evidence

Pocket guide = OSHA 3498 "Protecting Yourself from Noise in Construction", https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/3498noise-in-construction-pocket-guide.pdf (the /sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3498.pdf path 404'd; the guide states it "is not a standard or regulation").
No 1910.95 (general industry hearing conservation) rules are used. The 85 dBA figures below are guidance-only rules of thumb from the pocket guide and are presented as "assume", not as a limit.

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| Hearing loss from noise is permanent. | guidance: Pocket guide; OSHA Noise Health Effects | exact: "once permanent noise-induced hearing loss occurs, it cannot be cured or reversed." / "Exposure to high levels of noise can cause permanent hearing loss." |
| Surgery and hearing aids can't fix it, | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "Neither surgery nor a hearing aid can help correct this type of hearing loss." |
| and it usually comes on so slowly you don't notice until it's too late. | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "Hearing loss usually occurs gradually, so you may not realize it is happening until it is too late." |
| Stand about an arm's length from a coworker. If you have to raise your voice to be heard 2 to 3 feet away, assume it's 85 decibels or more. | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "Stand about an arm's length away from your coworker: If you have to raise your voice to be heard 2-3 feet away, you should assume that the sound level is at or above 85 dBA." |
| Ringing or humming in your ears after work, or hearing that seems dull when you leave, are signs the noise may be hazardous. | guidance: OSHA Occupational Noise Exposure | paraphrase: signs noise may be hazardous: "Hear ringing or humming in your ears when you leave work"; "Experience temporary hearing loss when leaving work." |
| A jackhammer can hit 100 decibels. | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "When noise is as loud as 100 dBA (like a jackhammer or stud welder)" |
| In construction, the limit is 90 decibels over 8 hours. | OSHA 1926.52(a), Table D-2 | exact: Table D-2 "8 hours — 90 dBA slow response"; protection required "when the sound levels exceed those shown in Table D-2" |
| The louder it gets, the less time is allowed: 95 for 4 hours, 100 for 2 hours, 105 for 1 hour, and 115 for 15 minutes or less. | OSHA 1926.52 Table D-2 | exact: 4 h 95; 2 h 100; 1 h 105; "¼ or less" 115 |
| Time at different noise levels adds up. The whole day counts, not just the loudest job. | OSHA 1926.52(d)(2)(i) | paraphrase: "When the daily noise exposure is composed of two or more periods of noise exposure of different levels, their combined effect should be considered, rather than the individual effect of each." |
| Above those limits, your company has to try engineering or administrative controls first, | OSHA 1926.52(b) | exact: "When employees are subjected to sound levels exceeding those listed in Table D-2 ... feasible administrative or engineering controls shall be utilized." |
| like moving the generator farther away or turning it away from the crew. | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "Move the generator farther away or face it in a direction that is away from where most people are working." |
| If that isn't enough, it has to provide hearing protection, and you have to wear it. | OSHA 1926.52(b); 1926.101(a) | exact: "If such controls fail to reduce sound levels within the levels of the table, personal protective equipment ... shall be provided and used"; "ear protective devices shall be provided and used." |
| Plain cotton in your ears is not hearing protection. | OSHA 1926.101(c) | exact: "Plain cotton is not an acceptable protective device." |
| Earplugs have to be fitted or picked for you individually by a competent person. | OSHA 1926.101(b) | exact: "Ear protective devices inserted in the ear shall be fitted or determined individually by competent persons." |
| Foam plugs only work if they're put in right and make a good seal. | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "Must be inserted properly to get the highest possible protection." / "If the plug doesn't make a good seal, it won't protect your hearing." |
| Follow the maker's directions. | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "Each type of hearing protection has manufacturer's directions for use and maintenance" ... "Follow these directions" |
| Wear them the whole time you're in the noise. | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "unless you wear them properly and wear them all the time in high noise areas, the devices will not be effective." |
| Music earbuds are not hearing protection. | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "Neither portable music player headphones nor hearing aids are substitutes for hearing protective devices." |
| Replace plugs or muffs that are worn, dirty or broken. | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "replace or fix the devices when they appear worn, dirty, or broken." |
| Earmuffs can be harder to fit over glasses. | guidance: Pocket guide | exact: "May be more difficult to get a good fit with glasses and/or may interfere with other protective gear." |

Notes:
- "decibels" in the script = dBA on the A-scale at slow response (1926.52(a)); simplified for reading aloud.
- Not used: 1926.52(d)(1) hearing conservation program (true for construction, but no program details exist in Part 1926 and the brief says not to import 1910.95); 1926.52(e) 140 dB peak impact noise (crew can't measure it); the pocket guide's "at no cost" and training statements (not in 1926.101 text as fetched); the pocket guide's recommendation to wear protection at 85 dBA (guidance, could be confused with the 90 dBA rule).
- Foam-plug roll/pull/hold insertion steps: not in any fetched OSHA page, so left out.
