# aerial-lift: evidence

Quotes fetched 2026-10-07 via WebFetch.
- Standard: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.453
- FS = OSHA Fact Sheet "Aerial Lifts" (DSG 4/2011): https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/AERIAL-LIFTS-FACTSHEET.pdf
- HA = OSHA Hazard Alert "Working Safely with Scissor Lifts" (OSHA 3842): https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3842.pdf

## Hook
- "A lift gets you up fast. Skip the basics and it can tip over or throw you out just as fast."
  - guidance: FS hazards list, "Tip-overs," "Ejections from the lift platform,"

## Before you go up
- "Only trained and authorized people run a lift."
  - 1926.453(b)(2)(ii): "Only authorized persons shall operate an aerial lift."; guidance: FS, "Only trained and authorized persons are allowed to operate an aerial lift."
- "Test the controls every day before use"
  - 1926.453(b)(2)(i): "Lift controls shall be tested each day prior to use to determine that such controls are in safe working condition."
- "and do a pre-start inspection each shift. If anything is defective, don't run it."
  - guidance: FS, "Prior to each work shift, conduct a pre-start inspection"; "Do not operate any aerial lift if any of these components are defective"
- "Set the brakes. Put outriggers on pads or solid ground. On a slope, chock the wheels when it's safe to do so."
  - 1926.453(b)(2)(vii): "The brakes shall be set and when outriggers are used, they shall be positioned on pads or a solid surface. Wheel chocks shall be installed before using an aerial lift on an incline, provided they can be safely installed."; guidance: FS, "Use wheel chocks on sloped surfaces when it is safe to do so."

## In a boom lift: tie off
- "Wear a harness with a lanyard attached to the boom or basket."
  - 1926.453(b)(2)(v): "A body belt shall be worn and a lanyard attached to the boom or basket when working from an aerial lift."; guidance: FS, "Use a body harness or a restraining belt with a lanyard attached to the boom or bucket." (Talk says "harness", following the fact sheet, rather than "body belt".)
- "Never tie off to a nearby pole, structure, or other equipment."
  - 1926.453(b)(2)(iii): "Belting off to an adjacent pole, structure, or equipment while working from an aerial lift shall not be permitted."
- "Stand firmly on the floor of the basket. Don't sit or climb on the edge, and don't use planks or ladders to get higher."
  - 1926.453(b)(2)(iv): "Employees shall always stand firmly on the floor of the basket, and shall not sit or climb on the edge of the basket or use planks, ladders, or other devices for a work position."

## In a scissor lift: the rails are your protection
- "A scissor lift must have guardrails."
  - guidance: HA, "Scissor lifts must have guardrails installed to prevent workers from falling" (HA cites 29 CFR 1926.451(g))
- "Stand only on the platform. Never stand on the rails or lean over them."
  - guidance: HA, "Only stand on the work platform; never stand on the guardrails."; FS, "Do not climb on or lean over guardrails or handrails."
- "OSHA covers many scissor lifts under its scaffold rules."
  - guidance: HA, "Many scissor lifts are covered under OSHA's Scaffolding standard."
- "Set up on firm, level ground, away from anything that could make the lift unstable."
  - guidance: HA, "Select work locations with firm, level surfaces away from hazards that can cause instability"

## Loads, travel, power lines, and wind
- "Never go over the manufacturer's load limit for the platform."
  - 1926.453(b)(2)(vi): "Boom and basket load limits specified by the manufacturer shall not be exceeded."; guidance: HA, "Never allow the weight on the work platform to exceed the manufacturer's load rating."
- "Don't drive with the platform raised unless the manufacturer says that lift is built for it."
  - 1926.453(b)(2)(viii): "An aerial lift truck shall not be moved when the boom is elevated in a working position with men in the basket, except for equipment which is specifically designed for this type of operation"; guidance: FS, "Do not drive with the lift platform raised"; HA, "Follow the manufacturer's instructions for safe movement—this usually rules out moving the lift in an elevated position."
- "Before moving a boom lift for travel, cradle the boom and stow the outriggers."
  - 1926.453(b)(2)(xii): "Before moving an aerial lift for travel, the boom(s) shall be inspected to see that it is properly cradled and outriggers are in stowed position"
- "Treat every overhead power line as live, and stay at least 10 feet away."
  - guidance: FS, "Treat all overhead power lines and communication cables as energized, and stay at least 10 feet"; HA, "Select work locations that do not approach electrical power sources (e.g., power lines, transformers) by at least 10 feet"
- "Don't run a lift in winds above the manufacturer's limit."
  - guidance: FS, "Do not operate an aerial lift in high winds above those recommended by the manufacturer."
- "Outdoor scissor lifts are generally limited to winds under 28 miles per hour."
  - guidance: HA, "Scissor lifts rated for outdoor use are generally limited to wind speeds below 28 miles per hour."

## Ask
- "What's the load limit on today's lift, and how much are we putting on it?" — question; ties to (b)(2)(vi).

## Dropped / not used
- "Scissor lifts are mobile scaffolds": the hazard alert does not use that phrase. It says "Many scissor lifts are covered under OSHA's Scaffolding standard" and cites 1926.451(g) and 1926.454. The talk uses the alert's wording.
- Tie-off requirement inside scissor lifts: not stated in the fetched sources. Left out.
- "Body belt" wording of 1926.453(b)(2)(v) not used; talk says harness per the fact sheet.
- Ground guides / traffic control (HA) and climbers (b)(2)(x) fetched but left out for length.
