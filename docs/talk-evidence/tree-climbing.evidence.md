# tree-climbing — evidence

Pages fetched 2026-10-08 with WebFetch (summaries plus short exact quotes).
HB3731 = https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHAHB3731.pdf (OSHA HB-3731-2014, June 2014) · 3752 = https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3752.pdf (OSHA 3752-12, 2014) · MEMO = https://www.osha.gov/memos/2021-06-30/inspection-guidance-for-tree-care-and-tree-removal-operations (June 24, 2021, Directorate of Enforcement Programs) · 132 = 1910.132 · 269 = 1910.269 · 333 = 1910.333 · TCS = https://www.osha.gov/tree-care/standards

**Scope note.** OSHA has no tree-care-specific standard. TCS: tree care employers must follow the general industry standards (29 CFR 1910). The MEMO says climbing and changing location in trees is assessed under the PPE standard (1910.132 hazard assessment and selection) and that 1910.140 (personal fall protection systems) "apply to personal fall protection systems used to protect workers" in elevated tree work. The 1910.140 page itself could not be fetched (provenance/permission refusal), so the 5,000-lb anchorage line is sourced to the MEMO's quote of 1910.140(c)(13) and is labelled guidance. 1910.269 applies only to line-clearance tree trimming ((a)(1)(i)(E)); the talk only names it and does not state any 1910.269(r) rule (the (r) paragraphs are on a second page of the osha.gov standard that could not be reached).

**Overlap check.** Existing talks tree-felling (drop zones, retreat path), tree-electrical (power lines, line clearance), harness (construction harness check), aerial-lift (construction lifts). This talk is limited to the climber: tree check, gear, tie-in/anchor, second means of fall protection, emergency contact. Power lines get one line pointing to the 10-foot rule.
