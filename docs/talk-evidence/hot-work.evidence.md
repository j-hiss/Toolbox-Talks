# hot-work evidence

Roofing guide = "Fire Protection and Prevention", https://osha.gov/sites/default/files/2019-03/fireprotection.pdf. Hosted on osha.gov but appears to be roofing-industry material (it points to the NRCA/MRCA Certified Roofing Torch Applicator Program); no OSHA author or Alliance disclaimer on the page. Used only for practice tips, never for a numeric rule. Reviewer may prefer to drop these lines if only OSHA-authored guidance is acceptable (marked [RG] below).

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| Torch-applied roofing is a serious fire hazard. [RG] | Roofing guide | "Torch-applied roofing materials pose a serious fire hazard to roofing contractors and building owners." |
| And the fire watch doesn't end when the torch shuts off. | OSHA 1926.352(e) | "...and for a sufficient period of time after completion of the work to ensure that no possibility of fire exists." |
| Move anything that can burn out of the way. | OSHA 1926.352(a) | "all movable fire hazards in the vicinity shall be taken to a safe place, or otherwise protected." |
| If it can't be moved, protect it, and keep the heat and sparks contained. | OSHA 1926.352(a)-(b) | "or otherwise protected."; "positive means shall be taken to confine the heat, sparks, and slag" |
| Never torch directly onto a deck or material that can burn. [RG] | Roofing guide | "Never torch directly to combustible decks or materials" |
| Never torch into an area you can't fully see. [RG] | Roofing guide | "Never torch to areas that cannot be seen fully" |
| Keep the torch away from vents and air intakes. [RG] | Roofing guide | "Not use torches near vents or air intakes" |
| Working on a wall, floor or ceiling? Take the same fire precautions on the other side. | OSHA 1926.352(f) | "the same precautions shall be taken on the opposite side" |
| When normal precautions aren't enough, an extra person is assigned to watch for fire. | OSHA 1926.352(e) | "When the welding, cutting, or heating operation is such that normal fire prevention precautions are not sufficient, additional personnel shall be assigned to guard against fire" |
| That's someone other than the person running the torch. | OSHA 1926.352(e) | "additional personnel" |
| The fire watch stays the whole time the torch is running, and after the work is done, long enough to make sure there's no chance of fire. | OSHA 1926.352(e) | "while the actual welding, cutting, or heating operation is being performed, and for a sufficient period of time after completion of the work to ensure that no possibility of fire exists." |
| Your supervisor will tell you how long. | n/a | Instruction; the standard sets no minutes (see dropped claims). |
| The fire watch has to know what fire hazards to look for and how to use the extinguisher on site. | OSHA 1926.352(e) | "Such personnel shall be instructed as to the specific anticipated fire hazards and how the firefighting equipment provided is to be used." |
| A working fire extinguisher has to be right there in the work area, ready to use. | OSHA 1926.352(d) | "Suitable fire extinguishing equipment shall be immediately available in the work area and shall be maintained in a state of readiness for instant use." |
| Where more than 5 pounds of flammable gas like propane is being used, there must be an extinguisher rated at least 10B within 50 feet. | OSHA 1926.150(c)(1)(vi) | "A fire extinguisher, rated not less than 10B, shall be provided within 50 feet of wherever more than 5 gallons of flammable or combustible liquids or 5 pounds of flammable gas are being used on the jobsite." |
| Never block the path to an extinguisher. | OSHA 1926.150(a)(2) | "Access to all available firefighting equipment shall be maintained at all times." |
| On the building, nobody should have to walk more than 100 feet to reach one. | OSHA 1926.150(c)(1)(i) | "Travel distance from any point of the protected area to the nearest fire extinguisher shall not exceed 100 feet." |
| Set cylinders upright on a firm base, or secure them so they can't fall. | OSHA 1926.153(g) | "Containers shall be upright upon firm foundations or otherwise firmly secured." (paragraph covers containers outside buildings) |
| Never store propane inside a building. | OSHA 1926.153(j) | "Storage of LPG within buildings is prohibited." |
| Where cylinders are stored, there must be an extinguisher rated at least 20-B:C. | OSHA 1926.153(l) | "Storage locations shall be provided with at least one approved portable fire extinguisher having a rating of not less than 20-B:C." |
| Think you have a leak? Never use a flame to find it. Use soapy water and look for bubbles. [RG] | Roofing guide | "they must not use fire to attempt to find the hole. Instead, they are to use soapy water and look for bubbles." |
| And no smoking near the propane or the work. | OSHA 1926.151(a)(3) | "Smoking shall be prohibited at or in the vicinity of operations which constitute a fire hazard" |

Dropped for lack of an OSHA source:
- Any fixed fire-watch duration. 1926.352(e) gives none; the roofing guide says "two hours", but that is industry practice, not an OSHA rule, so it was left out.
- Claims that a typical torch cylinder holds more than 5 lb (not stated in any fetched page).
- Keeping cylinders/hoses away from heat: 1926.153(h)(10) says this, but only for containers used inside buildings, so it was not applied to rooftop work.
