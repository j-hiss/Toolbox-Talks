# material-storage-gi — evidence

1910.176 is short and general (secure storage, aisles, housekeeping, clearance signs, rail cars, pit guarding). It has no paragraph specific to pipe or tubular racks. Pipe/round-stock specifics come from OSHA guidance: the OSHA "Materials Handling and Storage" booklet (fetched at https://obis.osha.gov/Publications/OSHA2236/osha2236.html; the page itself shows no publication number) and the OSHA Oil and Gas eTool, Drilling Ahead, Handling Tubulars.

Overlap: `racking` already covers 1910.176(b) tiered stacking, rack capacity, aisle clearance and ladders for warehouse racking. This talk is narrowed to round stock (pipe, tubulars, bar, drums): rolling, blocking/chocking, rack orientation, stack height marking. Aisle and housekeeping are one line each. `pipe-handling` (oil) covers racks/catwalk/V-door on rigs; this talk repeats only two eTool lines (level racks, chock or pin; work from ends) as they are the core pipe-rack guidance, and does not cover catwalk/V-door/slips.
Construction-only guidance in the booklet (lumber 16/20 ft, bricks 7 ft, masonry taper, 6 ft from hoistways) comes from 1926.250 and was NOT used.

## Hook
- "Pipe, tubing, bar stock and drums are round, and round things roll." Framing; eTool hazard "Being struck by rolling or falling tubulars"; booklet "Block the bottom tiers of drums ... to keep them from rolling". (framing)
- "OSHA warns that falling materials and collapsing loads can crush or pin workers, causing injuries or death." Booklet: "Falling materials and collapsing loads can crush or pin workers, causing injuries or death." (exact)

## The rule
- "OSHA's storage rule for general industry is short: stored material can't create a hazard." 1910.176(b): "Storage of material shall not create a hazard." (exact sense; the fetch summarized (b) as "Stored material must not create a hazard.")
- "Bundles and other material stored in tiers have to be stacked, blocked, interlocked and limited in height, so they're stable and won't slide or collapse." 1910.176(b): bags, containers, bundles, etc., stored in tiers "stacked, blocked, interlocked and limited in height so that they are stable and secure against sliding or collapse." (exact)

## Stop the roll
- "OSHA's guidance says to stack and block poles, structural steel, bar stock and other round material so it can't spread or tilt, unless it's in a rack." Booklet: "Stack and block poles as well as structural steel, bar stock, and other cylindrical materials to prevent spreading or tilting unless they are in racks." (exact)
- "On pipe racks, OSHA's oil and gas guidance says to level the racks and chock or pin the tubulars." eTool Drilling Ahead: "Level your pipe racks properly." "Chock or pin tubulars on the racks properly." (exact sense)
- "Rolling or falling tubulars can strike you." eTool hazard: "Being struck by rolling or falling tubulars." (exact sense)
- "Work tubulars from the ends, from ground level." eTool: "Work the tubulars from the ends from ground level." (exact)
- "Drums on their sides: block the bottom tier so it can't roll, and chock it on both sides so it can't shift either way." Booklet: "Block the bottom tiers of drums, barrels, and kegs to keep them from rolling if stored on their sides"; "Chock the bottom tier of drums, barrels, and kegs on each side to prevent shifting in either direction". (exact sense)
- "Put planks, plywood or pallets between tiers." Booklet: "Place planks, sheets of plywood dunnage, or pallets between each tier of drums, barrels, and kegs". (paraphrase)

## Racks and height
- "Don't store pipe and bars in racks that face main aisles. Pulling one out can put people walking by at risk." Booklet: "Do not store pipes and bars in racks that face main aisles to avoid creating a hazard to passersby when removing supplies". (exact sense)
- "Bound material can go on racks. Secure it by stacking, blocking or interlocking so it can't slide, fall or collapse." Booklet: "workers should consider placing bound material on racks, and secure it by stacking, blocking, or interlocking to prevent it from sliding, falling, or collapsing." (paraphrase)
- "Respect height limits." Booklet: "Observe height limitations when stacking materials;" (paraphrase)
- "OSHA suggests painting stripes on walls or posts to show the maximum stack height." Booklet: "Paint walls or posts with stripes to indicate maximum stacking heights for quick reference;" (paraphrase; guidance, so "suggests")
- "Blocking and timbers should be big and strong enough to hold the load." Booklet: "Blocking materials and timbers should be large and strong enough to support the load safely." (exact sense)

## Around the stack
- "Where forklifts work, aisles need enough room for safe movement, and they stay clear and in good repair." 1910.176(a): where mechanical handling equipment is used, sufficient safe clearances for aisles, at loading docks, through doorways and wherever turns or passage must be made; "Aisles and passageways shall be kept clear and in good repair". (paraphrase)
- "Use the forklift properly." eTool: "Use powered industrial truck (forklift) properly." (exact sense)
- "Where there are clearance limits, signs have to warn of them." 1910.176(e): "Clearance signs to warn of clearance limits shall be provided." (exact)
- "Keep storage areas free of piled-up material that could trip someone or start a fire." 1910.176(c): "Storage areas shall be kept free from accumulation of materials that constitute hazards" from tripping, fire, explosion, or pest harborage. (paraphrase)
- "Unloading pipe from a truck or trailer? Set the brakes and make sure it can't move." Booklet: "Set brakes and put other adequate protection in place to prevent movement of trucks, trailers" (sentence continues; only the start was returned). (paraphrase)
- "Stand clear of suspended, hoisted or moving loads." eTool: "Stand clear of suspended, hoisted, or moving loads." (exact)
