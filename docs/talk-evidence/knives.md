# knives evidence

Guidance page: OSHA Young Worker Safety in Restaurants eTool, Food Preparation, https://www.osha.gov/etools/young-workers-restaurant-safety/food-prep ("eTool Food Prep"). Age-based child-labor rules on that page were left out.

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| Knives, cleavers, and slicers are made to cut, and they'll cut you as easily as the food. | guidance: eTool Food Prep | paraphrase: workers "are exposed to cuts while using sharp kitchen tools such as knives or cleavers." |
| A few habits keep your hands out of the way. | n/a | Framing. |
| Keep your fingers and thumbs out of the cutting line. | guidance | exact: "Keep your fingers and thumbs out of the way of the cutting line." |
| Don't touch the blade, and don't chat with coworkers while you cut. | guidance | exact: "Do not touch knife blades." / "Do not talk with coworkers while using a knife." |
| Keep knives sharp and in good shape. A dull knife slips. | guidance | exact: "Keep knives sharpened and in good condition; dull knives tend to slip and may cause injuries." |
| Only trained, experienced workers should sharpen knives. | guidance | exact: "Allow only experienced, trained workers to sharpen knives." |
| If a knife falls, let it fall. Don't try to catch it. | guidance | exact: "Let a falling knife fall. Do not try to catch it." |
| Put knives back in their storage spot when you're done, never with the edge exposed. | guidance | exact: "Store knives, saws, and cleavers in a designated storage area when not in use." / "Do not store the blades with the cutting edge exposed." |
| Your company has to guard machines to protect you from the blade and other moving parts. | OSHA 1910.212(a)(1), (a)(3)(ii) | exact: "One or more methods of machine guarding shall be provided to protect the operator and other employees in the machine area from hazards such as those created by point of operation, ingoing nip points, rotating parts..." / "The point of operation of machines whose operation exposes an employee to injury, shall be guarded." |
| Use every guard, and never bypass one. | guidance | exact: "Use any machine guarding that is provided." / "Do not bypass safety guards." |
| Use a push stick or tamp to feed food in or pull it out. | guidance | exact: "Always use push sticks or tamps to feed or remove food from these types of machines." |
| Don't push small pieces of meat through a slicer with your hands. | guidance | exact: "Do not use your hands to feed smaller pieces of meat through slicers." |
| Never open a running machine or reach into it to stir or guide food. | guidance | exact: "Do not open up or put your hands into an operating machine to stir contents or guide food." |
| No loose clothing or jewelry around machines. They can get caught. | guidance | exact: "Do not wear loose clothing or jewelry that could become caught in machinery." |
| Turn the machine off and unplug it before you take it apart, clean it, or clear a blockage. | guidance | exact: "Turn off and unplug the machine before disassembling and cleaning." / "Turn off and unplug machinery before cleaning or removing a blockage." |
| On a plug-in machine, unplugging only protects you if the plug stays under your control the whole time, so nobody can plug it back in. | OSHA 1910.147(a)(2)(iii)(A) | paraphrase: the lockout standard does not cover "Work on cord and plug connected electric equipment for which exposure to the hazards of unexpected energization or start up of the equipment is controlled by the unplugging of the equipment from the energy source and by the plug being under the exclusive control of the employee performing the servicing or maintenance." |
| Servicing a machine that can't be unplugged falls under your company's lockout procedures. | OSHA 1910.147(a)(1)(i), (c)(1) | paraphrase: standard "covers the servicing and maintenance of machines and equipment in which the unexpected energization or start up... could cause injury"; "The employer shall establish a program consisting of energy control procedures, employee training and periodic inspections..." Kept general; note (a)(2)(ii) excludes some minor routine servicing during normal production when alternative protection is used. Reviewer: line is limited to "servicing", which is the standard's scope. |
| Your company has to train you on them. | OSHA 1910.147(c)(7)(i) | exact: "The employer shall provide training to ensure that the purpose and function of the energy control program are understood by employees..." |
| If you haven't been trained, ask before you start. | n/a | Instruction, no factual claim. |
| Where your hands could get severe cuts, your company has to choose the right hand protection and require you to wear it. | OSHA 1910.138(a) | exact: "Employers shall select and require employees to use appropriate hand protection when employees' hands are exposed to hazards such as ... severe cuts or lacerations..." |
| When you're given steel mesh or cut-resistant gloves for cutting, wear them. | guidance | paraphrase: "Use any protective clothing provided by employer such as steel mesh or Kevlar gloves." (brand name replaced with "cut-resistant") |
