# home-health evidence

Scope: OSHA has no home-healthcare standard. The OSHA Home Healthcare page (osha.gov/home-healthcare) lists hazards and points to letters of interpretation; it says the bloodborne pathogens standard applies "when occupational exposure is reasonably anticipated" (LOI 1992-07-22). 1910.1030(a): "This section applies to all occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials". Everything else in this talk is NIOSH guidance (kind "guidance").

Overlap with existing library: `vehicles` (distracted driving), `workplace-violence`, `sharps`, `patient-handling` already exist. This talk is narrowed to what's specific to visiting clients' homes: arriving at an unknown home, pets, leaving, unsanitary homes, carrying your own sharps container, the drive between clients.

Dropped for lack of a source or for length:
- "Avoid lifting alone / get a second person": NIOSH 2.4 says a second trained person reduces risk but is not enough to make manual handling acceptably safe; not given as a recommendation. Left out.
- NIOSH 2007 figure (27,400 recorded injuries among 896,800 home healthcare workers): accurate NIOSH figure, but dated; left out.
- Emergency kit (flashlight, batteries, flares, blanket, water): sourced (NIOSH 2012-122), cut for length.
- Verbal-abuse steps, drug-lab odors, daylight scheduling in high-crime areas, working in pairs: sourced (6.5.2), cut for length. Existing `workplace-violence` talk covers de-escalation.
- Home temperature/heat advice (NIOSH 2012-121): sourced, cut for length.
- NIOSH 2010-125 chapter 7 (Animals 7.2.2, Automobile Travel 7.2.9): the PDF text extraction ended in chapter 6, so chapter 7 could not be read. Pet and driving lines use the NIOSH fact cards 2012-121 and 2012-122 instead.
