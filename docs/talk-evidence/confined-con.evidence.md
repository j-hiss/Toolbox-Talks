# confined-con evidence

Paragraph notes: definitions are in 1926.1202 (the standard defines "authorized entrant", not "entrant"). Attendant duties are 1926.1209(a)-(j) (there is no (k)). Entrant duties are 1926.1208. Rescue is 1926.1211; non-entry rescue/retrieval is (c). 1926.1210 (entry supervisor) was not needed.

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| A space that is safe when you go in can turn deadly while you're inside. | guidance: OSHA 3825 p.14 | "a confined space that is safe when entered can become deadly" (exact) |
| And people who jump in to help have died too. | guidance: OSHA 3825 p.1 | "rescue workers have themselves died or been injured because they did not have the training and equipment necessary" (paraphrase) |
| A confined space is big enough to climb into, has a limited way in and out, and isn't built for people to stay in. | OSHA 1926.1202 "Confined space" | "Is large enough and so configured that an employee can bodily enter it; Has limited or restricted means for entry and exit; and Is not designed for continuous employee occupancy." (paraphrase) |
| Think manholes, tanks, pits, vaults, crawl spaces and some attics. | guidance: OSHA 3825 p.4 | List "include[s], but [is] not limited to" manholes, sewers, storm drains, tanks, pits, bins, boilers, silos, transformer vaults, crawl spaces, and attics (paraphrase) |
| It's permit-required if it has, or could have, a bad atmosphere, something that can bury you, walls or a floor that can trap you, or any other serious hazard. | OSHA 1926.1202 "Permit-required confined space" | "Contains or has a potential to contain a hazardous atmosphere; Contains a material that has the potential for engulfing an entrant; Has an internal configuration such that an entrant could be trapped or asphyxiated by inwardly converging walls or by a floor which slopes downward...; or Contains any other recognized serious safety or health hazard." (paraphrase) |
| An attic with easy access usually isn't a confined space. | guidance: OSHA 3914 | "an attic will not be considered a confined space" when access is easy and egress is unimpeded (e.g., pull-down stairs without hand-over-hand climbing) (paraphrase) |
| But extreme attic heat can be a serious hazard. | guidance: OSHA 3914 | "extreme heat in an attic can be considered a serious physical hazard." (exact) |
| A crawl space with a live exposed wire is a hazard too. | guidance: OSHA 3914 | crawl spaces "will not typically trigger the majority of the requirements of the standard" unless a physical hazard exists, such as "an exposed active electric wire." (paraphrase) |
| Before work starts, a competent person finds the confined spaces and decides which ones need a permit. | OSHA 1926.1203(a); guidance: OSHA 3914 | "Before it begins work at a worksite, each employer must ensure that a competent person identifies all confined spaces"; 3914: competent person determines which are permit-required (paraphrase) |
| A sign that says DANGER, PERMIT-REQUIRED CONFINED SPACE, DO NOT ENTER means exactly that. | OSHA 1926.1203(b)(1) and note | Sign wording "DANGER—PERMIT-REQUIRED CONFINED SPACE, DO NOT ENTER" or similar language (exact) |
| You don't go in unless you're authorized and trained. | guidance: OSHA 3914; OSHA 1926.1203(c); 1926.1207(b) | Workers "may not enter such spaces without authorization"; employer not authorizing entry "must take effective measures to prevent those employees from entering"; training "Before the employee is first assigned duties under this standard" (paraphrase) |
| Sticking your head or arm through the opening counts as entering. | OSHA 1926.1202 "Entry" | "any part of a person passes through an opening"; "considered to have occurred as soon as any part of the entrant's body breaks the plane of an opening into the space" (paraphrase) |
| Before anyone enters a permit space, the air gets tested: oxygen first, then flammable gases, then toxic gases. | guidance: OSHA 3825 p.18 | testing "in this sequence: oxygen, combustible gases or vapors, and toxic gases or vapors" (paraphrase) |
| The attendant stays outside the whole time, | OSHA 1926.1209(d) | "Remains outside the permit space during entry operations until relieved by another attendant." (paraphrase) |
| keeps count of who's inside, | OSHA 1926.1209(c) | "Continuously maintains an accurate count of authorized entrants in the permit space" (paraphrase) |
| and orders everyone out if conditions turn dangerous inside or outside. | OSHA 1926.1209(f) | Order exit if "there is a prohibited condition"; behavioral effects apparent; "a situation outside the space that could endanger the authorized entrants"; or the attendant can't do all duties (paraphrase) |
| If you're inside, get out fast when the attendant says so, when an alarm goes off, or when you notice any warning sign or symptom. | OSHA 1926.1208(e) | "Exit from the permit space as quickly as possible whenever: An order to evacuate is given by the attendant or the entry supervisor; There is any warning sign or symptom of exposure to a dangerous situation; The entrant detects a prohibited condition; or An evacuation alarm is activated." (paraphrase) |
| If someone goes down inside, the attendant calls for rescue. | OSHA 1926.1209(g) | "Summons rescue and other emergency services as soon as the attendant determines that authorized entrants may need assistance to escape" (paraphrase) |
| Don't go in after them. | OSHA 1926.1209(d) note; guidance: OSHA 3825 p.15, p.29; OSHA 3914 | Attendant may enter to rescue only if the program allows it and the attendant "has been trained and equipped for rescue operations"; training must "include the dangers of attempting such rescues"; 3914: workers not authorized for entry rescue are trained on the dangers of attempting them (paraphrase) |
| Untrained rescuers have died this way. | guidance: OSHA 3825 p.1 | "rescue workers have themselves died or been injured because they did not have the training and equipment necessary" (paraphrase) |
| Rescue is done from outside whenever the gear can help, using a harness and retrieval line. | OSHA 1926.1211(c); guidance: OSHA 3825 p.30 | "Non-entry rescue is required unless the retrieval equipment would increase the overall risk of entry or would not contribute to the rescue of the entrant."; "Each authorized entrant must use a chest or full body harness, with a retrieval line attached" (paraphrase) |
| For a vertical permit space more than 5 feet deep, a mechanical device to pull people out has to be there. | OSHA 1926.1211(c) | "A mechanical device must be available to retrieve personnel from vertical type permit spaces more than 5 feet (1.52 meters) deep." (paraphrase) |

- Alternate entry procedures (1926.1203(e)), multi-employer coordination (1926.1203(h)) and rescue-service evaluation (1926.1211(a)): employer-side detail; left out.
- "Is 911 your Confined Space Rescue Plan?" fact sheet: referenced by OSHA 3914 but not fetched; not used.
