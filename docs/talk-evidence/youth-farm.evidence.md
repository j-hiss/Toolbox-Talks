# Evidence: youth-farm

Fetched 2026-10-08 via WebFetch: DOL Wage and Hour Division Fact Sheet #40 (https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/40-child-labor-farms, rev. Dec 2016); OSHA Youth in Agriculture eTool home (https://osha.gov/etools/youth-agriculture), Rights and Laws (fetched at the osha.prod.pace.dol.gov mirror of https://www.osha.gov/etools/youth-agriculture/rights-laws; the www page timed out on a permission prompt), Tractors (https://osha.gov/etools/youth-agriculture/tractors), Other Machinery (https://www.osha.gov/etools/youth-agriculture/other-machinery).
No OSHA standard governs ages or tasks for youth; the child labor rules are DOL Wage and Hour Division (FLSA). The fact sheet does not cite 29 CFR 570.71 in the fetched text; I did not fetch 29 CFR 570 itself, so the talk cites the fact sheet only (kind "guidance").
Note: the fetch tool summarized the hazardous-occupations list rather than quoting it in full; lines are marked "paraphrase".

## Hook
- "Agriculture is one of the most dangerous industries in the nation." eTool home (exact): "Agriculture is one of the most dangerous industries in the nation."
- "Young workers are new to its hazards, so we match each job to their age and their training." Framing; supported by FS40 age-based job limits and eTool training guidance.

## The age rules
- "These are federal child labor rules from the U.S. Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division, not OSHA." FS40 is issued by WHD (FLSA youth employment provisions); eTool rights page: federal child labor law, the FLSA, protects young farmworkers.
- "At 16 and older, a young worker can do any farm job at any time." FS40 (exact): "Youths ages 16 and above may work in any farm job at any time."
- "At 14 and 15, they can work outside school hours, but not in jobs the Department of Labor has declared hazardous." FS40 (paraphrase): ages 14-15 may work outside school hours in jobs not declared hazardous by the Secretary of Labor.
- "Kids working on a farm their own parents own or run are not covered by these limits." FS40 (exact): "Youths of any age may work at any time in any job on a farm owned or operated by their parents." The hazardous-occupation ban also does not apply there.
- "Some states have stricter rules, and the more protective rule applies." eTool rights page (paraphrase): when federal and state rules differ, the rule giving more protection to young workers applies. Other Machinery page: state laws may be stricter.
- Dropped: 12-13 and under-12 rules (FS40) — left out for length.

## Off-limits under 16 (FS40 hazardous occupations; all paraphrase of the fetched list)
- "Driving a tractor over 20 PTO horsepower, or hooking up or unhooking its implements." FS40: operating tractors over 20 PTO horsepower, or attaching/detaching implements. Also Tractors page.
- "Running hazardous farm machines, like balers, augers, forklifts and power saws." FS40: certain farm machinery such as harvesters, balers, grinders, dryers, augers; forklifts; power-driven saws.
- "Working in a pen or stall with a breeding bull, boar or stud horse, a sow with suckling pigs, or a cow with a newborn calf." FS40 (paraphrase, close).
- "Working on a ladder or scaffold above 20 feet, riding on a tractor as a passenger or helper, going into manure pits or certain silos, or handling anhydrous ammonia or chemicals labeled Danger, Poison or Warning." FS40: ladders or scaffolds above 20 feet; riding on a tractor as a passenger or helper; certain confined spaces incl. manure pits and upright silos under certain conditions; toxic chemicals labeled "danger," "poison," or "warning" or skull and crossbones; transporting, transferring or applying anhydrous ammonia.
- Dropped for length: timber over 6 inches, driving passengers, explosives, trenchers/earthmoving.

## Training and speaking up
- "A 14- or 15-year-old with a 4-H or vocational agriculture training certificate may run certain equipment they were trained on. Student-learners have a similar exception." FS40 (paraphrase): 14-15-year-old holders of 4-H or vocational agriculture training certificates may work outside school hours on certain equipment they were trained to use; student-learners in vocational ag programs exempt from certain hazardous occupations when requirements are met. Kept general ("certain") because the fetched text did not list which tasks.
- "If you're not sure, ask before the job starts." Action prompt.
- "Every worker has the right to safety training in a language and words they understand, including training about pesticides." eTool home (exact): right to "Receive information and training (in a language and vocabulary the worker understands)." Rights page (exact): "Training about health and safety, including information about pesticides."
- "Get trained on a machine before you run it, and do the pre-start check first." Other Machinery / Tractors (paraphrase): be trained before operating any machinery; complete a pre-operation check and fix problems before starting.
- "You can report a hazard and use your rights without being punished for it." eTool home (exact): "Exercise their rights under the law without retaliation." Rights page: teen workers expected to report hazards.

## Around machines
- "Keep guards in place. Never reach into a machine to clear a jam." Other Machinery (paraphrase): keep guards in place and never remove them to clear a jam; never reach into machinery to clear a blockage.
- "Never allow extra riders on a tractor." Tractors (exact): "Never allow any extra riders on the tractor."
- "Tie back long hair, and skip loose clothes, jewelry and drawstrings near moving parts." Other Machinery (paraphrase): avoid jewelry, drawstrings, ties, loose clothing near moving parts; secure long hair.
