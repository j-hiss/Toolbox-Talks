# Evidence: skylights

Standards fetched 2026-10-07 from osha.gov: 1926.500, 1926.501, 1926.502. Guidance: OSHA construction video tool "Skylight" transcript, OSHA 3755 (Covers), OSHA Stop Falls.

## Hook
- "In one OSHA case, a roofer nailing shingles stepped backward onto a skylight. It broke under him, and he fell 15 feet to the floor below."
  - guidance: OSHA Skylight transcript (https://www.osha.gov/vtools/construction/skylight-fnl-eng-web-transcript): "He was installing shingles in the center of the roof near an unguarded skylight." / "As the worker nailed shingles near the unguarded skylight, he stepped backwards onto it." / "The skylight was not strong enough to hold his weight, and it broke." / "He fell 15 feet, and landed on the floor below."

## Skylights are holes
- "A hole is any gap 2 inches or more at its narrowest point, in a floor, roof, or other surface you walk or work on."
  - 1926.500(b) Hole: "a gap or void 2 inches (5.1 cm) or more in its least dimension, in a floor, roof, or other walking/working surface."
- "The rule names skylights as holes. Treat every one like an open hole."
  - 1926.501(b)(4)(i): "protected from falling through holes (including skylights)". "Treat every one like an open hole" is plain-language framing of the same.
- "If you could fall more than 6 feet through it, it needs a cover or a guardrail around it, or you need to be in a personal fall arrest system."
  - 1926.501(b)(4)(i): "Each employee on walking/working surfaces shall be protected from falling through holes (including skylights) more than 6 feet (1.8 m) above lower levels, by personal fall arrest systems, covers, or guardrail systems erected around such holes."
  - Reviewer note: the standard says "more than 6 feet" here (not "6 feet or more"); the talk keeps "more than".
- "Covers also keep you from tripping or stepping into a hole, and keep things from falling through onto people below."
  - 1926.501(b)(4)(ii): "protected from tripping in or stepping into or through holes (including skylights) by covers." (b)(4)(iii): "protected from objects falling through holes (including skylights) by covers."

## Covers done right
- "A cover must hold at least twice the weight of the workers, equipment, and materials that could be on it at one time."
  - 1926.502(i)(2): "All other covers shall be capable of supporting, without failure, at least twice the weight of employees, equipment, and materials that may be imposed on the cover at any one time."
  - Reviewer note: (i)(1) (roadway covers, twice the max axle load) intentionally left out as not relevant to roofs.
- "Secure it when you put it down, so wind, equipment, or people can't knock it out of place."
  - 1926.502(i)(3): "All covers shall be secured when installed so as to prevent accidental displacement by the wind, equipment, or employees."
- "Mark it HOLE or COVER, or color-code it, so everyone knows what's under it."
  - 1926.502(i)(4): "All covers shall be color coded or they shall be marked with the word "HOLE" or "COVER" to provide warning of the hazard."
- "The skylight's plastic dome is not a cover. In that OSHA case, the dome was the only thing over the opening."
  - guidance: Skylight transcript: "Originally, ... the skylight was only covered by a translucent plastic dome." / "The skylight was not strong enough to hold his weight, and it broke." / "Now, the skylight is guarded by a cover that meets OSHA requirements."
  - Reviewer note: "is not a cover" is inferred from the case (the dome broke under one worker, so it did not meet 1926.502(i)(2)); the transcript does not state it as a general rule. Consider "A plastic dome is not a cover you can count on" if a softer wording is preferred.

## Working near them
- "Guardrails at the roof edge don't protect you from a skylight in the middle. That employer had edge guardrails and thought his crew was protected. He was wrong."
  - guidance: Skylight transcript: "The roof already had guardrails, so the employer thought his workers were protected from fall hazards." / "But, he was wrong." / skylight was "in the center of the roof".
- "Before you start, find every skylight and hole on the roof and plan around them."
  - guidance: OSHA Stop Falls: "think about all of the different fall hazards, such as holes or skylights and leading edges".
- "If a cover has to come off for the work, that hole still needs a guardrail, or you need to be tied off, until the cover goes back on."
  - Application of 1926.501(b)(4)(i): protection from falling through holes more than 6 feet must be "by personal fall arrest systems, covers, or guardrail systems erected around such holes." With the cover off, one of the other two must be in place. Not a separately quoted rule.
- "Watch where you step when you back up while you work. That's how the roofer in that case ended up on the skylight."
  - guidance: Skylight transcript: "he stepped backwards onto it."
