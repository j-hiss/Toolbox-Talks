# nail-gun evidence

Guide = OSHA/NIOSH "Nail Gun Safety: A Guide for Construction Contractors" (OSHA 3459-8-11 / NIOSH 2011-202), fetched from CDC Stacks copy https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/6013/cdc_6013_DS1.pdf (the osha.gov URL in the brief, /sites/default/files/publications/NailgunSafety.pdf, returned 404).

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| Nail guns send an estimated 37,000 people to the emergency room every year. | Guide | "They are also responsible for an estimated 37,000 emergency room visits each year." |
| More than half of nail gun injuries are to the hand and fingers. | Guide | "More than half of reported nail gun injuries are to the hand and fingers." |
| A full sequential trigger only fires when you do things in order: press the tip to the work, then pull the trigger. | Guide | "This trigger will only fire a nail when the controls are activated in a certain order." / "First, the safety contact tip must be pushed into the work piece, then the user squeezes the trigger" |
| It's the safest kind of trigger. | Guide | "This is the safest type of nail gun trigger." |
| A contact trigger, the bump kind, can double fire. | Guide | "contact trigger nailers are susceptible to double firing"; "Bump firing or bounce nailing is using a nail gun with a contact trigger held squeezed" |
| It can also fire if you're holding the trigger and the tip gets knocked. | Guide | "will fire if the trigger is being held squeezed and the safety contact tip gets knocked" |
| The overall injury risk is twice as high with a contact trigger. | Guide | "the overall risk of nail gun injury is twice as high when using contact trigger nail guns" |
| If you're working from a ladder, use a full sequential nailer. | Guide | "If work must be done on ladders, use full sequential trigger nailers" |
| Keep three points of contact with the ladder. | Guide | "Maintain three points of contact with the ladder at all times to prevent a fall" |
| Keep your finger off the trigger when you carry the gun or hold it. | Guide | "keep their fingers off the trigger when holding or carrying a nail gun." |
| Never bypass or disable a safety feature. | Guide | "Never bypass or disable nail gun safety features. This is strictly prohibited." |
| That includes pulling the spring out of the safety contact tip. | Guide | "Tampering includes removing the spring from the safety-contact tip" |
| Keep your other hand at least 12 inches from the nailing point. | Guide | "keep hands at least 12 inches away from the nailing point at all times." |
| Use a clamp to brace the piece if you can. | Guide | "Consider using clamps to brace instead of your hands." |
| Always shoot away from your body and away from the crew. | Guide | "Always shoot nail guns away from your body and away from co-workers." |
| About 1 in 10 nail gun injuries happen to a co-worker. | Guide | "About 1 in 10 nail gun injuries happen to co-workers." |
| Unhook the air when you clear a jam, do any maintenance, leave the gun, or hand it to someone. | Guide | "Always disconnect the compressed air when:" "Leaving a nailer unattended;" "Passing the nail gun to a co-worker;" "Clearing jammed nails;" "Performing any other maintenance on the nail gun." |
| Unhook it before you go up or down a ladder or stairs. | Guide | "Travelling up and down a ladder or stairs;" (same list) |
| Never raise or lower a tool by its hose. | OSHA 1926.302(b)(6) | "The use of hoses for hoisting or lowering tools shall not be permitted." (Guide also: "Never lower the nail gun from above or drag the tool by the hose.") |
| Don't run the hose and fittings above the pressure the manufacturer says is safe. | OSHA 1926.302(b)(5) | "The manufacturer's safe operating pressure for hoses, pipes, valves, filters, and other fittings shall not be exceeded," |
| Check the surface before you nail. Look for knots, old nails, straps and hangers. | Guide | "Check lumber surfaces before nailing. Look for knots, nails, straps, hangers, etc." |
| Knots and metal hardware are common causes of nails bouncing off and flying. | Guide | "Wood knots and metal framing hardware are common causes of ricochets." / "it can bounce off the surface, becoming a projectile." |
| Take extra care with toe-nailing. | Guide | "Take extra care with toe-nailing." |
| Wear safety glasses or goggles marked Z87.1 with side protection, | Guide; OSHA 1926.102(a)(2) | "High Impact eye protection – safety glasses or goggles marked ANSI Z87.1"; "uses eye protection that provides side protection when there is a hazard from flying objects." |
| plus hearing protection, a hard hat and safety shoes. | Guide | "Hearing protection – either earplugs or earmuffs"; "Hard hats"; "Safety shoes, which help protect workers' toes from nail gun injuries" |
| Got hit, even a little? Report it and get medical attention right away. | Guide | "encourage reporting of nail gun injuries"; "seek medical attention immediately after nail gun injuries" |
