# Evidence: harness

Standards fetched 2026-10-07 from osha.gov: 1926.500, 1926.502, Subpart M Appendix C. Guidance: OSHA 3755 "Protecting Roofing Workers".

## Hook
- "Your harness only works if every piece of it works. Check it before every use, not once a week."
  - 1926.502(d)(21): "Personal fall arrest systems shall be inspected prior to each use". First sentence is framing.

## Check your gear
- "Look over your harness, lanyard, and hooks before each use for wear, damage, or anything breaking down."
  - 1926.502(d)(21): "Personal fall arrest systems shall be inspected prior to each use for wear, damage and other deterioration". PFAS components per 1926.500(b): "an anchorage, connectors, a body belt or body harness and may include a lanyard, deceleration device, lifeline".
- "Look for cuts, tears, scrapes, mold, or stretched spots. Look for damage from fire, acid, or other chemicals, bent hooks, and weak hook springs."
  - guidance: Subpart M Appendix C, Section II(g) Inspection considerations: "Any component with any significant defect, such as cuts, tears, abrasions, mold, or undue stretching;" / "damage due to deterioration; contact with fire, acids, or other corrosives; distorted hooks or faulty hook springs;"
  - Reviewer note: "scrapes" = abrasions, "bent" = distorted, "weak" = faulty hook springs.
- "Anything defective comes out of service. Don't patch it and keep going."
  - 1926.502(d)(21): "and defective components shall be removed from service." Second sentence is plain-language framing.
- "If it has stopped a fall, it comes out of service right away. It is not used again until a competent person inspects it and says it's undamaged and fit to reuse."
  - 1926.502(d)(19): "Personal fall arrest systems and components subjected to impact loading shall be immediately removed from service and shall not be used again for employee protection until inspected and determined by a competent person to be undamaged and suitable for reuse."

## Put it on right
- "Adjust the harness so it fits snug. The back D-ring should sit centered between your shoulder blades."
  - guidance: OSHA 3755 (Personal Fall Arrest System): "Adjust the harness to fit snugly." / "The D-ring attachment for the harness should be centered between the worker's shoulder blades".
- "Body belts are not allowed for fall arrest. Full body harness only."
  - 1926.500(b) Personal fall arrest system: "As of January 1, 1998, the use of a body belt for fall arrest is prohibited." guidance: OSHA 3755: "Body belts are not acceptable in a PFAS because they can cause serious injury during a fall".
  - Reviewer note: body belts are still allowed for positioning/restraint; the sentence is limited to fall arrest.
- "Use locking snaphooks only. Never clip a hook to another hook, or straight onto webbing or rope, unless the hook is designed for that connection."
  - 1926.502(d)(5): "Effective January 1, 1998, only locking type snaphooks shall be used." (d)(6): "Unless the snaphook is a locking type and designed for the following connections, snaphooks shall not be engaged: (i) directly to webbing, rope or wire rope; (ii) to each other;"
  - Reviewer note: (d)(6)(iii)-(v) (D-ring with another connector, horizontal lifeline, incompatible objects) left out for length.

## Pick the anchor
- "Your anchor has to hold 5,000 pounds for each person tied to it, or be part of a complete system with a safety factor of at least two, under a qualified person's supervision."
  - 1926.502(d)(15): "capable of supporting at least 5,000 pounds (22.2 kN) per employee attached, or shall be designed, installed, and used as follows: (i) as part of a complete personal fall arrest system which maintains a safety factor of at least two; and (ii) under the supervision of a qualified person."
- "Never share an anchor that's holding up a scaffold or platform. Your anchor has to be separate."
  - 1926.502(d)(15): "Anchorages used for attachment of personal fall arrest equipment shall be independent of any anchorage being used to support or suspend platforms".
- "Tie off at or above your D-ring when you can, and pick a spot that keeps you from swinging wide if you fall."
  - guidance: Appendix C II(k) Free fall considerations: "the tie-off attachment point to the lifeline or anchor should be located at or above the connection point". II(m) Obstruction considerations: "Tie-offs which minimize the possibilities of exaggerated swinging should be considered."
- "Keep your line off sharp or rough edges, and don't tie knots in it. Both cut its strength."
  - guidance: Appendix C II(h)(5): "Tie-off where the line passes over or around rough or sharp surfaces reduces strength drastically." II(h)(3): "Tie-off using a knot in a rope lanyard or lifeline (at any location) can reduce" [lifeline or lanyard strength].

## Clearance and rescue
- "Your system has to be rigged so you can't free fall more than 6 feet or hit anything below you."
  - 1926.502(d)(16)(iii): "be rigged such that an employee can neither free fall more than 6 feet (1.8 m), nor contact any lower level;"
- "Once it starts to catch you, it has to bring you to a full stop within 3.5 feet."
  - 1926.502(d)(16)(iv): "bring an employee to a complete stop and limit maximum deceleration distance an employee travels to 3.5 feet (1.07 m)". Deceleration distance (1926.500(b)) is measured "from the point at which the deceleration device begins to operate."
- "Know the rescue plan before you clip in. A worker hanging in a harness needs help fast."
  - 1926.502(d)(20): "The employer shall provide for prompt rescue of employees in the event of a fall or shall assure that employees are able to rescue themselves."
  - guidance: OSHA 3755 p. 15: "Employers need a plan for rescuing workers in the event of a fall whenever personal fall arrest systems are used." / "a medical emergency, such as suspension trauma, can develop if the fallen worker is not rescued quickly."
