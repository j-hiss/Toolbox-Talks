# Evidence: farm-emergency

Fetched 2026-10-08 via WebFetch: OSHA Agricultural Safety Fact Sheet "Emergency Preparedness for Farmworkers," DSG FS-3870 06/2016 (https://www.osha.gov/Publications/OSHA3870.pdf); OSHA 1928.21.
No OSHA standard: 29 CFR 1928 has no emergency action plan or first aid rule. 1928.21(a) lists the only Part 1910 standards that apply to agriculture (1910.142, 1910.111(a)-(b), 1910.266, 1910.145, 1910.1200, 1910.1027, 1910.1201, 1910.501 limited); 1928.21(b) says the rest of subparts B-T and Z "do not apply to agricultural operations." 1910.38 (subpart E, emergency action plans) and 1910.151 (subpart K, first aid) are not listed. So this talk is guidance (FS-3870) and uses "should," not "must." (Temporary labor camps have their own first-aid rule, 1910.142(k), covered in the labor-camps talk.)
Hint check: "1928.? if any applies" — none applies. The publication number is DSG FS-3870 (06/2016), verified on the PDF.

## Hook
- "Out in the field, there may be no clinic close by." FS-3870 (paraphrase): train at least one person in first aid "if no clinic or hospital is nearby."
- "The plan we make now is what gets someone help fast." Framing.

## Know the plan
- "OSHA's farm emergency guidance says the best plans are built for your own farm and the work you do." FS-3870 (exact): "The best EAPs are customized for your specific farm operations."
- "A good plan shows escape routes, places to shelter, and meeting points on a map. It names who's in charge, so nobody is guessing." FS-3870 (paraphrase): "Emergency escape procedures and routes"; mark escape routes, shelter-in-place locations, and rally points on floor plans or maps; set a chain of command to prevent confusion.
- "It also says how we account for every worker, and who handles rescue and first aid." FS-3870 (paraphrase): procedures to account for all workers; assign rescue and medical duties to designated workers.
- "Think about what could happen here: storms, lightning, wildfire, a fire or explosion, a chemical spill, a grain entrapment, an animal or vehicle incident." FS-3870 (paraphrase): natural — tornadoes, hurricanes, wildfires, floods, severe winter storms, dust storms, lightning, earthquakes; man-made — "explosions or fires," "animal handling incidents," "grain entrapments," "chemical releases or spills," "vehicle incidents."

## Calling for help
- "Emergency phone numbers should be posted where people can see them, inside farm vehicles, and on phones." FS-3870 (exact): "Emergency phone numbers should be posted in visible places, inside farm vehicles, and on telephones."
- "Know the one way we report an emergency on this farm, and who to call with questions about the plan." FS-3870 (paraphrase): state the preferred way to report emergencies; list contacts for questions about the plan.
- "Crews spread out across fields need a way to reach each other and first responders, like two-way radios." FS-3870 (paraphrase): "Emergency communication equipment, such as two-way radios or a public address system" for worker and first responder notification. ("Spread out across fields" is framing.)
- "Local first responders can be invited to walk the farm, so they learn the layout, the hazards and the utility shutoffs before they're ever needed." FS-3870 (paraphrase): invite local first responders to walk the farm and learn its layout, hazards, and utility shut-offs.

## First aid
- "Basic first aid supplies should be on hand." FS-3870 (exact fragment): "it is also essential that basic first-aid supplies are available."
- "If there's no clinic or hospital nearby, at least one person should be trained in first aid." FS-3870 (paraphrase): at least one person should be trained to give first aid where no nearby clinic or hospital exists.
- "Know who that is today." Action prompt.
- "The plan should keep a list of the electrical shutoffs, buildings, livestock and machinery, so responders know what they're walking into." FS-3870 (paraphrase): maintain a farm inventory of livestock, electrical shut-offs, buildings, and machinery make and model numbers. ("so responders know..." is framing, consistent with the first-responder walkthrough item.)

## Practice it
- "Training should be in a language you understand. It covers the alarm, how to evacuate, how to report, how to shut down equipment, and the kinds of emergencies we could face." FS-3870 (paraphrase/exact list): train on "Evacuation plans," "Alarm systems," "Reporting procedures for personnel," "Shutdown procedures," and "Types of potential emergencies," in a language workers understand.
- "Drills should happen every year or as needed. Afterward, and after any real emergency, we talk about what to fix." FS-3870 (paraphrase): run drills or exercises annually or as needed; hold a review meeting after each drill, exercise, or real incident.
- "The plan gets reviewed every year, with every new worker, and whenever it changes." FS-3870 (paraphrase): review the EAP annually, with each new worker, and after plan changes.
