# Evidence: roof-steep

Standards fetched 2026-10-07 from osha.gov: 1926.500, 1926.501, 1926.502. Guidance: OSHA Fact Sheet "Fall Protection in Residential Construction" (12/2010), OSHA 3755 "Protecting Roofing Workers", OSHA Stop Falls.

## Hook
- "On a steep roof, one slip can turn into a slide to the edge. Your protection has to be set up before that slip, not after."
  - Framing only. No number or rule. (Consistent with OSHA 3755 p. 8: "Employers need to develop a plan and ensure the proper equipment".)

## Know your pitch
- "A steep roof is steeper than 4 in 12. Know the pitch before you go up, because the pitch decides which rules apply."
  - 1926.500(b) Steep roof: "a roof having a slope greater than 4 in 12 (vertical to horizontal)."
  - guidance: OSHA 3755 p. 8: "Know the pitch of the roof and follow the appropriate standard." / "Low slope roof – 4:12 slope or less – 29 CFR 1926.501(b)(10)." / "Steep roof – above 4:12 slope – 29 CFR 1926.501(b)(11)."
- "On a steep roof with edges 6 feet or more above a lower level, you need guardrails with toeboards, a safety net, or a personal fall arrest system."
  - 1926.501(b)(11): "Each employee on a steep roof with unprotected sides and edges 6 feet (1.8 m) or more above lower levels shall be protected from falling by guardrail systems with toeboards, safety net systems, or personal fall arrest systems."
- "Warning lines are a low-slope roof tool. They are not on the steep-roof list."
  - 1926.501(b)(10) permits warning lines on low-slope roofs; 1926.501(b)(11) lists only guardrails with toeboards, nets, PFAS. guidance: Residential fact sheet: 1926.501(b)(10) "permits the use of warning lines and safety monitoring systems" for low-sloped roofs.
  - Reviewer note: deliberately did NOT say "safety monitors never count on steep roofs", because a residential fall protection plan can fall back to a safety monitor (1926.502(k)(8)).

## Houses count too
- "Building or roofing a wood-frame home? The same 6-foot rule applies: guardrails, a safety net, or a personal fall arrest system."
  - 1926.501(b)(13): "Each employee engaged in residential construction activities 6 feet (1.8 m) or more above lower levels shall be protected by guardrail systems, safety net system, or personal fall arrest system unless another provision in paragraph (b) of this section provides for an alternative fall protection measure."
  - guidance (fact sheet), definition of residential: "The end-use of the structure being built must be as a home, i.e., a dwelling." / "The structure being built must be constructed using traditional wood frame construction materials and methods."
- "A fall restraint setup can be used instead of fall arrest. That's a harness tied to an anchor at the center of the roof, on a line short enough that you can't reach the edge."
  - guidance (fact sheet): "OSHA allows the use of an effective fall restraint system in lieu of a personal fall arrest system." / "A fall restraint system may consist of a full body harness or body belt that is connected to an anchor point at the center of a roof by a lanyard of a length that will not allow a worker to physically reach the edge of the roof."
  - Talk uses the fact sheet wording "at the center".
- "OSHA did away with the old policy that let home builders use things like slide guards instead of these systems."
  - guidance (fact sheet): "has issued a directive rescinding the Interim Fall Protection Compliance Guidelines for Residential Construction" which allowed employers "to use specified alternative methods of fall protection (e.g., slide guards or safety monitor systems)". "With the issuance of the new directive, all residential construction employers must comply with 29 CFR 1926.501(b)(13)."

## When there's a fall protection plan
- "If the company can show those systems won't work on a job, or would be more dangerous, a qualified person has to write a fall protection plan for that site."
  - 1926.501(b)(13) Exception: "When the employer can demonstrate that it is infeasible or creates a greater hazard to use these systems, the employer shall develop and implement a fall protection plan which meets the requirements of paragraph (k) of § 1926.502."
  - 1926.502(k)(1): "The fall protection plan shall be prepared by a qualified person and developed specifically for the site where ... residential construction work is being performed".
- "Any change to the plan has to be approved by a qualified person, and a copy stays on the job. Ask to see it and know what it says."
  - 1926.502(k)(2): "Any changes to the fall protection plan shall be approved by a qualified person." (k)(3): "A copy of the fall protection plan with all approved changes shall be maintained at the job site."
  - "Ask to see it" is practical advice.

## Anchors on a house
- "Every worker who ties off gets their own harness."
  - guidance: OSHA Stop Falls: "provide a harness for each worker who needs to tie off to the anchor".
- "Your anchor has to hold 5,000 pounds for each person tied to it, or be part of a system a qualified person designs and oversees."
  - 1926.502(d)(15): "capable of supporting at least 5,000 pounds (22.2 kN) per employee attached, or shall be designed, installed, and used as follows: (i) as part of a complete personal fall arrest system which maintains a safety factor of at least two; and (ii) under the supervision of a qualified person."
  - Reviewer note: same simplification as the existing fall talk; the full safety-factor wording is in the harness talk.
- "Don't attach an anchor to sheathing alone, a single truss, or most guardrails. Fasten it into a structural member."
  - guidance: OSHA 3755 pp. 11–12: "it is important not to attach anchors to sheathing, single trusses, or most guardrails." / "Instead of attaching anchors to sheathing alone, attach an anchor to a structural member".
- "Install the anchor the way the manufacturer says, and put it above your work area."
  - guidance: OSHA 3755: "It is important to follow the manufacturer's instructions when installing anchorage." / "installing the anchorage point above the work area".
