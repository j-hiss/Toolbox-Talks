# high-pressure evidence

OSHA has no standard specific to high-pressure lines and iron on well sites. The OSHA Oil and Gas Extraction hazards
page lists no OSHA standard for its high-pressure section; it points to API/IADC industry documents (not used as
sources here) and to OSHA's Pressure Vessels and Compressed Gas topic pages. Every line below rests on OSHA guidance
(kind "guidance"). Narrowed from the existing "oil-struck-by" talk, whose "Pressure lines" section already covers:
line whip when connections fail, internal erosion/bursts, "secure lines that could whip", and the work-zone rule. This
talk adds matching/ratings, hammer unions, inspection, pressure testing, check valves, pinch points, operator at
controls, and bleeding off / trapped pressure.

Pages fetched: osha.gov/etools/oil-and-gas/servicing/special-services; /well-completion; /servicing/general-services;
/general-safety/equipment-condition; osha.gov/oil-and-gas-extraction/hazards.

## Dropped for lack of an OSHA source
- "Never hammer on (strike) a pressurized line / union." Searched the Special Services, General Services, Well
  Completion and Equipment Condition pages; none says this. Dropped.
- "Line of fire" as a term does not appear on these pages; the talk uses "stand clear" wording the eTool uses.
- "Whip checks" by name do not appear; the eTool says "anchor" and "hobble" lines, which the talk uses.
