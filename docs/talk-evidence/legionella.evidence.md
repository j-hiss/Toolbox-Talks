## Title
- "Legionnaires' Disease and Building Water" (title)

## Hook
- "Legionnaires' disease is a serious lung infection you get by breathing in fine mist from contaminated water."
  - guidance: OSHA home (exact): "a serious, potentially deadly, lung infection (i.e., pneumonia)"; exposure "especially through 'respirable fine mists small enough to inhale'" from water systems, cooling towers, or HVAC systems (paraphrase).
- "OSHA says it has a 10 percent death rate."
  - guidance: OSHA home (exact): "a serious condition with a 10-percent death rate."

## What it is
- "Legionnaires' disease is a serious, sometimes deadly pneumonia."
  - guidance: OSHA home (paraphrase of "serious, potentially deadly, lung infection (i.e., pneumonia)").
- "Pontiac fever is a milder, flu-like illness from the same bacteria."
  - guidance: OSHA home (paraphrase): Pontiac fever is "a less serious infection with milder symptoms similar to the flu"; both are legionellosis caused by Legionella bacteria.
- "It's not contagious. You get it from breathing in mist small enough to inhale, not from another person."
  - guidance: OSHA home (exact): "Legionnaires' disease is not contagious"; "respirable fine mists small enough to inhale."
- "Most healthy people don't get sick."
  - guidance: OSHA hazards (exact): "Most healthy people do not get sick after exposure to Legionella."
- "People over 50, smokers, and people with lung disease or a weak immune system are most at risk."
  - guidance: OSHA hazards (paraphrase): people "over 50 years old, smokers, or those with underlying medical conditions" are most susceptible, e.g. "chronic lung disease or immunosuppression."

## Where it grows
- "Watch cooling towers, hot water systems, hot tubs and spas, humidifiers, misters, and decorative fountains."
  - guidance: OSHA hazards (exact list items): "Cooling towers, evaporative condensers, and fluid coolers..."; "Potable water systems and domestic hot water systems."; "Spas, whirlpools, and hot tubs."; "Humidifiers, misters, foggers, and decorative or display fountains creating water spray."
- "It grows in warm water, between 68 and 122 degrees F."
  - guidance: OSHA hazards (exact): "In temperatures between 68° and 122°F (20°-50°C)."
- "Still water, scale, sediment, and slimy biofilm help it grow."
  - guidance: OSHA hazards (paraphrase): "When water ceases to flow or move, biofilms can develop."; scale leads to biofilm; "Organic particles suspended in water provide a source of nutrients"; "Slimy films of bacteria and other microorganisms can provide a source of nutrients for Legionella."
- "Dead legs and lines that don't circulate let water sit still. Water that sits is where trouble starts."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention (paraphrase): dead legs (capped or altered piping) let hot water sit still, and non-recirculated lines have the same effect. Second sentence is plain framing of the stagnation point above.

## Keeping water safe
- "There's no OSHA standard just for Legionella."
  - guidance: OSHA standards (exact): "There currently is no specific OSHA standard for Legionellosis."
- "OSHA can still use the General Duty Clause, which says your company has to keep the workplace free of recognized serious hazards."
  - guidance: OSHA standards (paraphrase): employers must provide a workplace "free from recognized hazards that are causing or are likely to cause death or serious physical harm"; OSHA may enforce this where a serious hazard has no specific standard, citing Legionella exposure in water systems.
- "A water management program is how a building keeps Legionella down: good design, upkeep, and routine cleaning and disinfection."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention (paraphrase): "Water management programs that effectively prevent Legionella growth in water systems rely on" control and prevention measures, including good system design, proper maintenance, and routine cleaning and disinfection.
- "CDC has a toolkit for writing one."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention names CDC's "Developing a Water Management Program to Reduce Legionella Growth and Spread in Buildings" toolkit as helping employers develop, implement, and assess these programs. Named as CDC, not OSHA.
- "OSHA's guidance: store hot water at 140 degrees F or higher, and deliver it at 122 degrees F or higher to every outlet."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention (exact): "Storing domestic hot water at a minimum of 60°C (140°F)" / "delivering hot water at a minimum of 50°C (122°F) to all outlets."
- "Clean and disinfect cooling towers at least twice a year."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention (exact): "Cleaning and disinfecting cooling towers at least twice a year."
- "Clean new systems and ones that sat unused for a long time, too."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention (paraphrase): also clean systems that have been out of service for an extended period, and new systems.

## Cleaning a tower
- "For tower cleaning, OSHA's guidance calls for at least the PPE for the chemicals you're using, plus a half-face respirator with an N-100 cartridge."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention (exact): "Workers engaged in tower cleaning must wear, at a minimum, the PPE required for any chemicals being used, and" "a half-face air-purifying respirator (APR) equipped with an N-100 cartridge." Reviewer note: guidance page wording uses "must"; the talk frames it as OSHA's guidance, since there is no standard.
- "Working with biocides? Wear eye protection, gloves, and the protective clothing the chemical maker recommends."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention (exact): "Equip workers using biocides to clean and disinfect systems with eye protection" "protective gloves, and suitable protective clothing as recommended by the chemical manufacturer."
- "A respirator has to seal. If a beard or big mustache gets in the way, you may need a different type, like a full-face or powered respirator."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention (paraphrase): "workers with facial hair that interferes with the respirator seal (beards and/or large moustaches)" may need full facepiece negative-pressure respirators, PAPRs, or SARs.
- "It all runs through your company's respirator program."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention (exact): "employers who permit voluntary use of respirators must implement a respiratory protection program" (meeting 1910.134). Reviewer note: page states this for voluntary use; required use under 1910.134 also needs a program (OSHA standards page lists 1910.134). Talk keeps it general.
- "If there's a known or suspected outbreak, anyone who may be exposed to the mist must wear a respirator."
  - guidance: OSHA control-prevention (exact): "In the event of a known (i.e., identified) or suspected Legionellosis outbreak, workers who may be exposed to aerosolized Legionella must wear respirators."
