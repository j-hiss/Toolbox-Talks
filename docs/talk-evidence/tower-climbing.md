Notes / corrections:
- 1910.269(g)(2) does not fit this talk: 1910.269 covers electric power generation, transmission and distribution, not communication towers. Its climbing rule is (g)(2)(iv)(C)(3) (qualified climbers must use fall protection on poles/towers from April 1, 2015, unless infeasible or a greater hazard); its work-positioning free-fall limit is 2 feet ((g)(2)(iv)(D)); 4-foot threshold in (g)(2)(iv)(C)(2). Not used.
- OSHA's Communication Towers compliance page lists 1926 Subpart M (1926.501-503), 1926.104-105, 1910.268 and 1910.132 as applicable standards. The talk's 1926.502 lines are prefixed "On construction work" or follow from that line; tower maintenance may fall under general industry (1910.268 / 1910 Subpart I), whose rules were not fetched.
- OSHA 3877 states it is "advisory in nature and informational in content"; lines from it are worded "best practices call for" / "should".
- The hoisting directive is CPL 02-01-056, dated July 2014 (not 2002). Its PDF is at osha.gov/sites/default/files/enforcement/directives/CPL_02-01-056.pdf.
- The directive does not contain an explicit "never ride the headache ball/load line" sentence; the talk uses its exact "no other load" and "gin pole raising line" provisions instead.

## Fact-check (independent reviewer, 2026-10-07)
- "You stay connected" cut: neither OSHA 3877 nor CPL 02-01-056 defines 100 percent tie-off as continuous attachment. Replaced with the source term: "Supervisors should enforce 100 percent tie-off at all times when anyone is climbing." OSHA 3877 p.7: "enforcing 100 percent tie-off at every worksite at all times when employees are climbing." (CPL App. A VIII.D.2 also: "maintain 100 percent tie-off while moving between the hoist line and the tower.")
