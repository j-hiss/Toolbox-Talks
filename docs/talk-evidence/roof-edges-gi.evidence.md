# roof-edges-gi evidence

General industry fall protection is 29 CFR 1910.28 (duty) and 1910.29 (criteria), with definitions in 1910.21(b). All fetched from osha.gov standardnumber pages on 2026-10-08. 1910.28 is written as employer duties ("The employer must ensure..."), so the talk says "your company has to" for duties.

Overlap check: existing `roof-lowslope` (con, 1926.501/502 warning lines and safety monitors) and `skylights` (con, 1926.501(b)(4)) cover construction. This talk is narrowed to the general industry rules (4-foot trigger, 1910.28(b)(13) 6/15-foot bands, temporary-and-infrequent exception, 1910.29(d) warning lines), which differ from construction. `dock-edges` (wh) covers dock edges, so docks are left out here.

Notes and corrections:
- 1910.28(b)(1) has only (i)-(iii) (no (iv)). (b)(1)(i) is the 4-foot rule.
- Skylights are 1910.28(b)(3)(i), not (b)(1). Low-slope roofs are (b)(13), as given.
- Warning lines / designated areas are 1910.29(d); covers 1910.29(e).
- 1910.29(d)(2) distances (6 ft for temporary and infrequent work, 15 ft for other work) were not separately stated in the talk; the talk uses the 1910.28(b)(13) bands instead.
- Dropped: anything on skylight screens (no screen provision found in 1910.29); "never step on a skylight dome" (no source fetched); the inspection/assessment exception 1910.28(a)(2)(ii) (left out to keep the talk short).
- "Quick" / "once in a while" are plain-language words for the rule's "temporary" / "infrequent"; the interpretation letter's definitions are given in the talk.
