# patient-handling evidence

**OSHA has no safe patient handling standard.** OSHA's Guidelines for Nursing Homes state they "are not a new standard or regulation and do not create any new OSHA duties," that "An employer's failure to implement the guidelines is not a violation, or evidence of a violation," and that "the extent of an employer's obligation to address ergonomic hazards is governed by the general duty clause" (OSH Act 5(a)(1)). Every line below is guidance, worded as advice ("should", "experts recommend"), not as a rule. The talk itself does not claim any rule.

Dropped for lack of a source:
- "Never lift a patient alone": no fetched OSHA page says this; replaced with the eTool-supported "some transfers take more than one person".
- Lift teams: mentioned only as one hospital's practice (Tampa General profile), not as a recommendation.
- Named device types (sit-to-stand, ceiling lift, friction-reducing sheets): not on fetched pages.
- Body-mechanics coaching (bend knees, avoid twisting): the PT eTool lists relying on body mechanics instead of lifting devices as a risk factor, so no technique tips were added.
- $20 billion back-injury cost and 2020 hospital injury rates: left out for length.

Notes / corrections:
- No OSHA standard exists for patient handling; code field says so. The OSHA healthcare safe-patient-handling page itself does not state this; the statement comes from the Nursing Home Guidelines.
- The "OSHA hospital eTool" has no single patient-handling page; used the Physical Therapy and Work-related MSDs eTool pages plus the osha.gov/hospitals patient-handling pages. osha.gov/etools/hospitals/hospital-wide-hazards/ergonomics returned 404; osha.gov/ergonomics/standards returned 404.
- The 35-pound figure appears in OSHA 3708 as "Experts recommend"; OSHA does not name the source in that text.
