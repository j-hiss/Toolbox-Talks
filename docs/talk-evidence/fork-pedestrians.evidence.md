# fork-pedestrians evidence

PED = eTool Pedestrian Traffic (https://www.osha.gov/etools/powered-industrial-trucks/workplace/pedestrian-traffic); TM = eTool Traveling & Maneuvering (https://www.osha.gov/etools/powered-industrial-trucks/operating-forklift/traveling-maneuvering).

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| A forklift can't stop suddenly. | guidance: PED | "Be aware that lift trucks cannot stop suddenly." (paraphrase) |
| Drivers, never assume the people on foot know you're there. | guidance: TM | "Never assume pedestrians or bystanders are aware of the presence of heavy equipment and the intended direction of travel." (paraphrase) |
| Walkers, stay out of the way. | guidance: PED | "Stand clear of lift trucks in operation." (paraphrase) |
| Use the marked walkways. If there aren't any, stay to one side of the aisle. | guidance: PED | "Use pedestrian walkways, or stay to one side of the equipment aisle." (paraphrase) |
| Stand clear of a forklift that's working. | guidance: PED | "Stand clear of lift trucks in operation." (paraphrase) |
| Watch the back end. It swings wide. | guidance: PED | "Be aware of the wide rear swing radius." (paraphrase) |
| Give a moving forklift plenty of room. Remember it can't stop fast. | guidance: TM; PED | TM: "Allow plenty of room for pedestrians." (operator side); PED: "lift trucks cannot stop suddenly" (paraphrase) |
| Nobody stands or walks under the raised forks or load, loaded or empty. That's the rule. | OSHA 1910.178(m)(2) | "No person shall be allowed to stand or pass under the elevated portion of any truck, whether loaded or empty." (paraphrase); PED: "Do not allow anyone to stand or pass under the load or lifting mechanism." |
| Stay out of the path where a load could fall, and keep a safe distance from the people working. | guidance: PED | "Employees should stay out of the potential path where a load can fall." "Maintain a safe clearance from coworkers." (paraphrase) |
| Drivers: never drive up to someone standing in front of a bench, a rack or anything else fixed. | OSHA 1910.178(m)(1) | "Trucks shall not be driven up to anyone standing in front of a bench or other fixed object." (paraphrase; "rack" is an example of a fixed object) |
| No riders, unless riding is allowed and there's a safe place made for it. | OSHA 1910.178(m)(3) | "Unauthorized personnel shall not be permitted to ride on powered industrial trucks." Second sentence requires a safe place to ride where riding is authorized. (paraphrase) |
| Keep arms and legs inside the truck and out of the mast. | OSHA 1910.178(m)(4) | "The employer shall prohibit arms or legs from being placed between the uprights of the mast or outside the running lines of the truck." (paraphrase) |
| Slow down and sound your horn at cross aisles and anywhere you can't see. | OSHA 1910.178(n)(4) | "The driver shall be required to slow down and sound the horn at cross aisles and other locations where vision is obstructed." (paraphrase) |
| Look where you're going and keep a clear view. | OSHA 1910.178(n)(6) | "The driver shall be required to look in the direction of, and keep a clear view of the path of travel." (paraphrase) |
| If the load blocks your view, drive with the load behind you. | OSHA 1910.178(n)(4) | "If the load being carried obstructs forward view, the driver shall be required to travel with the load trailing." (paraphrase) |
| Can't see? Don't move. | guidance: PED | "Do not move the truck if you do not have a clear view of travel." (paraphrase) |
| Use a spotter, and use the mirrors where they're installed. | guidance: PED; TM | "Use a spotter for blind spots."; "Use spotters, rear view mirrors, or other aids to increase visibility."; "Install convex mirrors at blind aisle intersections." (paraphrase) |
| Yield to people on foot, wait for them to pass, and make eye contact when you can. | guidance: PED | "Yield right of way to pedestrians." "Wait until the pedestrians pass by." "When possible, make eye contact with pedestrians or other forklift operators." (paraphrase) |
| Sound your horn or alarm when you back up. | guidance: PED | "Sound the horn or other alarm when you back up." (paraphrase) |
| Ask: Where are the blind corners in this building, and how do you get past them safely, on foot and driving? | n/a | Discussion prompt. |

Paragraph-number notes: 1910.178(o) (loading) covers load stability, rated capacity and long/high loads ((o)(1)-(o)(4)); it does not address people on foot, so it was not cited. Pedestrian load-clearance lines come from (m)(2) and the PED eTool instead.

Dropped / not used:
- Eye contact as a pedestrian duty: the eTool puts eye contact on the operator only, so the talk assigns it to drivers.
- Three-truck-length following distance ((n)(1)): truck-to-truck, not about pedestrians.
- Any stopping distance or speed figure: none in fetched pages.
