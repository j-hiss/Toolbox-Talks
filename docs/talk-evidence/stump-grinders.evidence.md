## Title
- "Stump Grinder Safety" (title)

## Hook
- "A stump grinder's wheel can pull a person in."
  - guidance: FACE 18CA002 (exact): "The rope caught in the stump grinder's grinding wheel and pulled the victim into its rotating motion."
- "In a California case that NIOSH helped fund, a rope tied to a running grinder caught the wheel and pulled a worker in headfirst. He died the next day."
  - guidance: FACE 18CA002 (paraphrase): Summary: rope tied around his waist and attached to the machine "caught in the grinding wheel and pulled him headfirst into the rotating disc. He died the next day from a blunt force head injury." Program: California FACE, "in cooperation with ... NIOSH, which funds the state-based FACE programs." Grinder was operating, wheel rotating, at the time.

## Before you start
- "There's no general OSHA standard written just for stump grinders. This talk uses NIOSH and OSHA guidance."
  - No stump-grinder standard found; OSHA landscaping and tree-care pages offer no stump grinder rule, only the FACE report link. See standard note.
- "Get trained on the machine you're running."
  - guidance: FACE Rec #1 (exact): "Ensure that workers who operate stump grinders are trained on and consistently follow safety policies and procedures."
- "In that California case, neither worker had formal stump grinder training."
  - guidance: FACE key facts (paraphrase): "Neither worker had formal stump grinder training."
- "Check the area for debris and buried objects before you set up, and make sure the machine sits stable."
  - guidance: FACE Rec #1 (exact): "Check for debris and buried objects before positioning the stump grinder, making sure the machine is stable."
- "This machine can grind below ground."
  - guidance: FACE (paraphrase): the rented grinder "could be lowered below ground level to grind" the roots.
- "If the job means digging, call for utility locates first."
  - guidance: OSHA landscaping hazards (exact, Irrigation and Hardscape Construction solutions): "Call for utility locates." Reviewer note: OSHA lists this for digging work, not for stump grinders by name; the talk ties it to "if the job means digging".

## The danger zone
- "Only the operator should be near the machine while it grinds."
  - guidance: FACE (exact): "Only the operator should be present during stump grinding operation."
- "In the NIOSH case, the maker's manual said to keep everyone else at least 75 feet away. Check the manual for your machine."
  - guidance: FACE (exact): "The manufacturer's instruction manual warns against allowing additional workers within 75 feet while grinding." and Rec #1: "Ensure no other people are within 75 feet while operating the stump grinder." Reviewer note: 75 feet is that manufacturer's figure, not an OSHA rule; the talk says so. "NIOSH case" = the NIOSH-funded FACE report.
- "Debris gets thrown. Look around for people and property, and use a barrier when you can."
  - guidance: FACE Rec #1 (exact): "Be mindful of surroundings to ensure there will not be property damage or injury to persons if debris is thrown from the machine." / "When possible, use a barrier to repel any debris from the cutting action."
- "Wear safety goggles."
  - guidance: OSHA landscaping hazards (exact, Tree Care solutions): "Wear safety goggles." Reviewer note: listed under tree care generally, not stump grinders by name.
- "Keep good footing and a clear way out. On a slope, keep the machine and yourself below the stump, and stay off steep slopes."
  - guidance: FACE Rec #1 (paraphrase): operator should have good footing and a clear exit path; position the machine and operator below the stump on slopes; avoid steep slopes.

## The wheel and the guards
- "Never tie a rope or chain to a running grinder. The eye hook is only for moving the machine when it's off."
  - guidance: FACE Rec #1 (exact): "ropes or chains should never be attached to a stump grinder while it is in operation." / "An eyehook or other attachment point should only be used for moving the machine (when off)".
- "Keep the guards and enclosures in place while it runs."
  - guidance: NIOSH ECD 190 (paraphrase): "ANSI Z133.1-2006 requires that stump cutters be equipped with enclosures and guards to reduce the risk of injury"; the page adds operators should keep those enclosures and guards in place while the machine is running. Reviewer note: this is an ANSI requirement as reported by NIOSH; the talk states it as practice, not as an OSHA rule.
- "Never modify or alter the grinder."
  - guidance: FACE Rec #1 (paraphrase): "Never modifying or altering the stump grinder."
- "Keep your hands and feet away from the wheel."
  - Practical line, derived from the hazard below; no source states it in these words. Flag for checker.
- "Moving parts and sharp edges on these machines can crush, cut off, burn, or blind."
  - guidance: NIOSH ECD 190 (paraphrase): machines "have moving parts, sharp edges, and hot surfaces" that can cause crushed fingers or hands, amputations, burns, or blindness. (Burns come from hot surfaces; the talk groups them.)
- "Need to clear chips or get near the wheel? Ask your supervisor for the shutdown steps for this machine before you do it."
  - Plain "ask your supervisor" prompt (allowed per rulings). No fetched source gives a shutdown rule for clearing chips on a stump grinder, so no rule is stated.

## Line-clearance work
- "On line-clearance tree trimming jobs near power lines, OSHA's power line standard has its own equipment rules that cover stump cutters."
  - guidance: OSHA eTool (paraphrase): the equipment requirements in "1910.269(r)(2) through (8)" apply to qualified employees and line-clearance tree trimmers who clear lines with stump cutters, among other tools.
- "Your supervisor will go over them before that work."
  - Plain supervisor prompt; no rule stated.
- "Not sure if a job counts as line-clearance work? Stop and ask before you start."
  - Plain stop-and-ask prompt.
