# sharps evidence

Scope: 1910.1030 "applies to all occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials" (1910.1030(a)). This talk is for healthcare crews with that exposure.

Dropped for lack of a source or for length:
- "Activate the safety feature right after use": no fetched OSHA page says this.
- Container "fill line" (e.g. three-quarters full): not in 1910.1030; dropped, kept "never overfilled".
- PEPline phone number (CDC hotline on the OSHA page): accurate, but a phone number in a read-aloud talk is not useful; left out.
- Sharps injury log, (h)(5): applies only to employers who keep OSHA 300 records; cut for length.
- "31 needlesticks per 100 average daily census" EPINet figure: third-party data; left out.
- Needlestick Safety and Prevention Act: not described on fetched pages.

## Fact-check (independent reviewer, 2026-10-07)

New hook: "A used needle can expose you to germs carried in blood, like hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV." Source: osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens, "Bloodborne pathogens are infectious microorganisms in human blood..." naming HBV, HCV, HIV; "Needlesticks and other sharps-related injuries may expose workers to bloodborne pathogens."
