# workplace-violence evidence

There is no specific OSHA standard for workplace violence ("There are currently no specific OSHA standards for workplace violence." — osha.gov/workplace-violence). `code` therefore cites the General Duty Clause, OSH Act Sec. 5(a)(1), which OSHA's enforcement page names as the basis; all sources are kind "guidance" (the OSH Act has no standardnumber page).

Pages abbreviated: WV = osha.gov/workplace-violence; FS = Workplace Violence Fact Sheet FS-3509; 3148 = OSHA 3148 (healthcare/social service guidelines); 3153 = OSHA 3153 (late-night retail); RES = /workplace-violence/resources. 3148 and 3153 are written for specific industries; only their general, cross-industry points are used, and phrased as advice, not rules.

Dropped for lack of an OSHA source:
- Specific de-escalation techniques (calm voice, don't match the threat, acknowledge feelings, give space). The fetched OSHA pages only name "conflict resolution and aggression management techniques" as training topics without describing them, so the talk only says "calm it down when you can."
- A specific list of personal warning signs (body language, weapon, intoxication cues). Not listed as such in any fetched page; kept to "shouting, pushing, threats."
- Raw counts (740 of 5,283 fatalities in 2023; 458 homicides). Available on WV but left out for a spoken talk; only the "third-leading cause" ranking is used.
- Any claim that a worker has a legal right to refuse a job: the fetched pages frame this as employer policy, so the talk only gives the advice.
