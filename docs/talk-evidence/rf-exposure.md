# rf-exposure evidence

Note: OSHA has no enforceable general-industry RF exposure limit. OSHA's RF overview page: "There are no specific OSHA standards for radiofrequency and microwave radiation issues." Its Standards page: the 1910.97 limit "is expressed in voluntary language" and "has been ruled unenforceable for Federal OSHA enforcement." Guidance on osha.gov for antenna work is thin, so this is a short talk (about 270 English words).

Notes / corrections:
- 1910.97 is advisory as the assignment said; the page itself does not use the word "advisory" — OSHA's RF Standards page says "voluntary language" and "unenforceable." Talk says "written as a recommendation" and "can't be enforced."
- 1926.54(l) is a mandatory construction limit (microwave), so it's stated as a rule but scoped to construction.
- The "Elements of a Comprehensive RF Protection Program" page is a 1995 presentation by the director of OSHA's Health Response Team, hosted on osha.gov but marked "not DOL or OSHA controlled material and is provided here for reference only." All lines from it use "should" / "some programs". Reviewer should decide whether this source is acceptable; if not, the "Power down and check" section and the marked-zones line lose their support.
- OSHA 3877 mentions RF only once (owners should have "methods for managing radio frequency hazards").

## Fact-check (independent reviewer, 2026-10-07)
- Removed source "Elements of a Comprehensive RF Protection Program" (role-of-rf-measurements; 1995, marked not DOL/OSHA controlled). Cut every line it alone supported: marked zones/barricades, keep-out, time and distance, lockout/tagout, competent person, monitors, "fence", report RF burn. (hazards-solutions keep-away/distance lines are about RF sealers/heaters, not antennas.)
- Rebuilt sections 2-3 from 1910.97 and OSHA RF pages only:
  - "averaged over any 6-minute period": 1910.97(a)(2)(i) "averaged over any possible 0.1-hour period" (exact).
  - "Those are not OSHA rules.": RF Standards page, "These are NOT OSHA regulations." (exact).
  - "Your eyes and testicles may be harmed by RF levels well above the guide": 1910.97(a)(2)(ii) "some parts of the human body (e.g., eyes, testicles) may be harmed if exposed to incident radiation levels significantly in excess of the recommended levels" (paraphrase).
  - AM tower crane-cable burns, sparks before/after grasping, insulate hook or ground cable, PPE "rubber-insert leather gloves, long sleeve shirts, safety helmets and safety glasses": OSHA HIB 1990-09-05 (linked from osha.gov/radiofrequency-and-microwave-radiation/health-effects) (paraphrase/exact).
- Tower-owner line: OSHA 3877 p.13 "All towers should have ... methods for managing radio frequency hazards" under the tower-owner bullet.
