# formaldehyde evidence

Standard: 29 CFR 1910.1048 fetched from osha.gov. Health effects, odor, first aid and spill practice from 1910.1048 Appendix A (osha.gov 1910.1048AppA), which is the standard's substance technical guideline. Uses from osha.gov/formaldehyde. Note: the standard has no (g) spill paragraph; leaks and spills are (j) housekeeping, emergencies are (k), training is (n).

| Formaldehyde shows up in more places than you'd think. | guidance: osha.gov/formaldehyde | paraphrase: page lists many uses |
| It's a preservative in medical labs, a sterilizer, an embalming fluid, and it's in resins used to make particle board, plywood and treated fabrics. | guidance: osha.gov/formaldehyde | exact: "well known as a preservative in medical laboratories, as an embalming fluid, and as a sterilizer"; resins used "as adhesives in the production of particle board and plywood" and "in the treating of textiles" |

| Some people feel it in their eyes, nose and throat at levels between 0.5 and 2 parts per million. | 1910.1048 App A | exact: "Concentrations of 0.5 to 2.0 ppm may irritate the eyes, nose, and throat of some individuals." |
| Liquid formaldehyde is a severe skin irritant. | 1910.1048 App A | exact: "Formalin is a severe skin irritant and a sensitizer." (formalin = aqueous solution) |
| It can also make you allergic, so later contact brings on a rash or hives. | 1910.1048 App A | paraphrase: previously exposed people may develop "an allergic eczematous dermatitis or hives" |
| Some people have gotten asthma or bronchitis from it, and it has the potential to cause cancer. | 1910.1048 App A | exact: "Some persons have developed asthma or bronchitis following exposure to formaldehyde"; "Formaldehyde has the potential to cause cancer in humans." |
| Don't trust your nose. Over time you get used to the smell and the sting, and that can lead to overexposure. | 1910.1048 App A | exact: "The perception of formaldehyde by odor and eye irritation becomes less sensitive with time as one adapts to formaldehyde. | Your company has to keep your exposure at or below 0.75 ppm over 8 hours, and 2 ppm over any 15 minutes. | 1910.1048(c)(1), (c)(2) | exact: employer shall assure no employee is exposed above "0.75 parts formaldehyde per million parts of air (0.75 ppm) as an 8-hour TWA"; not to exceed "two parts formaldehyde per million parts of air (2 ppm) as a 15-minute STEL" |
| Where the air goes over those limits, it's a regulated area with signs. Only trained, authorized people go in. | 1910.1048(e)(1)(i), (e)(2) | exact: "establish regulated areas where the concentration of airborne formaldehyde exceeds either the TWA or the STEL"; entrances posted (legend includes "AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY"); access limited to authorized persons who have been trained |
| For liquids with 1 percent or more formaldehyde, all eye and skin contact has to be prevented. | 1910.1048(h)(1)(i) | exact: "All contact of the eyes and skin with liquids containing 1 percent or more formaldehyde shall be prevented" |
| That means protective clothing formaldehyde can't get through, plus goggles or a face shield. | 1910.1048(h)(1)(i) | paraphrase: chemical protective clothing "made of material impervious to formaldehyde" and chemical splash goggles or face shields as appropriate |
| If you wear a face shield and it could reach your eyes, wear goggles too. | 1910.1048(h)(1)(iii) | exact: when a face shield is worn, "chemical safety goggles are also required if there is a danger of formaldehyde reaching the area of the eye" |
| Where eyes could get splashed with a solution of 0.1 percent or more, there has to be an eyewash in the work area. | 1910.1048(i)(3) | exact: solutions of "0.1 percent or greater formaldehyde"; eyewash "within the immediate work area for emergency use" |
| Where skin could get splashed with 1 percent or more, there has to be a quick drench shower. | 1910.1048(i)(2) | exact: quick drench showers where skin may be splashed with solutions of "1 percent or greater formaldehyde" |
| Splashed in the eyes? Flush right away with lots of water, lifting your lids, for at least 15 to 20 minutes. Then get medical help. | 1910.1048 App A | exact: "Wash the eyes immediately with large amounts of water occasionally lifting lower and upper lids" until no chemical remains "(at least 15 to 20 minutes)"; then get medical attention immediately |
| Splashed on skin? Get the wet clothes and shoes off right away, and wash with soap and lots of water for at least 15 to 20 minutes. | 1910.1048 App A | exact: "Remove contaminated clothing (including shoes) immediately"; wash with "soap or mild detergent and large amounts of water" "(at least 15 to 20 minutes)" |
| Your company has to look for leaks and spills on a regular basis. | 1910.1048(j)(1) | exact: "conduct a program to detect leaks and spills, including regular visual inspections" |
| Spills get cleaned up promptly by trained people wearing the right protection. | 1910.1048(j)(3) | paraphrase: "all leaks are repaired and spills are cleaned promptly" by employees wearing suitable protective equipment and trained in proper methods |
| Big spill? Leave the area quickly unless you have emergency duties. Don't touch the spilled material. | 1910.1048 App A | exact: "If a spill of appreciable quantity occurs, leave the area quickly unless you have specific emergency duties"; "Do not touch spilled material." |
