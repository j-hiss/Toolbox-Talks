# loading-docks evidence

LH = eTool Load Handling, truck trailers and railroad cars section (https://www.osha.gov/etools/powered-industrial-trucks/operating-forklift/load-handling); LD = eTool Loading Docks (https://www.osha.gov/etools/powered-industrial-trucks/workplace/loading-docks).

Important scope note: OSHA states that FMCSA braking rules "preempt OSHA from enforcing the requirements in 29 CFR 1910.178(k)(1) and 29 CFR 1910.178(m)(7) for commercial motor vehicles (CMVs)" (LH; Interpretation 2005-11-08). For non-CMV trailers OSHA enforces chocking. Separately, 1910.26(d) requires "measures, such as wheel chocks or sand shoes" to keep the vehicle from moving while employees are on the dockboard. The talk therefore says the trailer "has to be held so it can't move ... brakes set and wheels chocked, or the other method your site uses" and does not claim chocks specifically are always an OSHA requirement. Reviewer should confirm this framing.

Paragraph-number corrections to the brief:
- 1910.178(m)(7) does exist and is relevant (brakes set, wheel blocks, fixed jacks, trailer floor check); used.
- Dockboard securing/capacity is in 1910.178(n)(11) and 1910.26 (a), (c), (d). The LH eTool still cites the old "1910.30(a)" numbering for dockboards; that text moved to 1910.26 in the 2016 walking-working surfaces rule. Current 1910.30 is training only, so it is not cited.
- 1910.28(b)(4) covers fall protection for employees on dockboards (4 ft, with an exception for motorized materials handling up to 10 ft with 1910.30 training) and 1910.28(b)(1)(iii) covers loading-dock edges where fall protection is infeasible. Fetched but not used: too conditional for a spoken line.

Dropped / not used:
- "Trailer creep" as a term, and vehicle restraints (dock locks): no fetched OSHA page names them. The hazard is covered as "the trailer moves."
- Lighting level 2 lumens/sq ft (1910.178(h)(2)): a truck-equipment rule, left out in favor of the eTool's plain advice.
- Railroad car rules ((k)(2), (k)(4)): kept the talk to trailers.
