| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing on your truck should leak, spill, blow off or fall off. | 393.100(b) | exact: "to prevent the cargo from leaking, spilling, blowing or falling from the motor vehicle." |
| And nothing should shift enough to change how the truck handles. | 393.100(c) | paraphrase: prevent shifting "to such an extent that the vehicle's stability or maneuverability is adversely affected." |
| That's what the cargo securement rules are for. | 393.100(b)-(c) | framing |
| This is a DOT rule, not an OSHA rule. It's the FMCSA cargo securement rule in 49 CFR Part 393. | 49 CFR 393 subpart I | factual (Title 49, Chapter III = FMCSA, DOT) |
| It covers trucks, truck tractors, semitrailers, full trailers and pole trailers. | 393.100(a) | exact: "The rules in this subpart are applicable to trucks, truck tractors, semitrailers, full trailers, and pole trailers." |
| Cargo has to be firmly immobilized or secured. That can be the truck's own structure, dunnage, shoring bars, tiedowns, or a mix. | 393.106(b) | exact: "Cargo must be firmly immobilized or secured on or within a vehicle by structures of adequate strength," "dunnage or dunnage bags, shoring bars, tiedowns or a combination of these." |
| The system has to hold the load in a hard stop and against forces pushing it backward and to the side. The rule sets those at 0.8 g forward and 0.5 g backward and sideways. | 393.102(a)(1)(i)-(iii) | exact: "0.8 g deceleration in the forward direction;" "0.5 g acceleration in the rearward direction;" "0.5 g acceleration in a lateral direction." ("hard stop" = forward deceleration, paraphrase) |
| Every tiedown has a working load limit. It's the lowest rating of any part in it, or of the anchor point it hooks to, whichever is less. | 393.108(a) | exact: "is the lowest working load limit of any of its components" "or the working load limit of the anchor points to which it is attached, whichever is less." |
| Add up the working load limits of all the tiedowns on a piece of cargo. The total must be at least half the weight of that cargo. | 393.106(d) | exact: aggregate WLL of tiedowns "must be at least one-half times the weight of the article or group of articles." |
| Go by the rating marked on the strap, chain or binder. | 393.108(b) | exact: marked tiedowns "shall be considered to have a working load limit equal to the value for which they are marked." |
| If it isn't marked, the rule gives it a set default value from its tables. | 393.108(c)-(f) | paraphrase: unmarked synthetic cordage = polypropylene rope value (table); unmarked chain = grade 30 proof coil; unmarked wire rope = one-fourth nominal strength; manila per tables |
| Don't guess. | 393.108 | framing of the above |
| If nothing blocks the cargo from sliding forward: 5 feet long or less and 1,100 pounds or less needs at least one tiedown. | 393.110(b), (b)(1) | exact: "When an article is not blocked or positioned to prevent movement in the forward direction ... it must be secured by at least:" "One tiedown for articles 5 feet (1.52 meters) or less in length, and 1,100 pounds (500 kg) or less in weight" |
| 5 feet or less but over 1,100 pounds needs at least two. | 393.110(b)(2)(i) | exact: "5 feet (1.52 meters) or less in length and more than 1,100 pounds (500 kg) in weight" (two tiedowns) |
| Over 5 feet and up to 10 feet needs at least two, no matter the weight. | 393.110(b)(2)(ii) | exact: "Longer than 5 feet (1.52 meters) but less than or equal to 10 feet (3.04 meters) in length, irrespective of the weight." |
| Over 10 feet needs two, plus one more for every 10 feet, or part of 10 feet, past the first 10. | 393.110(b)(3) | exact: "Two tiedowns if the article is longer than 10 feet (3.04 meters)," "and one additional tiedown for every 10 feet (3.04 meters) of article length, or fraction thereof," "beyond the first 10 feet (3.04 meters) of length." |
| If a headerboard, bulkhead or other blocking keeps it from moving forward, it needs at least one tiedown for every 10 feet of length, or part of 10 feet. | 393.110(b) lead-in; 393.110(c) | exact (c): "If an individual article is blocked, braced, or immobilized to prevent movement in the forward direction" ... "at least one tiedown for every 3.04 meters (10 feet) of article length, or fraction thereof." |
| Before you drive, make sure the cargo is secured. | 392.9(a), (b)(1) | exact (b)(1): driver must "Assure himself/herself that the provisions of paragraph (a) of this section have been complied with before he/she drives." |
| Then inspect the cargo and tiedowns within the first 50 miles. | 392.9(b)(2) | exact: "Inspect the cargo and the devices used to secure the cargo within the first 50 miles after beginning a trip." |
| Check again whenever you change duty status, or after 3 hours or 150 miles of driving, whichever comes first. | 392.9(b)(3)(i)-(iii) | exact: "The driver makes a change of his/her duty status"; "The commercial motor vehicle has been driven for 3 hours"; "The commercial motor vehicle has been driven for 150 miles, whichever occurs first." |
| No damaged or weakened straps, chains or anchor points. | 393.104(b), (c) | exact: devices "must be in proper working order" with "no damaged or weakened components"; tiedown anchor points with "no damaged or weakened components." |
| No knots. | 393.104(f)(1) | exact: "Tiedowns and securing devices must not contain knots." |
| Every tiedown has to stay tight and fastened while you're moving. | 393.104(f)(3) | exact: "Each tiedown must be attached and secured in a manner that prevents it from becoming loose," (or unfastening, opening or releasing) "while the vehicle is in transit." |
| Use edge protection wherever a tiedown could get rubbed or cut. | 393.104(f)(4) | exact: "Edge protection must be used whenever a tiedown would be subject to abrasion or cutting" (at the point where it touches cargo) |
