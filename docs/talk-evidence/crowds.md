# crowds evidence

No OSHA standard covers crowd management. All lines come from the OSHA fact sheet "Crowd Management Safety Guidelines for Retailers" (DTSEM 11/2012), https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/Crowd_Control.pdf. These are recommendations ("should"), so the talk keeps them as guidance, not rules. Note: the fact sheet's publication code is DTSEM 11/2012, not FS-3800.

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| A big sale can bring a crowd that pushes hard at the doors. | guidance: Crowd fact sheet | paraphrase: barricade breaks/turns "reduce the risk of customers pushing from the rear and possibly crushing others, including workers." |
| OSHA names the dangers to staff: overcrowding, crowd crushing, getting hit by the crowd, violence, and fire. | guidance: Crowd fact sheet | exact: "Prepare an emergency plan that addresses potential dangers facing workers, including overcrowding, crowd crushing, being struck by the crowd, violent acts and fire." |
| There should be a staffing plan that puts each worker in a set spot, like an entrance or a sale area. | guidance | exact: "Create a detailed staffing plan that designates a location for each worker." / "(e.g., near the entrances and throughout the store)." |
| Know your spot before the event. | guidance | paraphrase of staffing plan + "Ensure that workers are properly trained to manage the event." |
| One manager makes the key calls during the event, and one worker is named to call emergency responders. | guidance | exact: "Designate a store manager to make key decisions as needed during the event." / "Designate a worker to contact local emergency responders if necessary." |
| Find out who they are. | n/a | Instruction, no factual claim. |
| You should be trained on the crowd plan and the emergency plan, and get a chance to practice it. | guidance | exact: "Train workers in crowd management procedures and the emergency plan. Provide them with an opportunity to practice the special event plan." |
| If you haven't been, speak up. | n/a | Instruction, no factual claim. |
| Barricades or rope lines go up well before customers arrive. | guidance | exact: "Set up barricades or rope lines for crowd management well in advance of customers arriving at the store." |
| The line should not start right at the door. | guidance | exact: "Make sure that barricades are set up so that the customers' line does not start right at the entrance to the store." |
| The line needs breaks and turns along the way. That keeps people in the back from pushing and crushing the people in front, including you. | guidance | exact: "Ensure that barricade lines have an adequate number of breaks and turns at regular intervals to reduce the risk of customers pushing from the rear and possibly crushing others, including workers." |
| Shopping carts and anything that could trip people or get thrown stay inside, away from the entrance, not out in the parking lot. | guidance | paraphrase: "Locate shopping carts and other potential obstacles or projectiles inside the store and away from the entrance, not in the parking lot." |
| Staff working outside need a radio or another way to reach the people inside and emergency responders. | guidance | exact: "Make sure that outside personnel have radios or some other way to communicate with personnel inside the store and emergency responders." |
| Staff use a separate entrance. | guidance | exact: "Provide a separate store entrance for staff." |
| Everyone working the event should know when the doors are about to open. | guidance | exact: "Make sure that all employees and crowd control personnel are aware that the doors are about to open." |
| If you're managing the crowd coming in, stand to the side of their path, not in the middle of it. | guidance | exact: "Position security or crowd managers to the sides of entering (or exiting) public, not in the center of their path." |
| When the store reaches maximum occupancy, nobody else comes in until the number drops. | guidance | exact: "When the store reaches maximum occupancy, do not allow additional customers to enter until the occupancy level drops." |
| Never block or lock an exit door, and never block the way out. | guidance | exact: "Do not restrict egress, and do not block or lock exit doors." (Also a rule in 1910.36(d)(1) for exit route doors; not cited since assignment is guidance only.) |
| Know where the first-aid kit and the AED are, and who on site is trained in CPR and the AED. | guidance | paraphrase: "Keep first-aid kits and Automated External Defibrillators (AEDs) available, and have personnel trained in using AEDs and CPR onsite." |
| In an emergency, follow what police, fire, or medical responders tell you, even if it's different from company rules. | guidance | exact: "Instruct employees, in the event of an emergency, to follow instructions from authorized first responders, regardless of company rules." |
| Ask: Where is your spot for this event, and who is the person who calls emergency responders if we need them? | guidance | ties to staffing plan and designated worker lines above. |
