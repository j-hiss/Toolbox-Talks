# near-miss evidence

All "OSHA recommends" lines are guidance (Recommended Practices for Safety and Health Programs), not regulations.

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| A near miss is a warning. Nobody got hurt this time. Report it, and we fix it before somebody does. | guidance: osha.gov/safety-management/hazard-Identification | Hook; paraphrase of "Investigate close calls/near misses" and "By thoroughly investigating incidents and reports, you will identify hazards that are likely to cause future harm." |
| If you see a hazard or have a close call, say something. OSHA recommends every workplace have a way to report injuries, close calls, hazards and concerns. | guidance: osha.gov/safety-management/worker-participation (action item 2) | exact: "Encourage workers to report safety and health concerns"; "Establish a process for workers to report injuries, illnesses, close calls/near misses, hazards" (and other safety and health concerns) |
| Talking to management about safety is protected. Your company can't punish you for it. | guidance: OSHA 3812 | exact: protected activity "Communicating about safety or health matters to management personnel"; "These rights are guaranteed under section 11(c) of the Occupational Safety and Health Act" |
| Some companies offer a way to report without your name. OSHA recommends that, so fear of getting in trouble doesn't stop anyone from speaking up. | guidance: worker-participation (action item 2) | exact: "Include an option for anonymous reporting to reduce fear of reprisal" |
| Near misses get looked into, the same as injuries. That's how we find the hazards that would cause the next injury. | guidance: hazard-Identification (action item 4) | exact: "Investigate close calls/near misses."; "By thoroughly investigating incidents and reports, you will identify hazards that are likely to cause future harm." |
| A good investigation doesn't stop at "somebody made a mistake." It looks for the root cause, and there's often more than one. | guidance: hazard-Identification (action item 4) | exact: "a good incident investigation does not stop when it concludes that a worker made an error."; "identify the root causes (and there is often more than one)" |
| People stop speaking up when investigations blame a person instead of the conditions that caused it. | guidance: worker-participation (action item 5) | paraphrase: "Participation will be suppressed ... if investigations focus on blaming individuals rather than the underlying conditions that led to the incident" |
| Easy, cheap fixes get done right away, like housekeeping or clearing a loose cord someone could trip on. | guidance: osha.gov/safety-management/hazard-prevention | paraphrase: "Promptly implement any measures that are easy and inexpensive—e.g., general housekeeping," removing obvious trip hazards such as loose cords |
| Bigger fixes take time. Until then, a temporary control goes in to keep people safe. | guidance: hazard-prevention | exact: "Use interim controls while you develop and implement longer-term solutions." |
| You often know the hazard best. Tell your supervisor how you'd fix it. | guidance: hazard-prevention | paraphrase: "Involve workers, who often have the best understanding of the conditions that create hazards"; "Get input from workers who may be able to suggest and evaluate solutions" |
| OSHA recommends that companies let any worker call for a pause or shutdown of unsafe work. Know how that works here. | guidance: worker-participation (action item 2) | exact: "Empower all workers to initiate or request a temporary suspension or shut down of any work activity or operation they believe to be unsafe" |
| The law also protects you if you refuse truly dangerous work, but only when all of these are true: where you could, you asked your company to fix it and they didn't; you refuse in good faith; a reasonable person would agree there's a real danger of death or serious injury; and there isn't time to fix it through normal channels, like an OSHA inspection. | guidance: osha.gov/workers/right-to-refuse | paraphrase of "protected if all of the following conditions are met": "Where possible, you have asked the employer to eliminate the danger, and the employer failed to do so"; "You refused to work in 'good faith.' This means that you must genuinely believe that an imminent danger exists"; "A reasonable person would agree that there is a real danger of death or serious injury"; "There isn't enough time, due to the urgency of the hazard, to get it corrected through regular enforcement channels, such as requesting an OSHA inspection." |
| If you refuse, tell your supervisor you won't do it until it's fixed. Ask to have it fixed or for other work, and stay on site unless you're told to leave. | guidance: right-to-refuse | paraphrase: "Ask your employer to correct the hazard, or to assign other work"; "Tell your employer that you won't perform the work unless and until the hazard is corrected"; "Remain at the worksite until ordered to leave by your employer." |
