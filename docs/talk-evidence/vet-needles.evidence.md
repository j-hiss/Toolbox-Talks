(OSHA's bloodborne pathogens standard, 1910.1030, is about human blood; it was not fetched this session, so nothing in the talk relies on it. Source quality flag (important for the checker): official NIOSH material on livestock needlesticks is thin. The NIOSH veterinary pages give only one sentence on sharps. The practical steps come from two "Monthly Safety Blast" pieces by the Southwest Center for Agricultural Health, Injury Prevention, and Education (UT Health Center at Tyler), a NIOSH-funded agricultural center; they are indexed in NIOSHTIC and hosted on CDC Stacks, but they are not NIOSH publications. The Nebraska FACE report is a state FACE investigation supported by NIOSH. The assignment's hint "NIOSH 2012-? needlestick injuries in livestock" did not turn up: no NIOSH-numbered livestock needlestick publication was found. If the reviewer requires NIOSH-authored sources only, the section "Hold the animal still" and most of "Handle needles right" would have to go, and the talk would fall below 200 words.

No product names: the Nebraska FACE report names the antibiotic by brand; the talk says only "a cattle antibiotic".
