# ethylene-oxide evidence

Scope: 1910.1047(a)(1) "applies to all occupational exposures to ethylene oxide (EtO)", with the (a)(2) exception for products shown by objective data not to release EtO at or above the action level. Appendix A is non-mandatory; its sterilizer section (VII, "Sterilant Use of EtO in Hospitals and Health Care Facilities") restates EPA recommendations, so the talk introduces it as "OSHA's guidance for hospitals" and its sources entry is kind "guidance".

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| Ethylene oxide sterilizes surgical tools and is used in some plants. | Fact sheet | exact: "as well as during hospital sterilization of surgical equipment"; "EtO is found in the production of solvents, antifreeze, textiles, detergents, adhesives". |
| It can cause cancer, and by the time you can smell it, you're far over the limit. | Fact sheet; App A | paraphrase: "EtO is a carcinogen that may cause leukemia and other cancers"; App A exact: "EtO does not have a detectable odor except at levels well above the permissible exposure limits." |
| Ethylene oxide, or EtO, is a flammable, colorless gas. | Fact sheet | exact: "Ethylene oxide (EtO) is a flammable, colorless gas at temperatures above 51.3 ºF". |
| It may cause leukemia and other cancers, and it's linked to miscarriage and nerve damage. | Fact sheet | exact: "may cause leukemia and other cancers"; "EtO is also linked to spontaneous abortion, genetic damage, nerve damage". |
| Short exposures can cause eye pain, sore throat, trouble breathing, headache, nausea and dizziness. | Fact sheet | exact: "In addition to eye pain and sore throat, exposure to EtO can cause difficult breathing and blurred vision."; paraphrase: dizziness, nausea, headache, vomiting also listed. |
| You usually can't smell it until it's around 700 ppm. | App A (non-mandatory) | exact: "Colorless gas with ether-like odor detected at approximately 700 parts EtO per million parts of air (700 ppm)." |
| The limit is 1 ppm. Your nose is not a detector. | 1910.1047(c)(1); App A | exact: "in excess of one (1) part EtO per million parts of air (1 ppm) as an 8-hour time-weighted average"; App A: no detectable odor "except at levels well above the permissible exposure limits." |
| No one may be exposed above 1 ppm averaged over 8 hours, or above 5 ppm over any 15 minutes. | 1910.1047(c)(1)-(2) | paraphrase: TWA 1 ppm (8-hour); excursion limit "5 parts of EtO per million parts of air (5 ppm) as averaged over a sampling period of fifteen (15) minutes." |
| At 0.5 ppm, the action level, more steps kick in. | 1910.1047(b) "Action level" | exact: "a concentration of airborne EtO of 0.5 ppm calculated as an eight (8)-hour time-weighted average." (Triggers monitoring (d) and medical surveillance (i).) |
| One is medical exams for people exposed at or above it for 30 days or more a year. | 1910.1047(i)(1)(i)(A) | paraphrase: surveillance for "all employees who are or may be exposed to EtO at or above the action level, without regard to the use of respirators, for at least 30 days a year." |
| Where exposure may go over the limits, your company sets up a regulated area. | 1910.1047(e)(1) | paraphrase: "The employer shall establish a regulated area wherever occupational exposure to airborne concentrations of EtO may exceed the TWA or wherever the EtO concentration exceeds or can reasonably be expected to exceed the excursion limit." |
| Only authorized people go in, and the sign warns it may cause cancer. | 1910.1047(e)(2); (j)(2)(i)(A) | exact: "Access to regulated areas shall be limited to authorized persons."; sign legend includes "MAY CAUSE CANCER" and "AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY". |
| If you could be exposed at or above the action level or the 15-minute limit, you get EtO training when you're first assigned and at least once a year after that. | 1910.1047(j)(3)(i) | exact: "The employer shall provide employees who are potentially exposed to EtO at or above the action level or above the excursion limit with information and training on EtO at the time of initial assignment and at least annually thereafter." |
| OSHA's guidance for hospitals says to take a load out right when the purge cycle ends. If you can't, run the purge again before you open the door. | App A VII.2.c (non-mandatory) | exact: "A load treated in a sterilizer equipped with a purge cycle should be removed immediately upon completion of the cycle"; "If this is not done, the purge cycle should be repeated before opening door." |
| Move loads to the aerator quickly, and pull carts, don't push them, so you stay out of the gas coming off the load. | App A VII.2.d (non-mandatory) | paraphrase: "to transfer treated loads quickly, thus avoiding excessive contact with treated articles"; exact: "they should be pulled not pushed by the sterilizer operators to avoid offgassing exposure." |
| Where exposure goes over the limits, your company's written plan has to include a schedule for leak checks. | 1910.1047(f)(2)(i)-(ii) | paraphrase: written compliance program required where the TWA and/or excursion limit is exceeded; exact (f)(2)(ii): "The compliance program shall include a schedule for periodic leak detection surveys". |
| If you can smell EtO while wearing a respirator, get to fresh air right away. | App A | exact: "If you can smell EtO while wearing a respirator, proceed immediately to fresh air." |
| Where an emergency is possible, your workplace has to have a written emergency plan. | 1910.1047(h)(1)(i) | exact: "A written plan for emergency situations shall be developed for each workplace where there is a possibility of an emergency." |
| In a release, people who could be exposed are warned and get out right away. | 1910.1047(h)(2) | paraphrase: "means shall be developed to alert potentially affected employees of such occurrences promptly"; affected employees "shall be immediately evacuated". |
| If liquid EtO soaks your clothes or shoes, take them off right away. | App A (first aid) | exact: "If EtO soaks through your clothing, especially your shoes, remove the clothing immediately". |
| Contaminated leather shoes get thrown out. | App A | exact: "Contaminated leather shoes or other leather articles should not be reused and should be discarded." |
| If it gets in your eyes, flush with lots of water. | App A | paraphrase: "wash your eyes immediately with large amounts of water, lifting the lower and upper eyelids." |
| Don't try a rescue until you've told at least one other person. | App A | exact: "attempt rescue only after notifying at least one other person of the emergency". |
| If you were exposed in an emergency, your company has to make a medical exam available to you. | 1910.1047(i)(1)(i)(B), (i)(2)(i)(D) | paraphrase: exams made available to "all employees who have been exposed to EtO in an emergency situation", as medically appropriate. |
| Ask: Where's the emergency plan for our sterilizer area, and what do you do first if the alarm goes off? | App A VII.1.h | Discussion prompt. App A calls for "An audible and visual indicator alarm system ... to alert personnel of ventilation system failures". |

Dropped / not used:
- "Employee rotation is prohibited" (fact sheet): accurate but employer-side; cut for length.
- Monitoring frequency (every 6 months / every 3 months, (d)(3)): employer-side; cut.
- Respirator situations ((g)(1)(i)-(iv), including emergencies): cut for length; the "respirators" talk covers fit and use.
- Leak-check frequency "every two weeks" (App A VII.2.e.i): non-mandatory EPA recommendation; left out to avoid it reading as a rule.
- Recordkeeping (30 years): employer-side; cut.

Notes / corrections:
- 1910.1047(h)(1) does not use the word "alarm"; the alerting duty is (h)(2). The alarm for ventilation failure is in non-mandatory App A VII.1.h.
- Container labels are (j)(2)(ii)(A), not (j)(1)(iii) ((j)(1)(iii) is access to labels and SDS).
