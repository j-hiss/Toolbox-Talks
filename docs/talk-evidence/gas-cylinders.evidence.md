# gas-cylinders evidence

Standard pages: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.101 ; https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.253
Guidance: OSHA interpretation letter "Safety of Compressed Gas Cylinders on Portable Carts", Nov. 18, 2021 (https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2021-11-18).

Scope note for reviewer: the storage and handling rules quoted from 1910.253 sit in the oxygen-fuel gas welding and cutting standard. 1910.101(b) covers all compressed gases but incorporates CGA P-1-1965, which could not be fetched (not on osha.gov). The talk applies the 1910.253 rules as the plant/warehouse practice; if the app needs strict scope, add a line saying these rules are written for oxygen and fuel gas cylinders.

| A gas cylinder looks tough, but rough handling, knocks or a fall can damage the cylinder, the valve or its safety devices and cause a leak. | 1910.253(b)(5)(iii)(B) | exact: "Rough handling, knocks, or falls are liable to damage the cylinder, valve or safety devices and cause leakage." |
| Today is about storing and moving them right. | n/a | Framing. |
| OSHA says cylinders must be upright or valve end up. | guidance: OSHA interpretation 2021-11-18 | exact: "OSHA standards require the compressed gas cylinders to be in an upright position (vertical) or valve end up position." |

| Each cylinder goes in an assigned spot where it can't be knocked over, hit by passing or falling objects, or tampered with. | 1910.253(b)(2)(ii) | exact: "Assigned storage spaces shall be located where cylinders will not be knocked over or damaged by passing or falling objects, or subject to tampering by unauthorized persons." |
| Keep that spot away from elevators, stairs and walkways. | 1910.253(b)(2)(ii) | paraphrase: "Cylinders should be stored in definitely assigned places away from elevators, stairs, or gangways." ("should" in the source; talk uses plain instruction, no "must".) |
| Store them dry and ventilated, away from radiators and other heat. | 1910.253(b)(2)(i)-(ii) | exact: "Cylinders shall be kept away from radiators and other sources of heat."; "stored in a well-protected, well-ventilated, dry location" (inside buildings). |
| Never in a closed locker or cupboard. | 1910.253(b)(2)(ii) | exact: "Cylinders shall not be kept in unventilated enclosures such as lockers and cupboards." |
| Inside a building, keep them at least 20 feet from highly combustible material like oil. | 1910.253(b)(2)(ii) | exact: "at least 20 feet (6.1 m) from highly combustible materials such as oil or excelsior." |
| Your company has to make sure cylinders are in safe condition, as far as a visual check can tell. | 1910.101(a) | exact (part): "Each employer shall determine that compressed gas cylinders under his control are in a safe condition" to the extent determinable by visual inspection. |
| If one looks damaged, report it. | n/a | Instruction supporting 1910.101(a). |
| If the cylinder takes a valve cap, the cap stays on hand-tight unless the cylinder is in use or hooked up for use. | 1910.253(b)(2)(iv) | exact: "Valve protection caps, where cylinder is designed to accept a cap, shall always be in place, hand-tight, except when cylinders are in use or connected for use." |
| Close the valve when work is finished, and close the valve on every empty cylinder. | 1910.253(b)(5)(ii)(G), (H); (b)(2)(iii) | exact: "Cylinder valves shall be closed when work is finished."; "Empty cylinders shall have their valves closed." |
| In storage, oxygen cylinders stay at least 20 feet from fuel gas cylinders and combustible materials. | 1910.253(b)(4)(iii) | exact (part): "Oxygen cylinders in storage shall be separated from fuel-gas cylinders or combustible materials (especially oil or grease), a minimum distance of 20 feet" |
| If you can't get 20 feet, there has to be a barrier at least 5 feet high with at least a half-hour fire rating between them. | 1910.253(b)(4)(iii) | paraphrase: "or by a noncombustible barrier at least 5 feet (1.5 m) high having a fire-resistance rating of at least one-half hour." |
| Keep oxygen away from oil and grease. | 1910.253(b)(4)(i) | exact: "Oxygen cylinders shall not be stored near highly combustible material, especially oil and grease" |
| Before you move one, close the valve, take off the regulator and put the cap on, unless it's secured on a special cylinder truck. | 1910.253(b)(5)(ii)(D), (F) | exact: "Unless cylinders are secured on a special truck, regulators shall be removed and valve-protection caps, when provided for, shall be put in place before cylinders are moved."; "Cylinder valves shall be closed before moving cylinders." |
| Never drop a cylinder or let cylinders bang into each other. | 1910.253(b)(5)(ii)(B) | exact: "Cylinders shall not be dropped or struck or permitted to strike each other violently." |
| Never use one as a roller or a support, full or empty. | 1910.253(b)(5)(ii)(K) | exact: "Cylinders shall never be used as rollers or supports, whether full or empty." |
| Never lift a cylinder by its cap. | 1910.253(b)(5)(ii)(C) | paraphrase: valve-protection caps shall not be used for lifting cylinders. |
| With a crane, use a cradle or platform, never a sling or a magnet. | 1910.253(b)(5)(ii)(A) | exact (part): "When transporting cylinders by a crane or derrick, a cradle, boat, or suitable platform shall be used." Slings or electric magnets shall not be used for this purpose. |

Dropped / not used:
- "Chained/secured so it can't fall" as a general-industry rule: "secured in an upright position at all times" is construction (1926.350(a)(9)); 1910.253 has no "upright"/"secured" wording. Talk uses the 2021 interpretation (upright/valve end up) and (b)(2)(ii) (stored where they can't be knocked over) instead.
- Anything from CGA P-1 (incorporated by 1910.101(b), not available on osha.gov).

Paragraph corrections: 1910.253(b)(4)(ii) is about outside generator houses, not storage inside buildings (the inside-building rule is (b)(2)(ii)). (b)(5)(ii)(B) is dropping/striking; lifting by crane is (A) and lifting by caps is (C).
