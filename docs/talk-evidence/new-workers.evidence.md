# new-workers evidence

All training and heat lines are OSHA guidance (Recommended Practices; Heat: Protecting New Workers), not regulations. "Should"/"recommends" wording is kept for that reason.

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| New on the crew, or back after time off? Your first days are when you need to slow down, ask questions, and stick with a buddy. | guidance: osha.gov/heat-exposure/protecting-new-workers; osha.gov/safety-management/education-training | Hook; paraphrase of "Use a buddy system and don't allow new workers to work alone", returning workers "after an absence of one week or more", and "Provide opportunities for workers to ask questions". |
| You have the right to safety training in a language you understand. | guidance: osha.gov/workers | exact: "Receive workplace safety and health training in a language you understand" |
| Training should cover the hazards of your job, what to do in an emergency, and how to report hazards, injuries and near misses. | guidance: education-training (action items 1 and 4) | exact: "Train workers so they understand and can recognize the hazards they may encounter in their own jobs"; "What to do in an emergency"; "How to report hazards, injuries, illnesses, and close calls/near misses" |
| If something isn't clear, ask. Know who to go to with questions or safety concerns. | guidance: education-training (action items 1 and 3) | exact: "Provide opportunities for workers to ask questions"; "Whom to contact with questions or concerns about the program" |
| Training isn't just for day one. You should get trained any time you're given a new task. | guidance: education-training (action item 4) | paraphrase: training "whenever a worker is assigned a new task" |
| Before you start, learn the hazards you could run into on this job and how to spot them. | guidance: education-training (action item 4) | paraphrase: "understand and can recognize the hazards they may encounter in their own jobs" |
| You have the right to the safety gear the job requires, like gloves or a harness and lifeline for falls. | guidance: osha.gov/workers | exact: "Receive required safety equipment, such as gloves or a harness and lifeline for falls" |
| In the heat, the first days are the most dangerous. Almost half of heat-related deaths happen on a worker's first day on the job, and over 70 percent in the first week. | guidance: protecting-new-workers | exact: "Almost half of heat-related deaths occur on a worker's first day on the job"; "Over 70 percent of heat-related deaths occur during a worker's first week" (OSHA cites Tustin 2018) |
| Your body needs time to adjust to the heat. | guidance: protecting-new-workers | paraphrase: "'acclimatization' means that the body gradually adapts and tolerates higher levels of heat stress." |
| OSHA recommends new workers work only 20 percent of a normal day on day one, with at least one rest break, and add 20 percent each day after until they reach a normal schedule. | guidance: protecting-new-workers | exact: "New workers should work only 20 percent of the normal duration on their first day."; "They should be given at least one rest break during the period when they are working."; "Increase work duration by 20 percent on subsequent days until the worker is performing a normal schedule." |
| Back after a week or more away from hot work? You need extra heat protections too, for at least a week. | guidance: protecting-new-workers | paraphrase: "Workers returning to work environments with potential exposure to heat hazards after an absence of one week or more"; "Maintain the additional heat protections for at least one week." |
| Use a buddy system. New workers shouldn't work alone. | guidance: protecting-new-workers | exact: "Use a buddy system and don't allow new workers to work alone." |
| Watch new workers closely for signs of heat illness. If someone has symptoms, let them stop working and start first aid. Never leave them alone. | guidance: protecting-new-workers | exact: "Monitor new workers closely for any symptoms of heat-related illness."; "allow them to stop working. Initiate first aid. Never leave someone alone who is experiencing symptoms!" |
| Ask: New folks, what's one thing about this job you're not sure about yet? Everyone else, who's your buddy today? | n/a | Discussion prompt. |

Dropped for lack of a source:
- "New workers get hurt more often" as a general (non-heat) claim: no fetched OSHA page gave a figure; the only new-worker statistic used is the heat one.
- "New workers are at the highest risk": replaced with the sourced first-day/first-week figures.
- A list of heat-illness symptoms: the protecting-new-workers page does not list them.
- The 1-2 week duration of new-worker precautions ("These increased precautions should last for 1-2 weeks") is sourced but left out for length.

Notes:
- The 20 percent schedule and buddy system are OSHA recommendations, not a federal standard; the talk says "OSHA recommends" / "should".
- The heat sentences only apply where there is heat exposure; they are scoped "In the heat" / "hot work".
