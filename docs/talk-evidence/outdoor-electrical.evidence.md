# outdoor-electrical — evidence

Sources fetched on osha.gov: 1910.303, 1910.304, 1910.305, 1910.333, 1910.334.
Correction to assignment: the general-industry GFCI rule is in 1910.304(b)(3), not 1910.303/305/333/334, so 1910.304 was added. 1910.333 is used only for deenergizing, qualified persons, and ladders.
Overlap: `electrical-gi` (mfg/wh) covers qualified persons, lockout, cord inspection, panel clearance, breaker reset. `electrical-gfci` is construction (1926.404). This talk is narrowed to outdoor/wet conditions: wet locations, wet hands/plugs, GFCI on temporary and rooftop outlets, weatherproof outlets for landscape lighting/irrigation controllers, holiday lighting. Cord inspection and breaker reset are repeated briefly because the land/facil industries do not get electrical-gi.

## Hook
- "Outside, you work around rain, sprinklers, puddles and wet grass. Your cords, outlets and lights have to be made for that." Framing; 1910.305(g)(1)(i) cords approved for conditions of use and location; 1910.305(j) wet/damp location rules. (framing)

## Cords and tools outside
- "Check every extension cord and corded tool before use on each shift. Look for loose parts, bent or missing pins, a damaged jacket, and crushed or pinched spots." 1910.334(a)(2)(i): cord- and plug-connected equipment and extension cords "shall be visually inspected before use on any shift for external defects (such as loose parts, deformed and missing pins, or damage to outer jacket or insulation) and for evidence of possible internal damage (such as pinched or crushed outer jacket)." Exception for cords that stay connected and aren't exposed to damage not stated (kept general). (paraphrase)
- "If it's damaged, it's out of service. Nobody uses it until it's repaired and tested." 1910.334(a)(2)(ii): "the defective or damaged item shall be removed from service, and no employee may use it until repairs and tests necessary to render the equipment safe have been made." (exact sense)
- "Cords have to be approved for where and how they're used." 1910.305(g)(1)(i): "Flexible cords and cables shall be approved for conditions of use and location." (exact sense)
- "If you're likely to be in contact with water, the cord and tool must be approved for that." 1910.334(a)(4): portable equipment and cords used in highly conductive locations "or in job locations where employees are likely to contact water or conductive liquids, shall be approved for those locations." (paraphrase)
- "Use labeled gear the way its label says." 1910.303(b)(2): "Listed or labeled equipment shall be installed and used in accordance with any instructions included in the listing or labeling." (paraphrase)
- "A cord for a grounded tool needs its ground wire." 1910.334(a)(3)(i): "A flexible cord used with grounding-type equipment shall contain an equipment grounding conductor." (exact sense)
- "Never cut off the ground pin, and never use an adapter that breaks the ground." 1910.334(a)(3)(ii): plugs and receptacles may not be connected or altered to prevent continuity of the grounding conductor; (a)(3)(iii): "Adapters which interrupt the continuity of the equipment grounding connection may not be used." (paraphrase)

## GFCI protection
- "For temporary power during repair, maintenance or remodeling work, every 125-volt, 15-, 20- or 30-amp outlet that isn't part of the building's permanent wiring needs GFCI protection." 1910.304(b)(3)(ii) lead-in (temporary wiring used during construction-like activities, including certain maintenance, remodeling, or repair) and (b)(3)(ii)(A): "All 125-volt, single-phase, 15-, 20-, and 30-ampere receptacle outlets that are not part of the permanent wiring of the building or structure and that are in use by personnel shall have ground-fault circuit-interrupter protection for personnel." (paraphrase; "single-phase" and "in use by personnel" omitted for speech)
- "The end of an extension cord used for temporary power counts as an outlet." Note 1 to (b)(3)(ii)(A): "A cord connector on an extension cord set is considered to be a receptacle outlet if the cord set is used for temporary electric power." (exact sense)
- "A GFCI cord set plugged into the outlet closest to the power source is an accepted way to do it." Note 2: "Cord sets and devices incorporating the required ground-fault circuit-interrupter that are connected to the receptacle closest to the source of power are acceptable forms of protection." (exact sense)
- "Permanent 125-volt, 15- and 20-amp outlets on rooftops must have GFCI protection too." 1910.304(b)(3)(i): "All 125-volt, single-phase, 15- and 20-ampere receptacles installed in bathrooms or on rooftops shall have ground-fault circuit-interrupter protection for personnel." (paraphrase; bathrooms omitted as off-topic)
- "If a breaker trips, don't keep resetting it. Generally it stays off until someone has checked that it's safe to turn back on." 1910.334(b)(2): circuit "may not be manually reenergized until it has been determined that the equipment and circuit can be safely energized. The repetitive manual reclosing of circuit breakers ... is prohibited." Note allows reclosing without examination if clearly an overload; hence "generally". (paraphrase)

## Wet hands, wet plugs
- "Your hands must be dry when you plug in or unplug anything that's live." 1910.334(a)(5)(i): "Employees' hands may not be wet when plugging and unplugging flexible cords and cord- and plug-connected equipment, if energized equipment is involved." (paraphrase)
- "If a plug or connector is wet, like one that's been sitting in water, it can only be handled live with insulating protective gear." 1910.334(a)(5)(ii): "Energized plug and receptacle connections may be handled only with insulating protective equipment if the condition of the connection could provide a conducting path to the employee's hand (if, for example, a cord connector is wet from being immersed in water)." (paraphrase)
- "Stop and get your supervisor." Practical prompt (no fact).
- "Don't staple cords or hang them in a way that damages the jacket. Never raise or lower a tool by its cord." 1910.334(a)(1): "Flexible electric cords connected to equipment may not be used for raising or lowering the equipment. Flexible cords may not be fastened with staples or otherwise hung in such a fashion as could damage the outer jacket or insulation." (exact sense)
- "Near exposed live parts, a ladder must have nonconductive side rails." 1910.333(c)(7): "Portable ladders shall have nonconductive siderails if they are used where the employee or the ladder could contact exposed energized parts." (paraphrase)

## Outdoor outlets, lights and controllers
- "An outlet in a wet spot that runs things nobody watches, like sprinkler controllers, landscape lighting or holiday lights, needs a cover that stays weatherproof with the plug in or out." 1910.305(j)(2)(vi): "A receptacle installed in a wet location where the product intended to be plugged into it is not attended while in use (for example, sprinkler system controllers, landscape lighting, and holiday lights) shall have an enclosure that is weatherproof with the attachment plug cap inserted or removed." (exact sense)
- "Light fixtures and lampholders in wet or damp spots have to be made for it, so water can't get in." 1910.305(j)(1)(iii): "Lampholders installed in wet or damp locations shall be of the weatherproof type." (j)(1)(iv): fixtures in wet or damp locations "identified for the purpose" and constructed or installed so "water cannot enter or accumulate in wireways, lampholders, or other electrical parts." (paraphrase)
- "Outdoor boxes, panels and switches in wet spots go in weatherproof enclosures." 1910.305(e)(1): enclosures (incl. panelboard enclosures) "shall be weatherproof in wet locations"; (e)(2): "Switches, circuit breakers, and switchboards installed in wet locations shall be enclosed in weatherproof enclosures." (paraphrase)
- "Temporary holiday lighting is allowed for up to 90 days, then it comes down." 1910.305(a)(2)(i)(B): temporary installations "For a period not to exceed 90 days for Christmas decorative lighting, carnivals, and similar purposes"; (a)(2)(ii): "Temporary wiring shall be removed immediately upon completion of the project or purpose." (paraphrase; applies to temporary wiring installations, 600 V or less)
- "Live parts get shut off before anyone works on or near them, with limited exceptions." 1910.333(a)(1): live parts "shall be deenergized before the employee works on or near them, unless the employer can demonstrate that deenergizing introduces additional or increased hazards or is infeasible". (paraphrase)
- "Only qualified people work on wiring that hasn't been shut off and locked or tagged out." 1910.333(c)(2): "Only qualified persons may work on electric circuit parts or equipment that have not been deenergized under the procedures of paragraph (b)" ((b) = lockout/tagging procedures). (paraphrase)

## Dropped / not used
- GFCI testing frequency: no 1910 standard paragraph fetched requires a test interval for GFCI devices; not claimed.
- Assured equipment grounding conductor program (1910.304(b)(3)(ii)(C)): applies only where GFCI is not available for receptacles other than 125 V 15/20/30 A; too narrow, left out.
- 1910.305(j)(2)(v) damp/sheltered outlets (weatherproof when covered): fetched, left out for length.
- 1910.303(b)(6) deteriorating agents: fetched; its wet-location wording is tied to deteriorating agents, so not used.
- 1910.333(c)(8) conductive jewelry: fetched, left out for length.
