Paragraph corrections to the assignment / my initial assumptions:
- 1910.94(c) has no rule that the booth exhaust keep running; that rule is 1910.107(d)(2).
- Respirator rule in 1910.94 is (c)(6)(iii)(a), not (c)(6)(ii) (which is solvent-vapor dilution to 25% of LEL).
- Hot surfaces are 1910.107(c)(3); (c)(6) is wiring. One-day supply is (e)(2).

Dropped for lack of a source:
- Specific face velocities (Table G-10: 100 fpm large booth / 150 fpm small) — accurate but table-dependent (gun type, crossdraft); left out to avoid overstating.
- Filter change rule (94(c)(7)(iii)(b)) — sits in the make-up-air paragraph; not clearly about overspray filters, so dropped.
- "Use supplied-air respirators for isocyanates" — INT says APRs "are now considered acceptable as long as appropriate precautions and change out schedules are in place" and SARs "may be the only appropriate type" in some circumstances. No blanket SAR rule, so the talk doesn't state one.
- Eye protection for isocyanates — OSHA isocyanates pages fetched don't state controls (the "Possible Solutions" page is only a reference list).
- "Asthma for life" — 3707 says symptoms "can continue ... even when the exposure stops"; kept out of the hook as too strong.
- NIOSH is used for sensitization and PPE because the OSHA isocyanates pages (main, hazards, solutions, standards) give no control or sensitization text. Reviewer may want to confirm NIOSH is acceptable here.
