# route-delivery evidence

OSHA has no ergonomics standard (OSHA interpretation of March 18, 2024: "While OSHA does not have a specific
ergonomics standard," hazards "may be addressed under Section 5(a)(1) of the Occupational Safety and Health (OSH)
Act," "commonly referred to as the General Duty Clause."). There is also no OSHA standard for hand trucks or for
backing at delivery stops (the Preventing Backovers solutions page says its measures are "not required by any OSHA
standard."). Every line rests on OSHA guidance. Narrowed from existing "lift" (basic lifting) and
"loading-ergonomics" (case weights, NIOSH lift limits, pallet heights): this talk is the hand truck itself, curbs/
stairs/ramps, and what happens at the customer stop.

The OSHA eTool is written for beverage delivery; the talk names it as "OSHA's beverage delivery guide" and applies its
hand-truck and stop advice to route delivery generally.
