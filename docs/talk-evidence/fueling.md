# fueling evidence

Pages fetched 2026-10-07:
- P1 = OSHA 1910.106 page 1, https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.106 (covers (a) through (g)(1)(i)(e)(2))
- P2 = OSHA 1910.106 page 2, https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.106_2 (covers (g)(1)(i)(e)(3) onward, including all of service stations (g)). Sources list uses the section URL per the brief; the (g) text was read on the _2 page.
- C = OSHA 1926.152, https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.152
- N = NIOSH Pub. 98-111 "Fire Hazard from Filling Portable Gas Cans in Pickup Trucks and Cars", https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/11557/cdc_11557_DS1.pdf

Scope note: 1910.106(g) applies to service stations (fuel dispensed from fixed equipment, (a)(3) definition); 1926.152(g) to construction service and refueling areas. Agriculture (part 1928) does not adopt 1910.106 as far as fetched pages show; the talk frames rules as "federal rules for fueling areas" / "at a service station" / "on construction sites" rather than claiming they bind every farm. REVIEWER NOTE: the assignment named OSHA guidance; I found no OSHA guidance page on static when filling portable cans, so the static/can-placement lines come from a NIOSH publication (allowed by the brief only "where named"). Drop section 2 items 1-2 if NIOSH is not acceptable here.

| Fuel vapor can be lit by an open flame, a cigarette, or a spark, even a static spark. | P1 1910.106(b)(6) | exact: "Sources of ignition may include open flames, lightning, smoking, cutting and welding, hot surfaces, frictional heat," "sparks (static, electrical, and mechanical)" |

| Shut the engine off. Federal rules for fueling areas say the motors of all equipment being fueled are shut off during fueling. | P2 (g)(8); C (g)(10) | exact: "The motors of all equipment being fueled shall be shut off during the fueling operation." (identical in both) |
| No smoking and no open flames where fuel is dispensed or fuel systems are worked on. | P2 (g)(8); C (g)(8) | exact: "There shall be no smoking or open flames in the areas used for fueling, servicing fuel systems for internal combustion engines, receiving or dispensing of flammable liquids." |
| Signs banning smoking have to be posted. | P2 (g)(8); C (g)(9) | exact: "Conspicuous and legible signs prohibiting smoking shall be posted within sight of the customer being served." |
| If the nozzle is the manual kind, hold it open by hand the whole time. | P2 (g)(3)(vi)(b) | exact: "Manual-closing type valves shall be held open manually during dispensing." |
| Know where the emergency shutoff is. A fueling area has a switch away from the pumps that cuts power to all of them. | P2 (g)(3)(iii); C (g)(6) | exact: switch or breaker "at a location remote from dispensing devices" to "shut off the power to all dispensing devices in the event of an emergency." |
| Take the can out of the truck bed or car and set it on the ground before you fill it. | guidance: N | exact: "Before filling, always remove the containers from the vehicle and place them on the ground." |
| Don't fill it in the vehicle. | guidance: N | exact: "Avoid the hazardous practice of leaving the portable gasoline containers in pickup trucks or cars when refueling!" |
| Touch the can with the nozzle before you take the lid off. | guidance: N | exact: "Touch the container with the gas dispenser nozzle before removing the container lid" |
| Keep the nozzle touching the can opening the whole time you fill. | guidance: N | exact: "Keep the nozzle in contact with the container inlet when filling" |
| That gives static a path to ground. | guidance: N | paraphrase: contact provides a path to dissipate static charge to ground |
| At a fueling station, gas goes only into a metal can with a tight screw or spring cover and a spout, or one built to pour without spilling. | P2 (g)(1)(v) | exact: "No delivery of any Category 1 or 2 flammable liquids, ... shall be made into portable containers unless the container is constructed of metal, has a tight closure with screwed or spring cover, and is fitted with a spout or so designed so the contents can be poured without spilling." | At a service station, spilled fuel can't be allowed to run into the building. | P2 (g)(7) | exact: provision shall be made "to prevent spilled liquids from flowing into the interior of service station buildings." |

Dropped / notes:
- "Stop Motor" signs: not in 1910.106(g) or 1926.152(g) text fetched. Not used.
- Self-service / attendant rules: not found in 1910.106(g)(3) as fetched. Not used.
- Latch-open devices: 1910.106 allows automatic-closing nozzles with an approved latch-open device at fixed dispensers ((g)(3)(vi)(b)), but 1926.152(g)(4) and 1910.106(g)(1)(i)(e)(5) (tank-vehicle dispensing) require no latch-open device. Rule varies by setting, so no latch line beyond "hold manual nozzles open by hand".
- Bonding: 1926.152(e)(2) and 1910.106(e)(6)(ii) bonding rules cover container-to-container transfer / industrial plants, not vehicle fueling at a pump. Not used; static line is from NIOSH guidance.
- 1926.152(a)(1) safety cans / DOT containers for 5 gallons or less: construction only; not used to keep the can rule to one setting.
- Re-entering the vehicle during fueling: no OSHA/NIOSH source fetched. Not used.
- Paragraph correction: in 1910.106(g), sources of ignition is (g)(8) and fire control is (g)(9) (not (g)(9) for ignition).
