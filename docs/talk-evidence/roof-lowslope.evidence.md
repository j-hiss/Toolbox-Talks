# Evidence: roof-lowslope

Standards fetched 2026-10-07 from osha.gov: 1926.500, 1926.501, 1926.502. Guidance: OSHA 3755.

## Hook
- "A flat roof feels safe. That's the trap. The edge sneaks up on you while your eyes are on your work."
  - Framing only, no factual claim.

## Know your roof
- "A low-slope roof is 4 in 12 or flatter. Anything steeper is a steep roof, and it has different rules."
  - 1926.500(b) Low-slope roof: "a roof having a slope less than or equal to 4 in 12 (vertical to horizontal)." Steep roof: "a roof having a slope greater than 4 in 12". Different rules: 1926.501(b)(10) vs (b)(11).
- "If the edge is 6 feet or more above a lower level, you need fall protection. That can be guardrails, a safety net, or a personal fall arrest system."
  - 1926.501(b)(10): "each employee engaged in roofing activities on low-slope roofs, with unprotected sides and edges 6 feet (1.8 m) or more above lower levels shall be protected from falling by guardrail systems, safety net systems, personal fall arrest systems, or ..."
- "It can also be a warning line used together with guardrails, a net, a personal fall arrest system, or a safety monitor."
  - 1926.501(b)(10): "a combination of warning line system and guardrail system, warning line system and safety net system, or warning line system and personal fall arrest system, or warning line system and safety monitoring system."
- "On a roof 50 feet wide or less, a safety monitor alone is allowed, with no warning line."
  - 1926.501(b)(10): "Or, on roofs 50-feet (15.25 m) or less in width (see Appendix A to subpart M of this part), the use of a safety monitoring system alone [i.e. without the warning line system] is permitted."

## Warning lines
- "The warning line goes around all sides of the work area, at least 6 feet back from the edge."
  - 1926.502(f)(1): "The warning line shall be erected around all sides of the roof work area." (f)(1)(i): "When mechanical equipment is not being used, the warning line shall be erected not less than 6 feet (1.8 m) from the roof edge."
- "When mechanical equipment is running, the line stays at least 6 feet from the edges alongside the machine's path, and at least 10 feet from the edge the machine is moving toward."
  - 1926.502(f)(1)(ii): "not less than 6 feet (1.8 m) from the roof edge which is parallel to the direction of mechanical equipment operation, and not less than 10 feet (3.1 m) from the roof edge which is perpendicular to the direction of mechanical equipment operation."
  - Reviewer note: "alongside the machine's path" = parallel edge; "edge the machine is moving toward" = perpendicular edge.
- "The line hangs 34 to 39 inches high, sag included, with high-visibility flags no more than 6 feet apart."
  - 1926.502(f)(2)(ii): "its lowest point (including sag) is no less than 34 inches (.9 m) from the walking/working surface and its highest point is no more than 39 inches (1.0 m)". (f)(2)(i): "flagged at not more than 6-foot (1.8 m) intervals with high-visibility material".
- "The space between the line and the edge is for roofing work only. If you're not doing roofing work out there, stay inside the line."
  - 1926.502(f)(3): "No employee shall be allowed in the area between a roof edge and a warning line unless the employee is performing roofing work in that area."

## Paths and equipment
- "The path from the ladder, hoist, or material storage to the work area is marked with two warning lines."
  - 1926.502(f)(1)(iii): "Points of access, materials handling areas, storage areas, and hoisting areas shall be connected to the work area by an access path formed by two warning lines."
- "When a path isn't being used, close it off at the warning line with a rope, wire, chain, or barricade as strong and as high as the line. Or offset the path so nobody can walk straight in."
  - 1926.502(f)(1)(iv): "When the path to a point of access is not in use, a rope, wire, chain, or other barricade, equivalent in strength and height to the warning line, shall be placed across the path at the point where the path intersects the warning line ... or the path shall be offset such that a person cannot walk directly into the work area."
- "Mechanical equipment can only be used or stored where people are protected by a warning line, guardrail, or personal fall arrest system."
  - 1926.502(f)(4): "Mechanical equipment on roofs shall be used or stored only in areas where employees are protected by a warning line system, guardrail system, or personal fall arrest system."

## The safety monitor
- "The monitor is a competent person the company picks. Their job is to spot fall hazards and warn you when you don't see one or you're working unsafe."
  - 1926.502(h)(1): "The employer shall designate a competent person to monitor the safety of other employees". (h)(1)(i): "competent to recognize fall hazards". (h)(1)(ii): "shall warn the employee when it appears that the employee is unaware of a fall hazard or is acting in an unsafe manner".
- "The monitor stays on the same roof as you, where they can see you and close enough to talk to you."
  - 1926.502(h)(1)(iii): "on the same walking/working surface and within visual sighting distance of the employee being monitored". (h)(1)(iv): "close enough to communicate orally with the employee".
- "The monitor does nothing else. No other task that pulls their attention off the crew."
  - 1926.502(h)(1)(v): "The safety monitor shall not have other responsibilities which could take the monitor's attention from the monitoring function."
  - Reviewer note: "does nothing else" is the plain-language version; the rule bars other responsibilities that could take attention from monitoring.
- "No mechanical equipment is used or stored in an area where a safety monitor is the protection."
  - 1926.502(h)(2): "Mechanical equipment shall not be used or stored in areas where safety monitoring systems are being used to monitor employees engaged in roofing operations on low-slope roofs."
- "When the monitor warns you, stop and fix it right away."
  - Practical advice. Consistent with 1926.502(h)(1)(ii) (monitor's duty to warn) and (h)(4): "Each employee working in a controlled access zone shall be directed to comply promptly with fall hazard warnings from safety monitors." (h)(4) is literally scoped to controlled access zones; for low-slope roofing the sentence is guidance, not a quoted rule.
  - guidance: OSHA 3755 p. 17: "The safety monitor is always a competent person".
