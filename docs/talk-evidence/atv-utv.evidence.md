# Evidence: atv-utv

There is no OSHA standard specific to ATVs or UTVs in agriculture; this talk is guidance only. Fetched 2026-10-07:
- OSHA Fact Sheet DSG FS-3758 (07/2015), "All-Terrain Vehicle Hazards during Farm Work": https://www.osha.gov/Publications/OSHA3758.pdf (the /sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3758.pdf path returned 403; the /Publications/ path loaded)
- OSHA Agricultural Operations, Hazards and Controls, ATV section: https://www.osha.gov/agricultural-operations/hazards
- Wisconsin FACE 00WI03901, on the NIOSH FACE site: https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/face/stateface/wi/00WI039.html
- Washington FACE report (corporate author WA Dept. of Labor and Industries; in NIOSH's CDC Stacks collection, description only): https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/228719
- OSHA SHIB 08-03-06 "Hazards Associated with ATVs in the Workplace" (shib080306.pdf): both osha.gov paths returned 403 / index page only. Not used.

## Hook
- "An ATV feels like an easy ride across the field. But losing control, rolling over, and getting thrown off are common causes of ATV incidents."
  - guidance: FS-3758 list of common causes of incidents (exact items): "Loss of control of the vehicle", "ATV rollovers", "Operators being thrown from the vehicle". First sentence is framing.

## Before you ride
- "Your company should never let anyone drive an ATV until they've been trained on its owner's manual."
  - guidance: FS-3758, "Employers should:" (exact): "Provide training on the ATV owner's manual to each operator." / "Never allow untrained workers to drive an ATV."
- "Do a safety check before and after every ride to make sure it's working right."
  - guidance: FS-3758 (exact): employers should train operators to "perform pre- and post-ride safety checks to ensure that the ATV is operating properly."
- "Don't ride when you're tired or impaired."
  - guidance: FS-3758 (exact): "Never allow workers to operate an ATV when tired or impaired."
- "Put it in neutral or park, with the parking brake on, before you start it."
  - guidance: FS-3758 (exact): "Place the gear in neutral or park, with the parking brake locked before starting the vehicle."

## Gear and riders
- "Wear a helmet. A bicycle helmet doesn't protect your head and face enough. Goggles and gloves too."
  - guidance: FS-3758 (exact): "Wear helmets/head protection; bicycle helmets do not provide sufficient face and head protection." OSHA Hazards page (exact): "Wear appropriate riding gear: DOT-, Snell ANSI-approved helmet, goggles, gloves".
- "Most ATVs are built for one rider. Never carry a passenger unless the ATV is made for more than one."
  - guidance: FS-3758 (exact): "Never allow multiple riders" unless the ATV is "specifically designed for more than one rider." OSHA Hazards page (exact): "ATVs are not made for multiple riders. Never carry anyone else on the ATV."

## Hills and rough ground
- "Keep your speed right for the ground, how far you can see, and how much experience you have."
  - guidance: FS-3758 (exact): "Maintain a speed that is proper for the terrain, visibility conditions, and the operator's experience level."
- "Slow down for hills and turns. Watch for holes, stumps, ruts, culverts, wires, fences, and big rocks."
  - guidance: FS-3758 (exact): "Be aware when approaching hills, turns, and other obstacles." / "Stay alert near marked and unmarked terrain hazards, such as holes, stumps, ruts, culverts, wires, fences, and large rocks." Also "Never operate an ATV at excessive speeds." "Slow down" is paraphrase of these.
- "Check the ground before you start. In one case, a farmer's ATV hit a hay bale hidden in tall grass on a steep hill and rolled over on him. He wasn't found until the next morning."
  - guidance: 00WI039 Recommendation #2 (exact): "Farmers should conduct a thorough evaluation of the terrain to identify hazards in the pathway prior to beginning an operation with an off-road vehicle." Summary (exact): "The vehicle apparently struck a small hay bale that was partially hidden in the grass on the uphill side and rolled over sideways down the hill." Narrative (exact): "At a place on the hill where there is about a 30° grade". Pinned underneath; family did not find him until the next morning (paraphrase of report summary).
- "No wheelies, jumps, or stunts. Stay off streets, highways, and paved roads."
  - guidance: FS-3758 (exact): "Operate ATVs in accord with the owner's manual and not attempt wheelies, jumps or hazardous stunts." OSHA Hazards page (exact): "Do not operate the ATV on streets, highways or paved roads."

## Loads and UTVs
- "Never load past the manufacturer's weight limit. Strap tools tight to the cargo rack."
  - guidance: FS-3758 (exact): "The total weight of the ATV should not go above the manufacturer's limits." / "Secure tools firmly to the ATV's cargo rack."
- "Attachments change how the ATV handles, stays stable, and brakes. Learn the trailer loading and pulling steps before you tow."
  - guidance: OSHA Hazards page (exact): "Any added attachments affect the stability, operating and braking of the ATV." FS-3758 (exact): "Understand the ATV's trailer loading and pulling procedures."
- "UTVs, the side-by-sides, have their own manual. Follow it. In one case, the manual said to wear a helmet and seat belt and stay off paved roads. The driver wore neither, drove onto a highway, was hit by a vehicle, and died of head injuries."
  - guidance: WA FACE (CDC Stacks 228719) description (exact): "Neither the victim nor the passenger were wearing seatbelts or employer provided helmets." Paraphrase of the rest: the manufacturer's manual said operator and passengers should wear a helmet and use a seatbelt and not operate on public or paved roads; the victim drove the UTV onto a 55 MPH highway; the UTV was struck by a vehicle (report title); he died of head injuries three days later. The employer had told employees to follow the manufacturer's safety instructions.
  - Reviewer note: "the side-by-sides" is a common-name gloss, not from the source.

## Ask
- "Who here has been trained on the ATV or UTV we use, and where is its weight limit posted or written?" — crew question tied to FS-3758 training and weight-limit lines.

## Dropped / not sourced
- UTV-specific rules beyond "follow the manual": no OSHA page fetched mentions UTVs. The Wisconsin FACE recommendation to choose "a non-ATV utility vehicle equipped with ROPS and designed to maintain a low center of gravity" was left out to keep the talk short; it is an equipment-purchase recommendation for employers.
- Age limits (OSHA Hazards page cites National Safety Council: 70-90cc age 12+, over 90cc age 16+): left out; aimed at youth, not adult crews.
- FS-3758 injury/fatality figures (2,090 injuries, 321 fatalities, 2003-2011): left out because the original data source was not confirmed in the fetch.
- Slope-specific angle limits: no source gives one.
