# fall-rescue: evidence

Quotes fetched 2026-10-07 via WebFetch from osha.gov.
SHIB = "Suspension Trauma/Orthostatic Intolerance", OSHA SHIB 03-24-2004 (updated 2011), https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/shib032404.pdf

## Hook
- "A harness stops the fall. It doesn't end the emergency. A worker hanging in a harness needs to come down fast."
  - 1926.502(d)(20): "The employer shall provide for prompt rescue of employees in the event of a fall"; guidance: SHIB, "Rescue suspended workers as quickly as possible."

## What your training covers
- "If you might be exposed to a fall hazard, you get trained first, by a competent person."
  - 1926.503(a)(1): "The employer shall provide a training program for each employee who might be exposed to fall hazards."; (a)(2): "trained, as necessary, by a competent person qualified in the following areas"
- "The company keeps a written record of that training."
  - 1926.503(b)(1): written certification record (name, date(s) of training, signature of trainer/employer) (WebFetch summary)
- "You learn the fall hazards in your work area, and how to set up, maintain, take down, and inspect the fall protection you'll use."
  - 1926.503(a)(2)(i): "The nature of fall hazards in the work area;" (a)(2)(ii): "The correct procedures for erecting, maintaining, disassembling, and inspecting the fall protection systems to be used;"
- "You learn how to use the systems on the job, like guardrails, harnesses, safety nets, and warning lines."
  - 1926.503(a)(2)(iii): "The use and operation of guardrail systems, personal fall arrest systems, safety net systems, warning line systems, safety monitoring systems, controlled access zones, and other protection to be used;"
- "You also learn your role in the job's fall protection plan."
  - 1926.503(a)(2)(vii): "The role of employees in fall protection plans;"

## When you get retrained
- Intro (heading) — 1926.503(c): "When the employer has reason to believe that any affected employee who has already been trained does not have the understanding and skill required by paragraph (a) of this section, the employer shall retrain each such employee."
- "When the job changes and your old training doesn't fit anymore."
  - 1926.503(c)(1): changes in the workplace render previous training obsolete (WebFetch summary)
- "When the fall protection system or equipment changes."
  - 1926.503(c)(2): changes in the types of fall protection systems or equipment render previous training obsolete (WebFetch summary)
- "When your work shows you've forgotten how to use your fall protection the right way."
  - 1926.503(c)(3): inadequacies in an affected employee's knowledge or use of fall protection indicate the employee has not retained the requisite understanding or skill (WebFetch summary)

## After a fall: rescue fast
- "The company has to plan for prompt rescue after a fall, or make sure workers can rescue themselves."
  - 1926.502(d)(20): "The employer shall provide for prompt rescue of employees in the event of a fall or shall assure that employees are able to rescue themselves."
- "Know that plan before you go up."
  - guidance: SHIB, "employers should implement plans to prevent prolonged suspension in fall protection devices."; training on "prompt rescue of employees in the event of a fall"
- "Hanging still in a harness can make you pass out. OSHA warns it can lead to unconsciousness and death in less than 30 minutes."
  - guidance: SHIB, "can result in unconsciousness, followed by death, in less than 30 minutes"
- "Get a hanging worker down as quickly as possible."
  - guidance: SHIB, "Rescue suspended workers as quickly as possible."

## If you're the one hanging
- "Pump your legs often to keep your blood moving."
  - guidance: SHIB, "“pump” his/her legs frequently to activate the muscles and reduce the risk of venous pooling"
- "If you can reach a foothold, use it to take the pressure off."
  - guidance: SHIB, "Footholds can be used to alleviate pressure, delay symptoms, and provide support for “muscle pumping.”"
- "Know the warning signs: feeling faint, dizzy, sweaty, sick to your stomach, short of breath, going pale, or your vision going grey."
  - guidance: SHIB signs list: Faintness, Breathlessness, Sweating, Paleness, ... Nausea, Dizziness, ... "“Greying” or Loss of Vision"; training: "How to recognize the signs and symptoms identified in this bulletin"
- "After any rescue, the worker gets checked by a health-care professional."
  - guidance: SHIB, "Monitoring the worker after rescue, and ensuring that the worker is evaluated by a health-care professional."

## Ask
- "If someone fell right now and was hanging in a harness, who would get them down, and how?" — question, no factual claim (ties to 1926.502(d)(20)).

## Dropped / not used
- Trauma straps / suspension relief straps: SHIB does not mention them (only footholds). Left out.
- Body belts: not mentioned.
