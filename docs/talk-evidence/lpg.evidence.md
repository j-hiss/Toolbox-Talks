## Hook
- "Propane cylinders show up everywhere: heaters, torches, forklifts, grills." Framing; 1910.110 covers containers for heaters ((c)(5)(i)(h)), hand torches ((c)(5)(ii)), and engine fuel/industrial trucks ((b)(6)(i) exceptions, (e)). (paraphrase/framing)
- "Each one is a container of fuel gas under pressure, so treat it that way." Framing; 1910.110 rates valves for 250 p.s.i.g. working pressure ((b)(7)(i)). (framing)
- "This is the general industry rule. Construction sites have their own rule." Framing; 1910.110 is Part 1910; construction is 1926.153 (not used). (framing)

## Where cylinders go
- "The rule starts simple: LP-gas containers go outside buildings. There are limited exceptions, like portable use inside when it's really needed, or storage that follows the storage rules." (b)(6)(i): "Containers, and first stage regulating equipment if used, shall be located outside of buildings" with exceptions incl. portable use under (c)(5) and containers awaiting use or resale stored under (f). (c)(5)(i): indoor use allowed when operational needs make portable use necessary and outdoor location is impracticable. (exact + paraphrase)
- "Set every cylinder on a firm, level spot, or secure it so it can't tip." (c)(4)(ii): "Containers shall be set upon firm foundation or otherwise firmly secured." (paraphrase)
- "Inside, except for small hand-torch cylinders, it stands upright and gets secured when needed." (c)(5)(i)(k): containers over 2½ pounds water capacity must stand on a firm, level surface and be secured upright when necessary; (c)(5)(ii): containers up to 2½ lb water capacity in hand torch assemblies. (paraphrase)
- "Cylinders hooked up for use are never stacked one on top of another." (b)(6)(iii): "Containers installed for use shall not be stacked one above the other." (exact)
- "Clear weeds and long dry grass for at least 10 feet around any container." (b)(6)(vi): "Readily ignitible material such as weeds and long dry grass shall be removed within 10 feet of any container." (exact)

## Storing cylinders
- "Store them where they won't get too hot, get hit, or get messed with by someone who shouldn't touch them." (f)(2)(i): located to minimize exposure to excessive temperature rise, physical damage, or tampering by unauthorized persons. (paraphrase)
- "Outside, keep them in an enclosure or protect them some other way." (f)(6)(ii): "Containers shall be in a suitable enclosure or otherwise protected against tampering." (exact)
- "Inside, never store cylinders near exits or stairways, or anywhere people need to get out safely." (f)(2)(ii): containers stored inside not near exits or stairways, or in areas used for the safe exit of people. (paraphrase)
- "Valves on stored cylinders stay closed." (f)(2)(iv): "The outlet valves of containers in storage shall be closed." (exact)
- "The valve has to be protected by a cap or collar, or by being built into the cylinder. If yours uses a cap, put it back on." (f)(2)(iii): valves protected by recessing into the container or by a ventilated cap or collar (withstands 30 lb dropped 4 ft). "Put it back on" follows from the cap being the protection method. (paraphrase) Reviewer note: (f) covers containers up to 1,000 lb water capacity awaiting use or resale ((f)(1)).
- "In a building the public doesn't visit, the general limit is 300 pounds of LP-gas, unless it's a special room built for it." (f)(4)(i): "The quantity of LP-Gas stored shall not exceed 300 pounds" in buildings not frequented by the public, subject to (f)(5) special buildings or rooms (up to 10,000 lb). (exact + paraphrase)
- "Empties stored inside count as full." (f)(2)(v): empty containers that were in LP-Gas service, if stored inside, count as full for the quantity limit. (paraphrase)

## Connecting and leaks
- "Only people trained for it install, remove, run or maintain LP-gas equipment." (b)(16): "Personnel performing installation, removal, operation, and maintenance work shall be properly trained in such function." (paraphrase)
- "If you haven't been trained on it, ask first." Practical prompt to ask (no fact).
- "Never test for a leak with a flame." (b)(8)(ix): "Test shall not be made with a flame." (exact; context is leak testing of piping systems)
- "If you smell gas, stop and tell your supervisor." Practical prompt to stop/tell (no fact).
- "Fill fuel cylinders only in the open air, or in a building made for that." (b)(14)(viii): "Fuel supply containers shall be gaged and charged only in the open air" or in buildings especially provided for that purpose. (paraphrase)
- "Someone stays close to the connection the whole time gas is being transferred." (b)(14)(i): "at least one attendant shall remain close to the transfer connection" from connection until disconnection. (exact sense)
- "Inside, hose from a cylinder is as short as practical and never more than 6 feet. It never runs through walls, floors or ceilings." (b)(9)(vii)(b): "For use inside buildings the hose shall be of minimum practical length, but shall not exceed 6 feet"; hose may not extend between rooms or pass through walls, partitions, ceilings, or floors. (exact + paraphrase)

## Heaters and cylinders
- "Portable heaters, including salamanders, need an automatic device that shuts off the gas if the flame goes out." (c)(5)(i)(h): "Portable heaters, including salamanders, shall be equipped with an approved automatic device" that shuts off gas if the flame is extinguished. (paraphrase)
- "When cylinders feed portable heaters inside, like temporary or emergency heat, keep each heater at least 6 feet from any cylinder, unless the heater and cylinder are built as one unit." (c)(5)(v)(b): heaters other than integral heater-container units at least 6 feet from any container. Applies in buildings under construction/renovation ((c)(5)(v)) and, by reference, to industrial space heating ((c)(5)(vii)) and temporary emergency heating ((c)(5)(viii)). (paraphrase)
- "Never aim a blower or radiant heater at a cylinder within 20 feet." (c)(5)(v)(b): blower and radiant type heaters shall not be directed toward any container within 20 feet. (paraphrase; same scope as above)
- "Temporary emergency heat is never left unattended." (c)(5)(viii)(b): "The temporary heating equipment shall not be left unattended." (exact)
