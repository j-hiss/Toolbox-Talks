# Evidence: solar-electrical

Fetched 2026-10-07 from osha.gov: 1926.416, 1926.417, 1910.333; guidance pages /green-jobs/solar, /green-jobs/solar/electrical, /green-jobs/solar/lockout-tagout.
Not found: no NIOSH solar page was located (a web search turned up only non-government pages), so none is cited. The OSHA solar PPE subpage (/green-jobs/solar/ppe) returned 404.

Scope note for reviewer: OSHA's solar electrical page says installations are "Typically ... covered by OSHA Subpart S standards in part 1910" and that "If solar panels supply power to a distribution system, then the provisions contained in 29 CFR 1910.269" apply. Installation work is construction (1926 Subpart K: 1926.416/.417); maintenance and electrical work practices on finished systems are general industry (1910.333). The talk mixes the two at a level where they agree. 1910.269 lockout (1910.269(d)) isn't quoted line by line; the talk stays general.

## Hook
- "Solar panels use sunlight to make electricity."
  - guidance: /green-jobs/solar: "PV systems are the most common and use semi-conductors and sunlight to make electricity." (exact)
- "You can't switch off the sun, so the panels and the circuits they feed can both hurt you."
  - guidance: /green-jobs/solar/lockout-tagout: "Solar energy equipment can generate electrical energy and may be connected to electrical circuits." (exact). The page goes on to say workers face hazards from the panels and from the circuits (paraphrase). "Can't switch off the sun" is plain-language framing of the same point.

## Why solar is different
- "The more panels in a system, the more electricity it makes."
  - guidance: /green-jobs/solar: "The more solar modules a PV system or array has, the more electricity will be generated." (exact)
- "Workers can get shocked and burned when they hook panels up to a circuit."
  - guidance: /green-jobs/solar/electrical: "Workers may be exposed to electric shocks and burns when hooking up the solar panels to an electric circuit." (exact)
- "Arc flash can burn you too."
  - guidance: /green-jobs/solar: "potentially exposed to a variety of serious hazards, such as arc flashes (which include arc flash burn and blast hazards), electric shock, falls, and thermal burn hazards" (exact)
- "When you install or service panels, your company should have you cover them. That's on top of protecting you from the circuits."
  - guidance: /green-jobs/solar/lockout-tagout: "employers should assure that workers cover the solar panels" (exact excerpt), stated in addition to protecting workers from the circuits (paraphrase). Kept as "should" (guidance wording), assigned to the employer.

## Shut it off and lock it out
- "Your company can't put you close enough to touch a circuit unless it's shut off and grounded, or guarded with insulation or another barrier."
  - 1926.416(a)(1): "No employer shall permit an employee to work in such proximity to any part of an electric power circuit that the employee could contact the electric power circuit in the course of work, unless the employee is protected against electric shock by deenergizing the circuit and grounding it or by guarding it effectively by insulation or other means." (paraphrase)
  - Related general-industry rule 1910.333(a)(1): "Live parts to which an employee may be exposed shall be deenergized before the employee works on or near them" (exceptions where deenergizing adds hazards or is infeasible).
- "Each disconnect used to shut it off gets locked and tagged."
  - 1910.333(b)(2)(iii)(A): "A lock and a tag shall be placed on each disconnecting means used to deenergize circuits and equipment on which work is to be performed, except as provided in paragraphs (b)(2)(iii)(C) and (b)(2)(iii)(E)" (paraphrase). Reviewer note: (C) and (E) allow tag-only or lock-only in defined cases; the talk does not list exceptions.
- "Tags go at every point where it could be turned back on."
  - 1926.417(b): "Equipment or circuits that are deenergized shall be rendered inoperative and shall have tags attached at all points where such equipment or circuits can be energized." (paraphrase)
- "Only the person who put a lock or tag on takes it off, unless your company has a set procedure for it."
  - guidance: /green-jobs/solar/lockout-tagout: "Only the authorized employee who placed the lock and tag must remove his/her lock or tag" (exact excerpt); the page says "unless a specific procedure allows otherwise" (paraphrase of the page's summary). Matches 1910.147(e)(3) structure (fetched for the chipper talk).
- "Stored electrical energy that could hurt someone gets released before work starts."
  - 1910.333(b)(2)(ii)(C): "Stored electric energy which might endanger personnel shall be released." (paraphrase)

## Test before you touch
- "Shut off doesn't mean safe. A qualified person tests the parts you'll be exposed to and makes sure they're dead."
  - 1910.333(b)(2)(iv)(B): "A qualified person shall use test equipment to test the circuit elements and electrical parts of equipment to which employees will be exposed and shall verify that the circuit elements and equipment parts are deenergized." (paraphrase)
- "That test also checks for voltage feeding back from another source, even when part of the circuit was shut off."
  - 1910.333(b)(2)(iv)(B): "The test shall also determine if any energized condition exists as a result of inadvertently induced voltage or unrelated voltage backfeed even though specific parts of the circuit have been deenergized and presumed to be safe." (paraphrase)
- "Only qualified people work on parts that haven't been shut off. They know the special methods, protective gear, and insulated tools it takes."
  - 1910.333(c)(2): "Only qualified persons may work on electric circuit parts or equipment that have not been deenergized under the procedures of paragraph (b) of this section. Such persons shall be capable of working safely on energized circuits and shall be familiar with the proper use of special precautionary techniques, personal protective equipment, insulating and shielding materials, and insulated tools." (paraphrase)

## Look up
- "If you're not qualified for line work, stay at least 10 feet from overhead lines up to 50,000 volts. Higher voltage needs more room."
  - 1910.333(c)(3)(i)(A): unqualified person in an elevated position near overhead lines: "For voltages to ground 50kV or below - 10 ft. (305 cm); For voltages to ground over 50kV - 10 ft. (305 cm) plus 4 in. (10 cm) for every 10kV over 50kV." (paraphrase). (c)(3)(i)(B) applies the same distances to conductive objects for unqualified persons on the ground.
  - guidance: /green-jobs/solar/electrical: "Workers must pay attention to overhead power lines and stay at least 10 feet away from them" (exact), citing 1910.333(c)(3).
  - Reviewer note: "50,000 volts" = 50 kV to ground; "not qualified for line work" simplifies "unqualified person".
- "That 10 feet counts for you and the longest conductive thing you're holding, like metal conduit or a metal ladder."
  - 1910.333(c)(3)(i)(A): "the person and the longest conductive object he or she may contact cannot come closer to any unguarded, energized overhead line than the following distances" (paraphrase). Examples (conduit, metal ladder) are illustrative.
