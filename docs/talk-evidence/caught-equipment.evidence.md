# caught-equipment evidence

Guide = OSHA Construction Focus Four: Caught-In or -Between Hazards instructor guide, https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/caught_iorb_ig.pdf (the brief's path pattern for this guide, /sites/default/files/2011_r_03.pdf, 404'd; found via search).

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| Caught-in or -between is one of the Focus Four hazards in construction. | guidance: OSHA Focus Four Hazards page (title "Focus Four Hazards: Falls, Caught-In or -Between, Struck-By, and Electrocution") | paraphrase |
| It means getting squeezed, caught, crushed, pinched or compressed between two or more objects. | Guide | exact: "Injuries resulting from a person being squeezed, caught, crushed, pinched, or compressed between two or more objects" |
| Equipment held up by slings, hoists or jacks has to be blocked or cribbed before anyone works under it or between it. | OSHA 1926.600(a)(3)(i) | exact: "suspended or held aloft by use of slings, hoists, or jacks shall be substantially blocked or cribbed to prevent falling or shifting before employees are permitted to work under or between them." |
| Dozer and scraper blades, loader buckets and dump bodies get fully lowered or blocked when they're being repaired or not in use. | OSHA 1926.600(a)(3)(i) | exact: "Bulldozer and scraper blades, end-loader buckets, dump bodies, and similar equipment, shall be either fully lowered or blocked when being repaired or when not in use." |
| During repairs or when it's sitting idle, controls go in neutral, the motor is stopped and the brakes are set, unless the job needs otherwise. | OSHA 1926.600(a)(3)(i) | exact: "All controls shall be in a neutral position, with the motors stopped and brakes set, unless work being performed requires otherwise." |
| Parked equipment gets the parking brake set. On a slope, chock the wheels too. | OSHA 1926.600(a)(3)(ii) | exact: "Whenever the equipment is parked, the parking brake shall be set. Equipment parked on inclines shall have the wheels chocked and the parking brake set." |
| A crane's turning upper works can hit you, or crush you against the machine or something else. | OSHA 1926.1424(a)(1)(i)-(ii) | paraphrase: rotating superstructure risk of "Striking and injuring an employee" / "Pinching/crushing an employee against another part of the equipment or another object." |
| Your company has to train you to spot those zones. It also has to mark them with barriers, warning lines or railings. | OSHA 1926.1424(a)(2)(i)-(ii) | exact: employer must "Train each employee assigned to work on or near the equipment ... how to recognize struck-by and pinch/crush hazard areas" and "Erect and maintain control lines, warning lines, railings or similar barriers to mark the boundaries of the hazard areas." (Exception allows signs + markings where barriers aren't feasible; not stated in script.) |
| Respect the line. Stay out of the swing radius of cranes and other equipment. | Guide | exact: "Stay out of the swing radius or cranes and other equipment." |
| If you have to go into that zone where the operator can't see you, make sure the operator knows first. | OSHA 1926.1424(a)(3)(i) | exact: "Before an employee goes to a location in the hazard area that is out of view of the operator, the employee (or someone instructed by the employee) must ensure that the operator is informed" |
| The operator can't swing until they get the agreed signal that you're in a safe spot. | OSHA 1926.1424(a)(3)(ii) | exact: "the operator must not rotate the superstructure until the operator is informed in accordance with a pre-arranged system of communication that the employee is in a safe position." |
| Equipment backing up with a blocked rear view needs a backup alarm you can hear, or a spotter who signals it's safe. | OSHA 1926.602(a)(9)(ii) | paraphrase: earthmoving or compacting equipment "which has an obstructed view to the rear" may not reverse unless it has "a reverse signal alarm distinguishable from the surrounding noise level" or an employee signals that it is safe. |
| Never put yourself between moving materials and something that won't move: a wall, a truck, or a stack of material. | Guide | exact: "Never place yourself between moving materials and an immovable structure, vehicle, or stacked materials" |
| Belts, gears, shafts, pulleys and other moving parts you could touch have to be guarded. | OSHA 1926.300(b)(2) | exact: "Belts, gears, shafts, pulleys ... or other reciprocating, rotating or moving parts of equipment shall be guarded if such parts are exposed to contact by employees" |
| Never take a guard off while the tool or machine is in use. | Guide | exact: "Never remove a safety guard when a tool is being used." |
| No loose clothing or jewelry around moving parts. | Guide | exact: "Be sure to avoid wearing loose clothing or jewelry that can be caught in moving parts." |
| Before you work on a machine, make sure it's shut off and can't start by accident. | Guide | exact: "Make sure that your equipment is de-energized and cannot be started accidentally." |
| Lock out the power source if you can. | Guide | exact: "If possible, lock out the power source to the equipment." |
| Ask: Where are the swing zones and pinch points ... | n/a | discussion prompt, no factual claim |

Notes:
- 1926.602 has no raised-blade/blocking paragraph; that rule is 1926.600(a)(3)(i). 1926.602 was used only for (a)(9)(ii) reverse alarms.
- 1926.1424 has no (a)(4); section (a) ends at (a)(3).
- Not used: 1926.602(a)(10) scissor-point guarding on loaders, seat belts/ROPS (rollover is better in a separate talk), trench-specific guidance.

## Fact-check 2026-10-07
- Swing-zone marking now includes the 1926.1424(a)(2)(ii) exception (signs and markings). Backup-alarm line scoped to earthmoving/compacting equipment ((a)(9)(ii)).
