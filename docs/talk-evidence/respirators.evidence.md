# respirators evidence

All 1910.134 paragraphs apply to construction through OSHA 1926.103: "The requirements applicable to construction work under this section are identical to those set forth at 29 CFR 1910.134".

| Poor fit, poor use or poor care can take that protection away. | OSHA 1910.134(k)(1)(i) | paraphrase: "how improper fit, usage, or maintenance can compromise the protective effect of the respirator" |

A respirator comes in when those aren't enough. | OSHA 1910.134(a)(1) | paraphrase: "primary objective shall be to prevent atmospheric contamination ... accomplished as far as feasible by accepted engineering control measures (for example, enclosure or confinement of the operation, general and local ventilation, and substitution...)"; respirators used when effective engineering controls are not feasible or while being instituted. |

| A tight-fitting respirator needs a fit test before you first use it, any time you switch to a different size, style, model or make, and at least once a year after that. | OSHA 1910.134(f)(1)-(2) | exact: "employees using a tight-fitting facepiece respirator pass an appropriate qualitative fit test (QLFT) or quantitative fit test (QNFT)"; "prior to initial use of the respirator, whenever a different respirator facepiece (size, style, model or make) is used, and at least annually thereafter." |
| You get fit tested again if something changes that could affect the fit, like facial scarring, dental work, cosmetic surgery, or an obvious change in weight. | OSHA 1910.134(f)(3) | exact: changes in physical condition "that could affect respirator fit. Such conditions include, but are not limited to, facial scarring, dental changes, cosmetic surgery, or an obvious change in body weight." |
| Inspect it before each use. | OSHA 1910.134(h)(3)(i)(A) | exact: "All respirators used in routine situations shall be inspected before each use and during cleaning." |
| Do a seal check every time. | OSHA 1910.134(g)(1)(iii) | exact: "perform a user seal check each time they put on the respirator" |
| Negative check: cover the cartridges with your palms, breathe in gently so the mask pulls in a little, and hold your breath for ten seconds. It should stay pulled in, with no air leaking in. | App B-1 I.B | exact: "Close off the inlet opening of the canister or cartridge(s) by covering with the palm of the hand(s) ... inhale gently so that the facepiece collapses slightly, and hold the breath for ten seconds." / "If the facepiece remains in its slightly collapsed condition and no inward leakage of air is detected, the tightness of the respirator is considered satisfactory." |
| Positive check: cover the exhale valve and breathe out gently. You should feel a little pressure build up, with no air leaking out at the seal. | App B-1 I.A | exact: "Close off the exhalation valve and exhale gently into the facepiece. The face fit is considered satisfactory if a slight positive pressure can be built up inside the facepiece without any evidence of outward leakage of air at the seal." |
| Beards and seals don't mix. If facial hair gets between the seal and your face, or gets in the way of a valve, you can't wear a tight-fitting respirator. | OSHA 1910.134(g)(1)(i)(A) | exact: employer "shall not permit respirators with tight-fitting facepieces to be worn by employees who have: Facial hair that comes between the sealing surface of the facepiece and the face or that interferes with valve function" |
| Wear glasses or goggles so they don't break the seal. | OSHA 1910.134(g)(1)(ii) | exact: worn "in a manner that does not interfere with the seal of the facepiece to the face of the user." |
| Gas and vapor cartridges get changed by an end-of-service-life indicator or by the change schedule in your company's program. Know your schedule. | OSHA 1910.134(d)(3)(iii)(B)(1)-(2) | paraphrase: for gases and vapors, respirator equipped with an ESLI "certified by NIOSH for the contaminant", or "the employer implements a change schedule for canisters and cartridges"; "The employer shall describe in the respirator program the information and data relied upon" |
| Leave the area if you notice vapor or gas getting through, if it gets harder to breathe, or if the mask leaks. | OSHA 1910.134(g)(2)(ii)(B) | exact: "detect vapor or gas breakthrough, changes in breathing resistance, or leakage of the facepiece" |
| Also step out to wash your face or to change a filter or cartridge. | OSHA 1910.134(g)(2)(ii)(A), (C) | exact: "To wash their faces"; "To replace the respirator or the filter, cartridge, or canister elements." |
| Store it where it's protected from damage, dust, sunlight, extreme heat or cold, moisture and chemicals. | OSHA 1910.134(h)(2)(i) | exact: "stored to protect them from damage, contamination, dust, sunlight, extreme temperatures, excessive moisture, and damaging chemicals" |

Notes:
- Fit testing (f) and seal checks (g)(1)(iii) are written for program (required) use. For voluntary use of filtering facepieces (dust masks) only, (c)(2)(ii) exempts the employer from the written program, so the script frames dust-mask/voluntary users only with Appendix D items and does not say dust masks must be fit tested.
- App B-1 also says that for most respirators the positive check requires removing the exhalation valve cover first; left out for length ("follow the maker's instructions" covers it). Some cartridge inlets can't be covered by palm (use thin glove); left out.
- (e)(1) wording is "before the employee is fit tested or required to use the respirator" (the task brief's "(e) medical" is correct; "(f) fit testing annually" is at (f)(2)).
