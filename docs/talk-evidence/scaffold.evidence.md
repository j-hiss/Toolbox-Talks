# scaffold: evidence

Quotes fetched 2026-10-07 via WebFetch from
https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.451 and .../1926.454

## Hook
- "A scaffold is only as safe as the way it was built and the last time it was checked. Know both before you climb."
  - Framing; ties to 1926.451(f)(7) (erection under competent person) and (f)(3) (inspection before each shift). No factual claim beyond those.

## Setup and footing
- "Before you work on a scaffold, you get trained on its hazards, including electrical, fall, and falling object hazards, and on how much load it can carry."
  - 1926.454(a): "The employer shall have each employee who performs work while on a scaffold trained by a person qualified in the subject matter to recognize the hazards..."; (a)(1): "The nature of any electrical hazards, fall hazards and falling object hazards in the work area;" (a)(4): "The maximum intended load and the load-carrying capacities of the scaffolds used"
- "Only trained, experienced workers put up, move, change, or take down a scaffold, and a competent person directs that work."
  - 1926.451(f)(7): "Scaffolds shall be erected, moved, dismantled, or altered only under the supervision and direction of a competent person ... Such activities shall be performed only by experienced and trained employees selected for such work by the competent person."
- "Legs and frames sit on base plates and mud sills, or another firm foundation."
  - 1926.451(c)(2): "Supported scaffold poles, legs, posts, frames, and uprights shall bear on base plates and mud sills or other adequate firm foundation."
- "The footing has to be level and solid enough to hold the loaded scaffold without settling or shifting."
  - 1926.451(c)(2)(i): "Footings shall be level, sound, rigid, and capable of supporting the loaded scaffold without settling or displacement."
- "Never set a scaffold or a plank on anything unstable."
  - 1926.451(c)(2)(ii): "Unstable objects shall not be used to support scaffolds or platform units."

## Planks and platforms
- "Every working level is fully planked or decked."
  - 1926.451(b)(1): "Each platform on all working levels of scaffolds shall be fully planked or decked between the front uprights and the guardrail supports"
- "Gaps between planks, and between planks and uprights, stay at 1 inch or less."
  - 1926.451(b)(1)(i): "the space between adjacent units and the space between the platform and the uprights is no more than 1 inch (2.5 cm) wide"
- "In the rare case a wider gap is needed, like fitting around uprights, the open space still can't go over 9 and a half inches."
  - 1926.451(b)(1)(i): "except where the employer can demonstrate that a wider space is necessary (for example, to fit around uprights when side brackets are used...)"; (b)(1)(ii): "the remaining open space between the platform and the uprights shall not exceed 9½ inches (24.1 cm)."
- "Platforms and walkways are at least 18 inches wide in most cases."
  - 1926.451(b)(2): "Except as provided in paragraphs (b)(2)(i) and (b)(2)(ii) ... each scaffold platform and walkway shall be at least 18 inches (46 cm) wide."
- "Each plank end runs at least 6 inches past the center of its support, unless it's cleated or hooked."
  - 1926.451(b)(4): "Each end of a platform, unless cleated or otherwise restrained by hooks or equivalent means, shall extend over the centerline of its support at least 6 inches (15 cm)."
- "On a plank 10 feet or shorter, the end can't stick out more than 12 inches past the support, unless it's built to hold weight there or guarded off."
  - 1926.451(b)(5)(i): "Each end of a platform 10 feet or less in length shall not extend over its support more than 12 inches (30 cm) unless the platform is designed and installed so that the cantilevered portion ... is able to support employees and/or materials without tipping, or has guardrails which block employee access to the cantilevered end."
- "Where planks overlap, they overlap only over a support, by at least 12 inches, unless they're nailed or held together."
  - 1926.451(b)(7): "the overlap shall occur only over supports, and shall not be less than 12 inches (30 cm) unless the platforms are nailed together or otherwise restrained to prevent movement."

## Getting up and staying on
- "If the platform is more than 2 feet above or below where you get on, use a ladder, stair tower, ramp, or other built access."
  - 1926.451(e)(1): "When scaffold platforms are more than 2 feet (0.6 m) above or below a point of access, portable ladders, hook-on ladders, attachable ladders, stair towers ..., ramps, walkways, integral prefabricated scaffold access, or direct access ... shall be used."
- "Never climb the cross braces."
  - 1926.451(e)(1): "Crossbraces shall not be used as a means of access."
- "On most scaffolds, more than 10 feet up you need guardrails or a personal fall arrest system."
  - 1926.451(g)(1): "Each employee on a scaffold more than 10 feet (3.1 m) above a lower level shall be protected from falling to that lower level."; (g)(1)(vii): "For all scaffolds not otherwise specified ... each employee shall be protected by the use of personal fall arrest systems or guardrail systems" ("most" because (g)(1)(i)-(vi) set specific rules for some scaffold types)
- "On supported scaffolds put in service after the year 2000, the top rail sits 38 to 45 inches above the platform, with a midrail about halfway."
  - 1926.451(g)(4)(ii): "on supported scaffolds manufactured or placed in service after January 1, 2000 shall be installed between 38 inches (0.97 m) and 45 inches (1.2 m) above the platform surface."; (g)(4)(iv): "When midrails are used, they shall be installed at a height approximately midway between the top edge of the guardrail system and the platform surface."
- "Don't stand on boxes, barrels, or other makeshift steps to get higher."
  - 1926.451(f)(14): "Makeshift devices, such as but not limited to boxes and barrels, shall not be used on top of scaffold platforms to increase the working level height of employees."
- "Ladders on a platform are only allowed in special cases on large area scaffolds."
  - 1926.451(f)(15): "Ladders shall not be used on scaffolds to increase the working level height of employees, except on large area scaffolds where employers have satisfied the following criteria:"

## Inspection, loads, and weather
- "A competent person inspects the scaffold for visible defects before each shift, and again after anything that could weaken it."
  - 1926.451(f)(3): "Scaffolds and scaffold components shall be inspected for visible defects by a competent person before each work shift, and after any occurrence which could affect a scaffold's structural integrity."
- "Damaged parts get fixed, replaced, or braced right away, or the scaffold comes out of service."
  - 1926.451(f)(4): "shall be immediately repaired or replaced, braced to meet those provisions, or removed from service until repaired."
- "Never load it past its rated capacity"
  - 1926.451(f)(1): "Scaffolds and scaffold components shall not be loaded in excess of their maximum intended loads or rated capacities, whichever is less."
- "and don't let debris pile up on the platforms."
  - 1926.451(f)(13): "Debris shall not be allowed to accumulate on platforms."
- "No work on a scaffold covered with snow, ice, or anything slippery, except to clear it off."
  - 1926.451(f)(8): "Employees shall be prohibited from working on scaffolds covered with snow, ice, or other slippery material except as necessary for removal of such materials."
- "No work in storms or high winds, unless a competent person says it's safe and you're protected by a personal fall arrest system or wind screens."
  - 1926.451(f)(12): "Work on or from scaffolds is prohibited during storms or high winds unless a competent person has determined that it is safe for employees to be on the scaffold and those employees are protected by a personal fall arrest system or wind screens."
