# first-aid-blood evidence

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| When someone gets hurt, the first few minutes count. Know who can help, where the kit is, and how to handle blood without putting yourself at risk. | OSHA 1926.50(b); 1910.151(b); guidance: osha.gov/medical-first-aid | Hook framing. Paraphrase of "prompt medical attention in case of serious injury" (1926.50(b)) and employer duty to "provide medical and first aid personnel and supplies commensurate with the hazards of the workplace" (guidance). |
| If there's no clinic or hospital close by, someone on site has to be trained to give first aid. | OSHA 1910.151(b); OSHA 1926.50(c) | exact: "In the absence of an infirmary, clinic, or hospital in near proximity to the workplace ... a person or persons shall be adequately trained to render first aid." 1926.50(c): "...shall be available at the worksite to render first aid." |
| On construction jobs, that person needs a valid first-aid certificate or equivalent training they can prove. | OSHA 1926.50(c) | exact: "a person who has a valid certificate in first-aid training from the U.S. Bureau of Mines, the American Red Cross, or equivalent training that can be verified by documentary evidence" |
| First aid supplies have to be easy to get to. | OSHA 1926.50(d)(1); 1910.151(b) | exact: "First aid supplies shall be easily accessible when required."; "Adequate first aid supplies shall be readily available." |
| On construction jobs, your company checks the kit before each job and at least weekly, so used items get replaced. | OSHA 1926.50(d)(2) | exact: "shall be checked by the employer before being sent out on each job and at least weekly on each job to ensure that the expended items are replaced." |
| On construction sites, if a 911 call won't send our location automatically, the site's location has to be posted where you can see it. | OSHA 1926.50(f)(2)(ii) | paraphrase: "When using a communication system in an area that does not automatically supply the caller's latitude and longitude information to the 911 emergency dispatcher, the employer must post in a conspicuous location at the worksite either" lat/long or other location-identification information. Exception (f)(2)(ii)(B) for landlines with automatic location; line kept conditional. |
| That's called universal precautions. Treat all human blood as if it carries disease, no matter whose it is. | OSHA 1910.1030(b) "Universal Precautions"; (d)(1) | paraphrase: "all human blood and certain human body fluids are treated as if known to be infectious" for HIV, HBV and other bloodborne pathogens. "no matter whose" restates "all human blood". |
| Wear gloves any time you could touch blood, or touch things or surfaces with blood on them. | OSHA 1910.1030(d)(3)(ix) | paraphrase: "Gloves shall be worn when it can be reasonably anticipated that the employee may have hand contact with blood, other potentially infectious materials, mucous membranes, and non-intact skin ... and when handling or touching contaminated items or surfaces." |
| Wash your hands right after you take off your gloves. | OSHA 1910.1030(d)(2)(v) | exact: "employees wash their hands immediately or as soon as feasible after removal of gloves" |
| If blood gets on your skin, wash with soap and water. If it gets in your eyes, nose or mouth, flush with water. | OSHA 1910.1030(d)(2)(vi) | exact: "wash hands and any other skin with soap and water, or flush mucous membranes with water immediately or as soon as feasible following contact" |
| A surface with blood on it gets cleaned with a disinfectant right away, or as soon as you can. | OSHA 1910.1030(d)(4)(ii)(A) | exact: "decontaminated with an appropriate disinfectant ... immediately or as soon as feasible when surfaces are overtly contaminated or after any spill of blood" |
| Don't pick up broken glass that may have blood on it with your hands. Use a brush and dustpan, or tongs. | OSHA 1910.1030(d)(4)(ii)(D) | paraphrase: "Broken glassware which may be contaminated shall not be picked up directly with the hands. It shall be cleaned up using mechanical means, such as a brush and dust pan, tongs, or forceps." (Rule says "glassware".) |
| Flood the area with water and clean any wound with soap and water. | guidance: osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens | exact: "immediately flood the exposed area with water and clean any wound with soap and water" |
| Then report it to your supervisor right away and get medical attention. | guidance: osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens | paraphrase: "Report this immediately to your employer and seek immediate medical attention." |
| Under OSHA's bloodborne pathogens rule, after you report an exposure, your company has to make a confidential medical evaluation available to you right away. | OSHA 1910.1030(f)(3) | exact: "Following a report of an exposure incident, the employer shall make immediately available to the exposed employee a confidential medical evaluation and follow-up" |
| That rule covers jobs that can expose you to blood. | OSHA 1910.1030(a) | paraphrase: "This section applies to all occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials"; occupational exposure = "reasonably anticipated skin, eye, mucous membrane, or parenteral contact with blood ..." |
| There, your company has to give you protective gear at no cost, train you, and offer the hepatitis B vaccine free. | OSHA 1910.1030(d)(3)(i); (g)(2); (f)(1)(i), (f)(1)(ii)(A) | exact: "the employer shall provide, at no cost to the employee, appropriate personal protective equipment"; "The employer shall train each employee with occupational exposure"; "make available the hepatitis B vaccine and vaccination series to all employees who have occupational exposure"; "Made available at no cost to the employee" |
| It doesn't cover most construction work, but designated first-aiders on construction jobs still have to be trained on bloodborne hazards. | guidance: OSHA interpretation 1994-05-13 (also 1992-11-06) | exact: "The bloodborne pathogens standard does not apply to the construction industry." (exception: maintenance work with occupational exposure, hence "most"); "the employer is required to train designated first aid providers in the hazards of bloodborne pathogens" under 29 CFR 1926.21(b)(2). |
