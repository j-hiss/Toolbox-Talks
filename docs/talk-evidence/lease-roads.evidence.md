# lease-roads: evidence

No OSHA standard covers employee highway driving. This talk uses OSHA and NIOSH guidance only (all sources kind "guidance").
Overlap note: the "vehicles" talk (all industries) already covers phones, general drowsy driving, backing and seat belts. This talk is narrowed to oil and gas: remote lease roads, journey management, fatigue after long hitches, weather/road, night, wildlife, loose cargo in the cab.

Pages fetched: osha.gov/oil-and-gas-extraction/hazards; osha.gov/etools/oil-and-gas/transportation; osha.gov/etools/oil-and-gas/transportation/transporting-personnel; NIOSH 2018-126 PDF (tool returned a summary, not verbatim, so NIOSH lines are marked paraphrase); NIOSH 2018-126 landing page (cdc.gov/niosh/publications/numbered/2018-126.html, short exact quotes); OSHA Fatal Facts 3614 (osha.gov/Publications/OSHA-FF-3614.pdf).

## Hook
- "Highway crashes are the leading cause of death for oil and gas extraction workers." OSHA hazards page: "Highway vehicle crashes are the leading cause of oil and gas extraction worker fatalities." (paraphrase); NIOSH 2018-126 page: "Crashes are the leading cause of death for oil and gas extraction workers." (exact)
- "OSHA says roughly 4 of every 10 workers killed in this industry die in a highway vehicle incident." OSHA hazards page and eTool Transportation: "Roughly 4 of every 10 workers killed on the job in this industry are killed as a result of a highway vehicle incident." (exact; OSHA cites BLS CFOI)

## Plan the trip
- "Wells are often far out, down secondary and lease roads." eTool Transportation: "Wells are often located in remote areas, and require traveling long distances, often over secondary and lease roads" (paraphrase)
- "Plan the drive before you leave, the same way you'd plan a job." Transporting Personnel, Trip Planning: "Conduct pre-trip planning with risk assessment and transport-related job safety analysis (JSA)." (paraphrase)
- "Journey management means cutting the total miles driven and the risks on the road." Transporting Personnel: "Implement journey management." "This is a strategy to reduce the total number of miles driven and the risks of road travel." (paraphrase)
- "Fewer trips, fewer vehicles, and trips timed to miss heavy traffic." Transporting Personnel, Other Drivers: "Use journey management to minimize trips and vehicles on the roads." "Time trips to avoid high traffic." (paraphrase)
- "Check the weather forecast and traffic before you go." Weather and Road Conditions: "Obtain weather forecasts for the planned travel." "Use a GPS or smart phone for traffic updates and directions before departing" (paraphrase)
- "Know where you can stop to rest or stay along the route." Illness and Fatigue: "Identify available hotels or other rest options before driving."; Weather: "Identify available rest areas or lodging along the route..." (paraphrase)

## Fatigue after a long hitch
- "Long shifts plus long drives add up to fatigue." NIOSH 2018-126 page: "The combination of long trips with long shifts can result in fatigue." (paraphrase)
- "Drowsy driving is a major cause of crashes in this industry." NIOSH 2018-126 page: "Fatigued (drowsy) driving is a major cause of crashes in this industry." (exact)
- "Know the warning signs: frequent yawning, heavy eyelids, drifting out of your lane, or not remembering the last few miles." NIOSH 2018-126 PDF warning signs: frequent yawning, heavy eyelids, drifting from your lane, forgetting the last few miles driven (paraphrase)
- "Get 7 to 9 hours of sleep." NIOSH 2018-126 PDF: get 7-9 hours of sleep in a dark, quiet space (paraphrase)
- "Coffee, loud music, or an open window don't fix fatigue." NIOSH 2018-126 PDF myths: caffeine, rolling down the window or turning up the radio don't cure fatigue (paraphrase)
- "There is no substitute for sleep." NIOSH 2018-126 PDF: "There is no substitute for sleep." (exact)
- "Too tired to drive safely? Use your stop work authority." NIOSH PDF: use stop-work authority if driving feels unsafe (paraphrase); Transporting Personnel: "Stress the use of STOP WORK authority when a worker is too sick or fatigued to drive." (paraphrase)
- "Pull over somewhere safe and nap 15 to 30 minutes." NIOSH PDF: if too tired to continue, pull over, have a coffee, and nap for 15-30 minutes (paraphrase)
- "If a coworker looks too tired to drive, speak up." NIOSH PDF: speak up about a coworker who seems fatigued (paraphrase)

## Road and weather
- "Slow down for rain, snow, ice, fog and rough roads." Transporting Personnel: "Inclement weather (snow, ice, heavy rain, fog) and poor road conditions" increase risk; FF 3614: "reduce driving speed during inclement weather and when road conditions have deteriorated." (paraphrase)
- "Drive a safe speed for the conditions, and never over the posted limit." Speeding hazard: "Driving too fast for conditions or driving in excess of posted speed limits can greatly increase driving risk." FF 3614: "comply with all highway safety regulations" (paraphrase)
- "In one OSHA case, a rig crew drove home at night on a wet road after a 12-hour shift." FF 3614 incident: rig crew driving home after a 12-hour shift, wet two-lane road at night (paraphrase)
- "The truck took a curve too fast, skidded and rolled." FF 3614: curve at about 47 mph above the 40 mph posted limit for dry conditions; skidded, left the road, rolled over five times (paraphrase)
- "Only one of the four wore a seat belt." FF 3614: "Only one of the four wore a seat belt." (paraphrase of incident text)
- "The driver and a passenger were thrown out and killed." FF 3614: driver and one rear passenger were ejected and killed (paraphrase)
- "At night, slow down so you don't out-drive your headlights." Night Driving: "Train employees about reducing speed to avoid over-driving the reach of headlights" (paraphrase)
- "Stay extra alert where wildlife is likely." Wildlife: "Stay extra-alert when driving in areas where wildlife are expected." (paraphrase)

## Buckle up and pack it right
- "Not wearing a seat belt sharply raises your chance of being hurt or killed in a crash." Failure to Wear Seatbelt: "Failure to wear seat belts significantly increases the possibility of injury or death in an accident." (paraphrase)
- "Everyone buckles up, every seat, every trip." FF 3614: "Require employees to wear seat belts when they are traveling in company vehicles." (paraphrase; framed as crew practice)
- "Loose gear in the cab becomes a high-speed missile in a crash." Loose Objects: "Loose objects in the vehicle become high-speed projectiles in the event of an accident." (paraphrase)
- "Strap heavy items down in the bed or trunk, and keep the cab clear." Loose Objects: "Secure heavy objects in the trunk of a car or bed of a pickup with cargo straps or tie-downs." "Avoid loose objects in the cab..." (paraphrase)
- "Inspect the vehicle before you drive it, and report anything wrong so it gets fixed." Improper Vehicle Maintenance: "Require drivers to inspect vehicles before use"; "Consider the use of a discrepancy reporting program for vehicle maintenance issues." (paraphrase)
