# welding evidence

Standard pages: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.252 ; https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.253
Guidance: OSHA FactSheet DSG FS-3647 (03/2013) "Controlling Hazardous Fume and Gases during Welding".

| English sentence | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| Welding sparks can slip through cracks and openings and land on something that burns. | 1910.252(a)(2)(i) | paraphrase: openings or cracks in floors, walls or windows that can't be closed need precautions so sparks don't reach "readily combustible materials" below or beyond. |
| And the arc can hurt the eyes of people who aren't even welding. | 1910.252(b)(2)(iii) | paraphrase: "Workers or other persons adjacent to the welding areas shall be protected from the rays". |
| Hot work needs approval first. The person who authorizes it inspects the area and sets the precautions, often on a written hot work permit. | 1910.252(a)(2)(iv) | paraphrase: "Before cutting or welding is permitted, the area shall be inspected by the individual responsible for authorizing cutting and welding operations"; precautions designated "preferably in the form of a written permit." ("often" reflects "preferably"; not stated as mandatory.) |
| Where you can, move anything that burns at least 35 feet away. | 1910.252(a)(2)(vii) | exact: "Where practicable, all combustibles shall be relocated at least 35 feet (10.7 m) from the work site." |
| If it can't move, cover or shield it. | 1910.252(a)(2)(vii); (a)(1)(ii) | paraphrase: where relocation is impracticable, protect with flameproof covers or shields; "guards shall be used to confine the heat, sparks, and slag". |
| If the area can't be made safe, there's no welding. | 1910.252(a)(1)(iii); (a)(2)(xv) | paraphrase: if (i) and (ii) can't be followed, "welding and cutting shall not be performed"; "Cutting or welding shall be permitted only in areas that are or have been made fire safe." |
| If there's combustible debris on the floor, sweep it clean for 35 feet around. | 1910.252(a)(2)(v) | exact: where combustible materials such as paper clippings, wood shavings or textile fibers are on the floor, "the floor shall be swept clean for a radius of 35 feet (10.7 m)." |
| Never weld or cut on a used drum, barrel or tank until it's been cleaned so nothing flammable or toxic is left inside. | 1910.252(a)(3)(i) | paraphrase: "No welding, cutting, or other hot work shall be performed on used drums, barrels, tanks or other containers" until cleaned so "there are no flammable materials present or any substances ... which when subjected to heat, might produce flammable or toxic vapors." |
| A fire watcher is assigned when a fire could be more than minor, like when combustible material is closer than 35 feet. | 1910.252(a)(2)(iii)(A) | paraphrase: "Fire watchers shall be required whenever welding or cutting is performed in locations where other than a minor fire might develop"; (A)(1) "Appreciable combustible material ... closer than 35 feet". |
| The fire watch has an extinguisher ready, knows how to use it, and knows how to sound the alarm. | 1910.252(a)(2)(iii)(B) | paraphrase: fire watchers have extinguishing equipment readily available, are trained in its use, and are familiar with facilities for sounding an alarm. |
| The watch stays at least a half hour after the welding or cutting is done, to catch smoldering fires. | 1910.252(a)(2)(iii)(B) | exact: "A fire watch shall be maintained for at least a half hour after completion of welding or cutting operations to detect and extinguish possible smoldering fires." |
| Keep gas cylinders far enough away that sparks, slag or flame can't reach them, or put up a fire-resistant shield. | 1910.253(b)(5)(ii)(I) | exact: "Cylinders shall be kept far enough away from the actual welding or cutting operation so that sparks, hot slag, or flame will not reach them, or fire-resistant shields shall be provided." |
| Never strike an arc on a cylinder. | 1910.253(b)(5)(ii)(J) | paraphrase: striking an arc on a cylinder is prohibited. |
| Ventilation has to keep fumes below safe limits. | 1910.252(c)(1)(iii) | paraphrase: "Local exhaust or general ventilating systems shall be provided" to keep toxic fumes, gases or dusts below the limits in 1910.1000. |
| For general welding, mechanical ventilation is required if there's less than 10,000 cubic feet of space per welder or the ceiling is under 16 feet. | 1910.252(c)(2)(i)(A)-(B) | exact: "In a space of less than 10,000 cubic feet (284 m3) per welder." "In a room having a ceiling height of less than 16 feet (5 m)." ("general welding" = (c)(2) scope; metals in (c)(5)-(c)(12) have their own rules.) |
| Position yourself so you're not breathing the fumes. Outdoors, stay upwind. | guidance: OSHA FS-3647 | exact: "Workers should position themselves to avoid breathing welding fume and gases." "workers should stay upwind when welding in open or outdoor environments." |
| Arc welding or cutting: helmet or hand shield with the right filter shade. | 1910.252(b)(2)(i)(A); (b)(2)(ii)(H) | exact: "Helmets or hand shields shall be used during all arc welding or arc cutting operations, excluding submerged arc welding." Shade table in (b)(2)(ii)(H). |
| Gas welding or oxygen cutting: goggles or other suitable eye protection. | 1910.252(b)(2)(i)(B) | exact: "Goggles or other suitable eye protection shall be used during all gas welding or oxygen cutting operations." |
| Helpers need proper eye protection too. | 1910.252(b)(2)(i)(A) | paraphrase: helpers or attendants shall be provided with proper eye protection. |
| People working near you have to be protected from the arc rays with flameproof screens or proper goggles. | 1910.252(b)(2)(iii) | exact (part): "Workers or other persons adjacent to the welding areas shall be protected from the rays by noncombustible or flameproof screens or shields or shall be required to wear appropriate goggles." |
| Put the screens up before you start. | 1910.252(b)(2)(iii) | Instruction applying the rule above (protection must be in place while the arc is struck). |

Dropped / not used:
- "Arc flash"/"arc eye" injury descriptions — no fetched OSHA page describes the eye injury; kept to "can hurt the eyes" backed by (b)(2)(iii).
- Fume health effects (FS-3647: lung damage, cancer) — sourced but cut for length.

Paragraph corrections: 1910.252(b)(1) is railings and cable placement, not "installation and operation". (b)(2)(i) runs (A)-(D) only; there is no (E)-(H) in (b)(2)(i). The written-permit language is in (a)(2)(iv) and says "preferably", so the talk does not say a written permit is always required. The OSHA welding topic page (https://www.osha.gov/welding-cutting-brazing) had no usable hazard statements.
