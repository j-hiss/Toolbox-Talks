# Evidence: livestock

There is no OSHA standard specific to livestock handling. OSHA's Agricultural Operations pages (https://www.osha.gov/agricultural-operations and /hazards) were fetched 2026-10-07 and contain no flight-zone, escape-route, or handling guidance (the only handling-related lines are under animal-acquired infections: "Tracking aggressive animals, so that restraints may be used when necessary"). The OSHA youth-agriculture eTool returned 403/404. All content below comes from FACE (Fatality Assessment and Control Evaluation) reports in NIOSH's collections:

- 06MI205, NIOSH FACE (Michigan FACE), https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/166183
- 14MI014, MIFACE report hosted at https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/face/pdfs/14mi014.pdf (the PDF itself names MIFACE/Michigan State University and MIOSHA, not NIOSH; it is served from the NIOSH FACE site)
- Two Farmers / Brothers Killed By Young Angus Bull, https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/166789
- 96MN007, Minnesota FACE, https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/face/stateface/mn/96mn007.html

## Hook
- "In one NIOSH case, a farmer was moving a heifer down an alley to a trailer. She kicked him in the head, and he died the next day."
  - guidance: 06MI205 (exact segments): "a 76-year-old male farmer was injured when he was struck by a heifer as it was walking in an alley" / "toward the loading chute to a livestock trailer." / "The decedent was kicked in the head by the heifer." / "The decedent was transported to a local hospital where he died the next day."
  - Reviewer note: the event was unwitnessed and the report says it is unknown how he was positioned; "moving a heifer" matches the report title ("Leading Heifer Toward a Loading Chute").

## Read the animal
- "Every animal has a flight zone. That's its personal space. Step into it, and the animal turns and moves away from you."
  - guidance: 14MI014 (exact): the animal's "flight zone" (i.e., personal space); "Invading the flight zone causes animals to turn and move away from the handler (or predator)."
- "Learn how each animal acts. Know its flight zone before you try to move or load it."
  - guidance: 06MI205 Recommendation 2 (exact): "Livestock farmers should review each individual cow's behavioral characteristics and determine the flight zone around each cow so that the handler can provide a safe loading experience for the cow as well as for the handler."
- "Cattle see a wide area, including to the side and behind them. They notice movement back there, so don't assume they can't see you."
  - guidance: 14MI014 (exact): "Cattle have wide range panoramic field of vision" and cattle "can see, and be stimulated by, movement or activities to the side and rear of them."

## Keep it calm
- "Sudden, quick moves and sudden high-pitched noises can startle cattle. A sudden move near a dairy animal can set off a kick."
  - guidance: 14MI014 (exact): "Sudden jerky movements, quick movements, and sudden intermittent or high pitch noises can startle them." / "a sudden movement near a dairy animal can cause the kicking reflex."
- "So move slow and steady, and keep the noise down."
  - guidance: 14MI014, practical application of the line above (paraphrase). The report gives no explicit "calm handling" recommendation; this is the direct converse of its startle statement.
- "Watch for sick animals. Health problems can make an animal harder to handle and move."
  - guidance: 14MI014 (exact): "Animal health can complicate animal handling and movement with certain health conditions"; Recommendation 2 (exact): "Train workers/family members to recognize animal health issues and attack scenarios and behaviors."

## Tight spaces and a way out
- "Before you go into a pen, alley, or stall, know your escape route or protected spot, like a passage gate."
  - guidance: 14MI014 (exact): "accessible escape routes or protected areas, such as passage gates for working in close quarters with livestock." Brothers/bull report Recommendation 2 (exact): "Handling facilities for bulls should include escape routes and protected areas for handlers." That report's summary: "There was no escape route or protected areas from animal attack in the pen."
- "Don't go into a stall from behind an animal. Move it from the empty stall next to it."
  - guidance: 14MI014 (exact): "Entry into the partitioned freestall from the rear of the animal should not occur." Recommendation 1 (exact): "workers prompt animal movement from the resting position in a partitioned freestall from an empty freestall next to the animal being directed."
- "In tight spots, an animal getting up can pin you against a stanchion. That's how crushing injuries happen."
  - guidance: 14MI014 (exact): "the decedent was struck and pinned against a stanchion by a cow which was getting up." / "then a crushing injury, including a fatality, could result."
- "When you can, move the animals out before you work in their area."
  - guidance: 96MN007 Recommendation #2 (exact): "Whenever possible, workers should move and isolate animals from livestock areas before work is performed in those areas."

## Bulls
- "Never be in a pen with a bull alone. Have a second person who knows animal behavior watch the bull and warn you."
  - guidance: 14MI014 Recommendation 4 (exact): "Workers should not be permitted to be in a pen with a bull alone"; discussion (exact): "If a worker is in a pen with a bull, it is recommended that another individual trained in animal behavior and attack scenarios be present to watch the bull and to alert the individual working."
- "Always know where the bull is and what it's doing."
  - guidance: 14MI014 Recommendation 4 (exact): "should maintain constant awareness of the location and behavior of bulls when entering a holding pen."
- "Put a barrier, like a fence or a tractor, between you and the animal when you can."
  - guidance: 14MI014, MIOSHA recommendation to the farm (exact): "Protect employees from injuries from a bull and other livestock by ensuring there is a barrier like a tractor or fence to prevent the animal from being in the same area as the employee." Also 96MN007 discussion (paraphrase): consider using tractors or pickup trucks to create protected areas.

## Ask
- "In the pen or alley we're working in today, where is your way out?" — crew question tied to the escape-route guidance.

## Dropped
- "Never get between animals in tight spaces": no fetched source states this directly. Covered instead by the sourced pinning/stanchion line and the freestall-entry rule.
- Point of balance: 14MI014 defines it but gives no recommendation; left out to keep the talk short.
