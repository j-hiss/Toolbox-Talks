# grain-elevators evidence

Sources fetched 2026-10-08:
- S = 29 CFR 1910.272, Grain handling facilities. The osha.gov standardnumber page would not load (provenance error) and the osha.prod.pace.dol.gov mirror failed, so the text was checked against eCFR: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-R/section-1910.272 . Sources keep the osha.gov URL as the brief requires.
- GH = OSHA Grain Handling topic page, https://www.osha.gov/grain-handling
- MN = NIOSH FACE (Minnesota) 00MN036, https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/face/stateface/mn/00mn036.html (published by NIOSH through the FACE program). Used as a case finding.

Note on scope: 1910.272 duties belong to the grain facility's employer. A driver hauling to an elevator usually works for someone else, so the talk states the elevator's duties as facts about the site ("pits have to be covered", "elevators have to tell contractors") and keeps driver actions to guidance.

| Moving grain can bury a person in seconds. | GH | exact: moving grain can "bury a worker in seconds." |
| That's true in a bin, and it's true in the back of your truck while it drains. | GH; MN | GH: "Suffocation is a leading cause of death in grain storage bins."; MN paraphrase: worker "stepped into the flowing wheat" while grain drained from a belly-dump semi-truck box, "was pulled under". |
| Never climb into a grain trailer or hopper while it's being emptied. | MN | exact rec.: "Workers should never enter grain hauling equipment or a grain storage structure ... when the equipment or structure is being emptied." |
| In one NIOSH case, a 19-year-old stepped into wheat draining from his truck, was pulled under, and died. | MN | paraphrase: 19-year-old farm worker who drove a truck for a harvesting crew; "While grain was draining from the truck, he stepped into the flowing wheat, was pulled under", pronounced dead at hospital. |
| Stop the unloading equipment and lock out the power before anyone gets into hauling equipment. | MN | exact rec.: "All grain handling equipment should be stopped and the power source locked out ... before workers enter grain hauling equipment or grain storage structures." |
| Never go into the elevator's bins, silos or tanks. | S (g), (e)(2) | paraphrase: entry is a "special task" requiring training ((e)(2)) and the (g) precautions; drivers are not bin-entry workers. | Entry takes a permit or a supervisor there the whole time, lockout, air testing, a lifeline when required, and an observer outside. | S (g)(1)(i)-(iii), (g)(2), (g)(3) | exact: "The employer shall issue a permit for entering bins, silos, or tanks unless" the employer or representative is present for the entire operation; equipment "shall be deenergized and ... locked out"; atmosphere "shall be tested for oxygen content unless" continuous air movement; (g)(2) harness with lifeline or boatswain's chair where required; (g)(3) "An observer, equipped to provide assistance, shall be stationed outside". |
| Only workers trained for bin entry go in. | S (e)(2) | exact: "Employees assigned special tasks, such as bin entry," shall be trained to perform those tasks safely. |
| Truck receiving pits have to be covered by grates. | S (k) | exact: "Receiving-pit feed openings, such as truck or railcar receiving-pits, shall be covered by grates." |
| The openings can be no wider than 2 and a half inches. | S (k) | exact: grate openings "a maximum of 2 1/2 inches" in width. |
| If a grate is missing or out of place, stop and tell the elevator staff before you dump. | S (k) (practical) | practical: follows from (k); no fetched page says these words. **Reviewer may drop.** |
| Augers and conveyors have moving parts that can catch you and cause amputations. | GH | exact: moving parts of augers and conveyors "present serious entanglement and amputation hazards." |
| Keep your hands, clothes and tools clear. | GH (practical) | practical: follows from the entanglement hazard; no fetched page gives this wording. **Reviewer may drop.** |
| Grain dust is the main fuel for explosions in grain handling. | GH | exact: "Grain dust is the main source of fuel for explosions in grain handling." |
| Elevators have to keep a written housekeeping program for dust. | S (j)(1) | exact: "The employer shall develop and implement a written housekeeping program that establishes the frequency and method(s)" for reducing fugitive grain dust. |
| In priority areas, dust has to be cleaned up when it builds past 1/8 inch. | S (j)(2)(ii) | paraphrase: at grain elevators, fugitive dust accumulations at priority areas shall be removed promptly whenever they exceed 1/8 inch. (Priority areas: floors within 35 ft of inside bucket elevators, enclosed grinding and dryer areas; truck dumps are not listed.) |
| Smoking is a common ignition source. | S (e)(1)(i) | paraphrase: training must cover "recognition and preventive measures for the hazards related to dust accumulations" and common ignition sources "such as smoking". |
| Follow the site's no-smoking rules. | S (e)(1)(i), (i) (practical) | practical: follows from smoking as an ignition source and (i) site safety rules. |
| Welding or torch work at an elevator needs a hot work permit, with a few exceptions. | S (f) | exact: "The employer shall issue a permit for all hot work, with the following exceptions:" (employer/representative present; authorized welding shops; authorized hot work areas outside the structure). |
| This OSHA rule covers grain elevators, feed mills, flour mills and similar plants. | S (b)(1) | exact: applies to "grain elevators, feed mills, flour mills, rice mills," "dust pelletizing plants, dry corn mills, soybean flaking operations", dry grinding of soycake. |
| Elevators have to tell contractors who work there about fire and explosion hazards, the site safety rules, and the emergency plan. | S (i) | exact: employer shall "inform contractors performing work at the grain handling facility of known potential fire and explosion hazards", plus applicable safety rules and emergency action plan provisions. |

- Dropped: falls into pits (no fetched page addresses falls at dump pits beyond the grate rule); auger guarding specifics (not found in fetched pages); "post grain boxes as confined spaces" (MN rec. 3) left out for length.
- Overlap: the existing `grain-bins` talk (ag) covers bin entry in detail. This talk keeps bin entry to one item and focuses on the driver at the elevator: draining trucks, pit grates, augers, dust, site rules.
